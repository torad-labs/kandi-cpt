Gender unknown

Gender unknown (Japanese: 性別不明(せいべつふめい) sex unknown) is a gender designation that species of Pokémon can have. Pokémon such as Voltorb, Staryu, and Mew that display neither a ♂ nor ♀ symbol in battle or on their summary screen are gender-unknown Pokémon.

Terminology

In English-language official media, these Pokémon are generally referred to as being gender unknown,[1] as having or being of unknown gender,[2] or simply by stating that their gender is unknown.[3] As a prenominal adjective, it is generally hyphenated, such as in "gender-unknown Pokémon",[4] though it has also appeared without being hyphenated.[5]

Alternate terms that have been used officially include:

  • no gender, which was used in a strategy article on Pokémon.com during Generation VIII and in various official guidebooks during Generations II and IV;[6]
  • genderless, which was used in the Prima Games Pokémon Gold & Silver: Prima's Official Strategy Guide guidebook, in the Nintendo Power Pokémon Gold and Silver: Complete Pokédex guidebook, in Pokémon.com mailbag responses from November 2002 and January 2003, and in a previous version of the Play! Pokémon Video Game Rules, Formats & Penalty Guidelines document;[7]
  • neuter, which was used in Nintendo Power guidebooks during Generation II;[8] and
  • indeterminate gender, which was used in Pokémon Platinum: Prima's Official Strategy Guide.[9]

In Pokémon Diamond, Pearl, Brilliant Diamond, and Shining Pearl, Picnicker Siena on Route 205 mentions that there are some Pokémon whose gender can't be told, while in Pokémon Platinum, she mentions that there are some Pokémon that are neither male nor female; both of these correspond to usages of 「性別不明(せいべつふめい)」 "sex unknown" in Japanese.[10]

The Japanese versions of the games also refer to gender-unknown Pokémon as having (Japanese: せいべつなし no gender, formatted in kanji as 性別(せいべつ)なし no gender in Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee! and Pokémon HOME on the Nintendo Switch) in the search feature of the Pokémon Storage System and Pokémon HOME.[11] The official Japanese site for Pokémon Black 2 and White 2 also refers to gender-unknown Pokémon as (Japanese: 性別のないポケモン Pokémon without a gender).[12]

As an Egg Group

In Pokémon Stadium 2, Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen: Official Nintendo Player's Guide, Pokémon Emerald: Official Nintendo Player's Guide, and in official Japanese and Chinese guidebooks for the Generation II and III games, Gender unknown also refers to an Egg Group consisting of gender-unknown Pokémon that are able to breed with Ditto, but this Egg Group has never actually existed in the coding of the games. In these sources, gender-unknown Pokémon are listed in this Egg Group rather than in their actual Egg Groups. While Pokémon Diamond and Pearl: Official Nintendo Player's Guide also lists Gender Unknown as an Egg Group, it lists the Pokémon with their actual Egg Groups in the data section.

In Pokémon Ruby and Sapphire: Official Nintendo Player's Guide, Pokémon Emerald: Prima's Official Strategy Guide, Pokémon 10th Anniversary Collector's Edition Complete Pokédex, and early Japanese official guidebooks for Pokémon Ruby and Sapphire, gender-unknown Pokémon are instead listed in the No Eggs group, as these books do not cover the role Ditto plays in breeding. This was likely due to it not being available at release until it could be traded from a later gameRS, or not being available until after obtaining the National Pokédex in the Desert UnderpassE. Official guidebooks since Generation IV consistently list gender-unknown Pokémon in their actual Egg Groups.

Mechanics

Gender-unknown Pokémon can only breed with Ditto. However, most gender-unknown Pokémon are in the No Eggs Discovered group, meaning that those Pokémon cannot breed at all. Ditto itself can be bred with any Pokémon that can breed (except Ditto), but the Eggs produced are always based on the other Pokémon's species. Prior to Pokémon Scarlet and Violet, gender-unknown Pokémon could not inherit Egg Moves.

In battle, gender-unknown Pokémon are immune to gender-based effects. They cannot become infatuated nor infatuate other Pokémon. The moves Attract and Captivate fail if used by or on these Pokémon. The Abilities Cute Charm and Rivalry do not affect gender-unknown Pokémon and have no effect if possessed by them.

Characteristics

While most Pokémon are either male or female, gender-unknown Pokémon are characterized by the fact that they are not known to be of either gender. In-universe, this is because no one has been able to determine the gender of these Pokémon, including scientists.[13]

Most of these Pokémon are in the No Eggs Discovered group, including many highly rare Pokémon (Legendary Pokémon, Mythical Pokémon, Ultra Beasts, and Paradox Pokémon), as well as Unown, Gimmighoul, and Gholdengo. The Galar Fossil Pokémon are also included, possibly referencing their chimerical nature. In addition to the Pokémon in the No Eggs Discovered group, Ditto, the sole member of its own Egg Group, is also unable to produce Eggs containing itself. Although some of these Pokémon have been observed hatching from Eggs, Eggs containing these Pokémon cannot be obtained in-game.

Of the gender-unknown Pokémon that can breed, most of them are based on machines or other inanimate objects that do not have a biological sex, or are based on lifeforms that can reproduce asexually. Almost all of them are in the Mineral group, including many artificial Pokémon such as Porygon. Apart from these, Staryu and Starmie are based on starfish, which are known for being able to reproduce asexually, while Falinks, Tandemaus, and Maushold consist of multiple individuals with different roles, similar to a colony.

Solgaleo's Pokédex entry in Pokémon Moon states that it is said to be a male Evolution of Cosmog, while Lunala's Pokédex entry in Pokémon Sun states that it is said to be a female Evolution of Cosmog, despite both of these Pokémon being gender unknown. Similarly, despite Zacian being gender unknown, its Pokédex entry in Pokémon Shield states that some say it it Zamazenta's elder sister. Despite Manaphy, Diancie, and Calyrex being gender unknown, they are also referred to by gendered titles ("Prince of the Sea", "queen" or "princess", and "King of Bountiful Harvests", respectively). In the Pokémon Mystery Dungeon games, several Pokémon that are gender unknown in the core series games are referred to as male or female by in-game characters.

Pokémon

Of the 1028 Pokémon species, 155 are gender unknown (15.08%).
