move

sync stones

Mechanics
Basics
During battle, a sync move can only be used if the sync move countdown is at 0. At the start of the battle, the sync move countdown begins at 9 (6 for the opposing team's center-most sync pair, and 4 for opponents in the
Legendary Arena
/Gauntlet), and decreases by one each time a sync pair uses a move. Once the countdown is down to 0, a sync move can be unleashed at any point. Once a sync move has been used, the countdown resets, and its user's team receives a damage-boosting sync buff.
Any sync pair from the player's team can use a sync move once the countdown reaches 0. In contrast, on the opponent's team, only the center sync pair/Pokémon can use sync moves. Moreover, while the player's sync move usually targets only one opponent, the opponent's sync move will cause damage to all of the player's Pokémon and, before version 1.6.0, would reset all their raised stat ranks to zero.
In addition, despite having
move
in its name, sync moves are treated differently from
moves
, especially in how they interact with
skills
. However, all damage-dealing moves, sync moves, and
max moves
are sometimes grouped together as "attacks."
Power
The base power of a sync move, as well as the base power of the sync pair's regular moves, can be increased by raising the sync pair's move level. Move level can be increased by using a Move Candy or by obtaining a duplicate of the sync pair. Move level can be increased four times, up to move level 5. Each increase of move level raises the power of moves, including the sync move, by 5%. This results in a sync move at move level 3 having 10% more power than the same attack at move level 1, and a sync move at move level 5 having 20% more power than the same attack at move level 1. The only exceptions are
Leaf  & Eevee
,
Professor Sycamore  & Xerneas
, and
Sygna Suit Dawn  & Cresselia
—all of whom have
non-damaging
sync moves.
Some sync moves have an additional effect that increases the move's power based on if or how well a certain condition has been met. Depending on the condition, there are different ways the increase in power is calculated.
Some sync moves have a condition that is either true or false. If the condition is true when the sync move is performed, then the sync move's power doubles (or increases by ×2).
An example description of a sync move with a condition that is either true or false is "This attack's power increases when the terrain is Electric Terrain."
Some sync moves have a condition based on how much a
stat
of the user has been raised or how much a stat of the target has been lowered. If the user's/target's stat has been raised/lowered by N stat ranks when the sync move is performed, then the attack's power is multiplied by a factor of 1 + (1/6 × N). Since each stat (excluding critical-hit rate, which has never been used in a sync move condition) can be raised or lowered to a maximum of six stat ranks, this factor increases to a maximum of ×2.
An example description of a sync move with a condition based on one stat of the user is "The more the user's Speed is raised, the greater the power of this attack."
An example description of a sync move with a condition based on one stat of the target is "The more the target's Defense is lowered, the greater the power of this attack."
Some sync moves have a condition based on how much all the user's stats have been raised or how much all the target's stats have been lowered. If the user's/target's stats (including critical-hit rate) have been raised/lowered by a total of N stat ranks when the sync move is performed, and N is less than 18, then the attack's power is multiplied by a factor of 1 + (1/15 × N). If N is greater than or equal to 18, then the attack's power is multiplied by a factor of 1 + (1/15 × 18). So, if the user's/target's stats have been raised/lowered by a total of at least 18 stat ranks, this factor reaches its maximum potential of ×2.2.
The description of a sync move with a condition based on the user's stats is "The more the user's stats are raised, the greater the power of this attack."
The description of a sync move with a condition based on the target's stats is "The more the target's stats are lowered, the greater the power of this attack."

General properties
The sync move removes any
status condition
or status change that would prevent the Pokémon from using a Pokémon move (namely,
Sleep
,
Freeze
, and
Flinch
), it will also not trigger damage from
Burn
,
Poison
, and
Trap
, nor will it trigger HP regeneration. Furthermore, if the opponent has the Enduring effect—a status change allowing them to survive the first time their HP reaches 0—said effect won't be triggered, and the opponent will be defeated if their HP reaches 0 as a consequence of a sync move. If offensive, the sync move will always hit the opponent, regardless of the status conditions and negative
accuracy
stages of the user, and if the opponent is in the semi-invulnerable period of an offensive move, their move will be negated and the sync move will hit them. As a result, if the user has the Sure Hit Next effect—a status change that allows them not to miss the next hit—that effect won't be lost when using a sync move. Finally, the
critical hit
rate of the sync move is lower than that of the other offensive Pokémon moves—reaching only 80% if critical-hit rate is fully raised to three stat ranks. However, having the Critical Hit ↑ Next effect when using a sync move or possessing both a fully raised critical-hit rate and a
skill
that makes sync moves' critical hits land more easily will make sync moves always land critical hits (except in special circumstances).
Also, all sync moves grant sync buffs to their users' team after they are used. Each sync buff multiplies a sync pair's damage from their attack moves, sync moves, and max moves by 1.5× (×150%), and sync buffs stack additively, which means that sync pairs with two of them deal double (or ×200%) damage, those with three deal 2.5× (or ×250%) damage, and so on.
6★ EX
Furthermore, sync pairs raised to
6★ EX
get the following effects added to their sync move depending on their role:
Strike
: Sync move targets all opponents without its damage scaling down, even if multiple targets are present.
Tech
: Sync move deals 1.5× more damage to the target.
Support
: Doubles the number of sync buffs given to each teammate (from 1 to 2) the first time their sync move is used each battle.
Sprint
: Sync move countdown is reduced by three the first time the user's sync move is used each battle.
Field
: Implements a specified field effect just before the first time the user's sync move is used each battle, and extends its duration for that time only.
Multi
: Lowers the type rebuff of the sync pair's type for all opposing sync pairs by one rank the first time the user's sync move is used each battle.
If a sync pair is both raised to 6★ EX and has their
EX role
unlocked, then they will have two of the above effects: the ones corresponding to their original role and their EX role.
Divine moves
Arc suit
sync pairs have special sync moves called divine moves that have a higher base power than standard sync moves and always have an innate additional effect, though the additional effect varies between sync pairs. Divine moves have a slightly different animation than standard sync moves, which shows them receiving power from
Arceus
rather than using their
sync stone
to activate the move. Otherwise, they are mechanically identical to standard sync moves.
Defunct mechanics
Prior to version 1.9.0, upon entering any battle, a Sync Orb Chance could activate. If a sync move was used during such a battle, five sync pair-specific sync orbs (in single player) or 25 co-op sync orbs (in co-op) would be awarded.
List of sync moves
The following is a list of currently available sync moves.
Generic sync moves
Generic sync moves have no extra effect, and follow a simple naming scheme of "(Type) Sync Beam/Impact", with "Beam" indicating that the sync move is Special, while "Impact" indicates that the move is Physical.
These sync moves tend to be used by
Egg Pokémon
sync pairs, as well as sync pairs with Pokémon that can evolve through Evolution sync pair stories, but who have not yet reached their final stage. NPC opponents who do not correspond to obtainable sync pairs will also tend to use generic sync moves.

Trivia
Miracle Idol
Draco Meteor
is the only
Dragon-type
attack that is potentially less powerful in a regular Dragon
Zone
(1.5× damage for Dragon-type attacks) than outside it (2× damage if neither the opponent's field of play nor the entire field of play has a field effect, including any zone). However, this phenomenon does not occur if the sync move is used in an EX Dragon Zone (3× damage for Dragon-type attacks).
Island
Splintered Stormshards
is the only sync move that removes any field effects.
All non-damaging sync moves charge their user's move gauges and are owned by support sync pairs.
Audacious
Multi-Attack
is the first sync move that can have its
type
changed.
It is also the only sync move that can be any type.
Gentlemanly
Geomancy
is the only sync move with its info box saying that it targets all allies.
Storm Clout
Steel Beam
has the same second half of its name as generic
special
Steel-type
sync moves, but unlike the latter attacks, the former is based on a
move
from the
core series
games.
Nightmares Begone
Lunar Dance
is the only sync move with the "Heals" effect tag.
Desert Spirit Dragon Beam is the only sync move used by an anniversary-commemorating sync pair,
Raihan (Anniversary 2022) & Flygon
, that is not to be based on a core series move.
Gloria (Alt. 2) & Cinderace
are the only sync pair with three sync moves of different names and types: Ace of the Team Fire Impact, A Taste of My Skill Flying Impact, and Score a Victory Fighting Impact. The
Fire-type
attack is the default one, but this sync move's
type
changes to
Flying
or
Fighting
if this sync pair uses their Flying- or Fighting-type
max move
in battle, respectively.
