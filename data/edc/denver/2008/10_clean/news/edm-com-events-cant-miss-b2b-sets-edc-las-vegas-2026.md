30 years in, EDC still knows how to stack a lineup.

The festival’s anniversary edition in Sin City raises the bar with a slew of collaborative bookings that give ravers plenty of reasons to make the trip out to the Las Vegas Motor Speedway.

Read on to discover six sets worth building your festival schedule around. EDC returns May 15-17, 2026 and three-day passes are sold out, but a limited number of Sunday tickets remain.

## Laidback Luke & Chuckie

A pair of electro pioneers are throwing down on opening night for one of EDC’s more “hidden gem” sets. Dutch house legends Laidback Luke and Chuckie defined an entire generation of dance music, and watching them trade selections on the festival’s most popular stage is a rare and genuine full-circle moment.

Luke, who famously collaborated with Swedish House Mafia’s Axwell, Steve Angello and Sebastian Ingrosso to release the generational dance anthem ‘Leave the World Behind’ back in 2009, remains deeply influential. Chuckie brings the same raw Amsterdam energy that made him a global force, and together, they’ll flood Kinetic Field with massive drops and nostalgic bangers to set the perfect tone for the entire weekend.

**Space Laces & Eptic**

When two of dubstep’s most creative minds share a stage, the result tends to be pretty memorable. Space Laces and Eptic have co-produced tracks and shared the decks before, most notably alongside SVDDEN DEATH as the supergroup MASTERHAND, but a b2b between just the two of them is genuinely rare.

Space Laces is one of the most gifted producers in electronic music, reshaping modern dubstep and bass music with sound-design techniques that few can match but many attempt to emulate. Eptic is a formidable producer and performer in his own right, bringing a no-holds-barred approach built on brutal drops, galvanizing vocals and sounds most producers would not dare touch. The consensus from anyone who has seen them perform is simple: it’s absolutely nuts.

**Doctor P, Flux Pavilion & FuntCase**

**Doctor P, Flux Pavilion ****&** FuntCase

**&**FuntCase

Few bookings can deliver a nostalgia rush quite like this one. Dubstep icons Doctor P and Flux Pavilion founded the influential label Circus Records, and FuntCase has been a core part of its roster since the label launched in 2009. He rounds out the trio with a heavier approach that will sharpen the set’s edge.

Together, the three artists represent the uncompromising dubstep sound of the early-2010s, and if you grew up in that era of electronic music, this b3b is a full-circle moment.

**GRiZ** & **Wooli**

While they’ve appeared onstage together intermittently, EDC marks the first time Wooli and GRiZ have been booked for a formal b2b set. The two are no strangers, having collaborated on ‘Chaos Theory’ last year, but translating that chemistry to a live set is a different animal entirely.

GRiZ has built one of the most distinctive identities in electronic music, incorporating saxophone into his productions and moving between funk, glitch-hop and dubstep without losing his signature sound. Wooli arrives with an evolving discography full of fiery dubstep and riddim drops, and he recently announced he’s recording his debut album. Considering EDC’s popularity and reputation as a testing ground, attendees will likely hear new music from the record.

**Peggy Gou ****&** KI/KI

**&**KI/KI

Peggy Gou is already on the bill for a solo set Friday, but she returns Saturday for what will be her first-ever b2b set with Dutch techno and trance powerhouse KI/KI, making it of the more intriguing pairings of the weekend.

Gou’s eclectic house selections are well-established but KI/KI brings an edge with her high-energy, acid-tinged sound, which she flexed in tracks like ‘Losing Control’ and ‘What’s a Girl to Do in ’25,’ both of which landed on *EDM.com*‘s list of the best songs of 2025. The contrast between their styles is striking, and exactly what makes their set unmissable.

**Josh Baker, Prospa ****&** KETTAMA

**&**K

Three of house music’s most versatile acts sharing the decks at once is reason enough to stop by the Neon Garden, but this trio just DJed together at Coachella’s Day 0 on the campgrounds, which means they arrive at EDC already warmed up

Each brings their own identity to the floor. KETTAMA is a master of crowd control while Baker brings tech house sensibilities and Prospa operates with warmth and euphoria. On their own, each is capable of a set worth attending, but together, they represent techno and house music storytelling at its finest.
