# From Classroom Epiphany to Festival Stages: Inside the Breakneck Rise of AVELLO - EDM

It feels like AVELLO arrived in the electronic music scene with calculation, not by chance. His path to the EDM.com Class of 2026, marked by viral remixes and high-profile label signings, began with a moment of pure fandom in the heart of Florida.

Before the millions of streams and massive festival sets, there was a high school senior with his head on a desk, listening to ILLENIUM’s *Awake*.

“I was always listening to EDM off and on,” AVELLO, whose real name is Adam Lawrence, tells *EDM.com*. “I think everybody was into artists like Flume and ODESZA around 2015 and 2016. In 2017, I started getting more into the festival scene. I had just turned 18 and was entering my senior year. I was listening to a lot of Zeds Dead and similar acts, but when I heard *Awake*, something really clicked. I fell in love with it.

“The moment the first track started, I sat straight up and thought, ‘Holy sh*t, this is so cool, I have to know how he did this.’ I wanted to understand how he captured those emotions in music. Feelings I’d never heard conveyed that way before. That’s what’s so fascinating about electronic music: it can capture emotions that classical instruments or words often cannot.”

The realization that electronic music could convey what words could not sparked a fire that led him to his first rave at Life In Color Orlando in 2017. Lawrence looks back on the experience with reverence for the “magic” that exists before entering the music industry.

“I think everyone remembers those first events with nostalgia,” he says. “That ‘magic’ before you see how everything works behind the curtain.”

Since stepping behind that curtain, Lawrence has moved with a speed and precision that defies the typical “starving artist” narrative. His viral remix of Disco Lines and Tinashe’s ‘No Broke Boys’ demonstrated an efficiency that proves the best ideas sometimes come with the least friction.

“That remix taught me that simplicity is key,” he explains. “Spending more time on a project doesn’t always make it better. That remix came together in a single day. The original dropped on a Friday; Sunday at noon I sat down to flip it, and by 10pm it was mostly done. I spent a little time Monday and Tuesday polishing the mix and master, and it was out by Thursday.”

This approach has shaped his studio habits: embracing time constraints and letting ideas develop naturally.

“I stopped forcing things after that,” Lawrence says. “If you have to force it, maybe it’s not a strong idea. The best ideas flow effortlessly. First, make it exist. Later, make it good. Too many people try to do both at the same time.”

That workflow has enabled a relentless release schedule, with new tracks dropping nearly every three weeks. In an industry that thrives on constant attention, Lawrence avoids the anxiety of the “ID teaser” cycle by never staying quiet.

“Being quiet between releases has never really been my thing,” he notes. “Since ‘No Broke Boys’ dropped, I’ve put out a track almost every three weeks. The music is the content, and staying consistent means there’s no downtime on my socials.”

Underlying this output is an intuitive grasp of the industry. Although Lawrence earned a bachelor’s degree in marketing and an MBA from Florida State University, his professional experience remained in supply chain management. He never worked in marketing, but the principles shape how he builds consumer trust.

“What I take away from that is consumer trust,” he says. “It all comes down to trust. People don’t want to part with their time or money without feeling it will be worth it. Trust is hard to build and easy to destroy.”

For Lawrence, trust is earned in seconds. “If someone finds a video on their explore page and follows, the next video has to deliver,” he explains. “If it disappoints, you’ve lost that potential fan. Whether they spend 15 seconds watching or their money on a show, people don’t want to waste time. Build trust, and the audience knows it will be worth it. Stick around for my 15-second video, and there’s a dopamine hit or a song they’ll love.”

This clarity has made him a sought-after artist for labels like Seven Lions’ Ophelia Records, Excision’s Subsidia and Breakaway Projects, the label and artist management arm of Breakaway Music Festival. Now, labels come to him for his signature sound.

“I’m in a position where labels want my sound,” Lawrence says. “I don’t write for them; I write for me. That’s why they offer the deal. Labels now say, ‘We want an AVELLO release,’ so I make an AVELLO song.”

As far as his biggest ambitions, Lawrence points to the prospect of working with influential indie and pop acts like Florence + The Machine. His dream producer collaboration would be The Naked and Famous, the chart-topping indietronica band behind the global synth-pop hit ‘Young Blood.’

“I want to write a beautiful ballad with Florence, a party-style pop song with Kesha, and something emotional and catchy with Halsey,” he says. “Those are my biggest goals. I’ll work with them at some point… That electronic indie-pop from 2012 to 2015 shaped me. I still listen to The Naked and Famous: ‘Punching In A Dream,’ ‘Girls Like You.’ All their songs are so cool.”

Looking ahead, Lawrence is focused on manifestation. A year ago, he photoshopped his name onto festival posters. He’s now commanding those stages today, with confirmed slots at iconic music festivals like Lollapalooza and EDC Las Vegas.

“There are over 15 festivals this year, many unannounced,” he gushes. “I also focus on my vision board. A year ago, I put myself on the Ultra lineup and imagined accepting a Grammy or reaching 10 million monthly listeners. Back then, it felt lofty. I hadn’t even been officially playlisted. Now, I’ve been playlisted about 30 times in a few months. Things happen faster than you think.”
