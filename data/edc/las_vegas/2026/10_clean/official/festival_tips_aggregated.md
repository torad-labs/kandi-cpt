## The Top Refrains (What Veterans Always Say to First-Timers)

### Feet First

This is the number-one piece of advice across every veteran source.
Wear comfortable, broken-in sneakers — not sandals, not flip-flops,
not new shoes you've never walked in. Expect to walk 10-20 miles per
day. The Speedway is mostly paved with rough concrete and some sections
aren't paved at all. Heels are out of the question. Foot care supplies
(blister pads, bandages, gel insoles, extra socks) are worth their
weight. Train the week before with a few longer walks if you have time.

If your feet go down, your whole festival goes down. People skip the
last night because their feet are wrecked from the first night.

### Pace Yourself — It's a Marathon

You have three 10-hour nights. Don't burn yourself out on the first
night. Veterans suggest some people are sober on night 1 just to get a
feel for the festival and the grounds. You can ramp up on night 2 and
night 3 if you want. Many veterans take a full day off between nights
to actually sleep and recover.

Up to 20% of people don't make it to night 1 — they're already too
tired or hungover from Vegas pre-parties. Don't be one of them.

### Hydrate Properly — Not Just Water

Drink 16 oz (480 mL) per hour as a baseline. Cap at 27 oz (800 mL) per
hour to avoid hyponatremia ("water poisoning"). Electrolytes matter
just as much as water — Powerade, Gatorade, Pedialyte, electrolyte
tablets, or the free Electrolit sticks at the water stations. Pure
water without electrolytes doesn't fix dehydration alone.

Bring an empty CamelBak or Insomniac water bottle (or buy a water
bottle inside at the merch booth). Refill at the free water stations —
they're marked with a water-droplet symbol on the festival map.

In an emergency you can ask for bottled water inside the first aid
tents.

### Ear Protection

High-fidelity earplugs (custom-molded or non-custom like Etymotic,
EARasers, Loop) preserve the music while cutting volume by 15-25 dB.
Foam plugs muffle the music. Bring something. Tinnitus from a single
EDC weekend is real.

### Plan for Rest Days

Veterans recommend: arrive Thursday, leave Tuesday. Many people use a
Thursday rest day to acclimate to Vegas heat and recover from travel.
A Monday rest day before flying home prevents the worst version of
post-festival exhaustion.

### Pick Your Priorities

You will not see every set. Accept it before you arrive. The math
doesn't work — 240+ artists across 9 main stages plus 8 secondary
venues. Choose 3-5 must-see sets per night and let the rest happen.
The grounds are huge; running between stages eats time and energy.

### Use the Buddy System with Real Meeting Points

Cell service at the speedway is unreliable to nonexistent. SMS may
arrive hours late. Snapchat sometimes works for location sharing,
sometimes doesn't. Plan accordingly.

Pick a fixed, unmistakable meeting spot — not "near the daisy" (the
giant Daisy art piece moves throughout the night). Veterans suggest
the lockers area (well lit, contained, easy to find familiar faces).
Set meeting times every 1-2 hours. Don't change the meeting spot
between nights.

If you can't find your friends, don't panic. People at raves are
generally welcoming. You'll find them eventually. If you have a safe
ride back to your hotel (your shuttle bracelet guarantees it), you'll
be fine even alone.

### Restroom Strategy

Lines pile up at the front of the porta-potty rows. Walk to the back
of the array — back-row stalls usually have shorter waits or are
empty. The toilet paper and hand sanitizer Insomniac provides runs
out about halfway through the first night, so bring your own. Wet
wipes are a multi-purpose lifesaver.

### Get the Shuttle Bracelet

If you're staying on the Strip, the EDC shuttle is the easiest way to
get to and from the venue. Shuttle passes are bought beforehand. The
shuttle bracelet stays on your wrist all weekend — it's your fail-safe
ride home no matter how the rest of the night goes.

Pickup is from 10 specified hotels along the Strip. Shuttles run back
and forth all night.

### Sunrise Sets — At Least Once

EDC's sunrise sets at Kinetic Field (Above & Beyond, Armin van Buuren,
others depending on year) are one of the festival's signature
experiences. Night 2 is usually ideal for the sunrise commitment —
night 3 has Monday morning Vegas traffic that makes leaving after
sunrise punishing.

### Leave Before 4:30am or Suffer

The post-festival traffic and shuttle lines are brutal after about
4:30am. If you can't stay until the very end, peace out a little
early. If you stay until 5:30am, expect to wait 1-2 hours in line to
get on a shuttle home.

### Crowd Etiquette (PLUR)

Watch for people sitting in crowd flow paths. Practice simple manners —
"excuse me," "sorry," "thank you" matter a lot in tight spaces. PLUR
(Peace Love Unity Respect) is the unofficial code of conduct. Trading
kandi (beaded bracelets) is the friendship signal of the scene; bring
extras if you want to make friends. Compliment costumes. Help people
who look lost. Watch out for anyone who looks unwell.

## What to Pack

Standard packing list compiled from RollSafe + Insomniac + Reddit:

- Comfortable, broken-in sneakers (not new!)
- Foot care: blister pads, bandages, gel insoles, extra socks
- High-fidelity earplugs
- Wet wipes (lots)
- Sunscreen (UV is low until 8am but the desert sun is brutal post-dawn)
- Sealed ChapStick (the Nevada wind eats lips alive)
- Hat or shades (for sunrise lingering)
- Layered clothing — desert nights drop into the 50s F by 4am, dancing
  near the speakers stays hot
- Lightweight fanny pack or small daybag (festival-allowed size only)
- Power bank for phone
- Hand fan (peak crowd at Kinetic Field gets hot)
- Anti-chafe product (BodyGlide, Squirrel's Nut Butter) — chafing on
  thighs and underarms is the silent festival killer
- Pre-festival hydration: electrolyte loading the day before
- Some cash for prepaid card exchange or emergency
- Photocopy of your ID separate from your ID
- Phone case with a wrist lanyard
- Optional: totem (visible meeting marker), kandi to trade

## Wayfinding Texture (Speedway Layout)

EDC is held at the Las Vegas Motor Speedway, which is a NASCAR track.
The festival lays out across the infield and outside the track. The
main entrance puts you in front of Kinetic Field — it's straight
ahead, the iconic main stage with the giant face / owl mascot. From
Kinetic Field, the other stages fan out around the grounds.

The exit from the venue runs through the raised bleachers, which are
visible from most points in the venue. If you get totally lost,
spotting the bleachers gives you a fix on where the exit is.

The Daisy art installation moves through the night — it's a poor
meeting point. The Lockers area, the merchant store, info booths, and
specific stage entrances are better fixed reference points.

The Ferris wheel and carnival rides are in Downtown EDC, between the
main stages. Good navigation reference, good sunrise vantage. Time it
right and you can ride the Ferris wheel with a view of fireworks.

Each stage has its own visual signature. Kinetic Field has the giant
mascot face. Cosmic Meadow is the open-grounds stage where the opening
ceremony happens. Circuit Grounds has LED walls and fire plumes. Neon
Garden is darker, more enclosed. Wasteland's post-apocalyptic aesthetic
is unmistakable.

## What the Reddit / Aggregator Tips Always Mention

Across r/electricdaisycarnival posts, recap threads, and "first EDC"
Q&A, the most-repeated tips are:

1. Comfortable shoes — the universal #1 refrain
2. Hydrate + electrolytes (not just water)
3. Earplugs
4. Don't try to see everything — pick priorities
5. Buddy system + fixed meeting points (NOT the Daisy)
6. Get there early on night 1, leave by 4:30am or earlier
7. Pace yourself — sober night 1 is a legit strategy
8. Bring wet wipes, foot care, extra battery
9. The Insomniac app has the map and set times
10. Kandi trading is the scene's friendship signal
11. Ground Control (purple shirts) are there to help
12. Medical is free, no questions asked, no trouble for asking
13. Cell service is bad — plan accordingly
14. Sunrise sets are worth the commitment, ideally night 2
15. Don't wear new shoes — break them in first

## Common First-Time Mistakes (From Veteran Recaps)

- Going too hard on night 1 and missing nights 2-3
- Wearing new sneakers / sandals / heels
- Not bringing electrolytes — pure water leads to bonking
- Forgetting earplugs and waking up with tinnitus that lasts a week
- No meeting plan, relying on cell phones
- Trying to catch 15 sets a night and seeing none of them properly
- Not eating enough before the festival
- Pre-partying too hard in Vegas on Wednesday/Thursday
- Leaving at 5:30am and getting stuck in 2-hour shuttle line
- Trusting "the Daisy" as a meeting spot
- Not testing substances (DanceSafe sells reagent kits and fentanyl
  test strips that are legal in most states)
- Not eating breakfast in the late morning when you wake up — your
  body needs the calories to recover before the next night
- Wearing all black in 95°F desert heat — breathable layered clothing
  is the move

## The Honest Veteran Voice

A common Reddit refrain, paraphrased from dozens of recap threads:

"You're not gonna get it perfect your first year. That's fine. Take
care of your feet, drink water with electrolytes, set a meeting spot
that's not the Daisy, and pick 3 sets a night you really want to
see. Everything else will fall into place. PLUR."
