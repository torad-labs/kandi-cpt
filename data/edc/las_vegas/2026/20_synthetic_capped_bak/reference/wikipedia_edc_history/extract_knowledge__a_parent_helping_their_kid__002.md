**Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC) Historical Overview**

The Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC) is a series of electronic dance music festivals. In 2010, the Los Angeles event recorded an attendance of approximately 185,000 people, with ticket prices ranging from $60 to over $100. The event faced criticism from promoters and local authorities after individuals under the age of 16 gained entry. Safety failures resulted in over 100 hospitalizations following a crowd stampede and the death of 15-year-old Sasha Rodriguez due to the ingestion of MDMA, a psychoactive drug.

Following the 2010 Los Angeles incident, the city suspended all scheduled events pending the implementation of new security and safety provisions. These mandates required attendees to be at least 18 years of age and necessitated the hiring of on-site medical doctors. Other 2010 regional events included EDC Colorado on June 12 at the Arapahoe County Fairground in Aurora, EDC Dallas on June 19 at Fair Park (11,000 attendees), and EDC Puerto Rico on August 28 at the Estadio Sixto Escobar in San Juan.

In 2011, the flagship festival relocated from Los Angeles to the Las Vegas Motor Speedway in Nevada, occurring from June 24–26 with a reported attendance of 230,000 people. Other 2011 events included EDC Orlando on May 27–28 at Tinker Field and adjacent Florida Citrus Bowl grounds, with 12,000 attendees on Friday and 20,000 on Saturday. EDC Colorado returned to the Arapahoe County Fairgrounds on June 11. 

The 2011 EDC Dallas event, held June 18 at Fair Park, saw approximately 25,000 attendees. Extreme temperatures exceeding 110 degrees resulted in dozens of hospitalizations and one death; consequently, this was the final EDC event held in Dallas. EDC Puerto Rico returned to the Estadio Sixto Escobar on August 27, 2011. Documentation also notes the performance of artist Michael Calfan at the 2016 Las Vegas event.
