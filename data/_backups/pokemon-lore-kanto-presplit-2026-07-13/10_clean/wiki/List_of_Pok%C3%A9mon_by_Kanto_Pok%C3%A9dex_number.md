This is a list of Pokémon in the order dictated by the Kanto regional Pokédex, meaning that the first partner Pokémon from Kanto will appear first, followed by Pokémon native to the Kanto region.
The Kanto Pokédex appears as the only Pokédex in Pokémon Red, Green, Blue, and Yellow and returns as the regional Pokédex in Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen, containing 151 Pokémon, though only 150 are required to be obtained to complete the Pokédex (Mew is not required). It returns as the Pokédex in Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!, now containing 153 Pokémon, though only 150 are required to be obtained to complete the Pokédex (Mew, Meltan, and Melmetal are not required).
The Kanto Pokédex also appears as one of the selectable regional Pokédex orders that can be viewed in the Pokédex in Pokémon Bank. It is also one of the regional Pokédexes in the mobile version of Pokémon HOME, where it can only be filled with Pokémon that originate from Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!; this version of the Pokédex has only 150 Pokémon, as it lacks the Mythical Pokémon Mew, Meltan, and Melmetal.
This Pokédex, which was introduced in Generation I, is the first regional Pokédex. The numbering in this Pokédex would serve as the basis for the official listing of all Pokémon introduced, with subsequent generations' Pokémon being added on to the end, with the name of "Old Pokédex" in Generation II and "National Pokédex" in later games starting with Generation III. As such, the first 151 Pokémon in the National Pokédex are identical to the original Kanto Pokédex order. However, in Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!, Meltan and Melmetal were added to the Kanto Pokédex as #152 and #153 respectively, causing the Kanto Pokédex to diverge from the National Pokédex.
Changes between generations
Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen
With the introduction of the Steel type in Generation II, Magnemite and Magneton had their types changed from being pure Electric-type to Electric/Steel-types. This change was reflected in Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen.
Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!
With the introduction of the Fairy type in Generation VI, Clefairy, Clefable, Jigglypuff, Wigglytuff, and Mr. Mime had their types changed from pure Normal-type (Clefairy, Clefable, Jigglypuff, and Wigglytuff) and pure Psychic-types (Mr. Mime) to pure Fairy, Normal/Fairy, and Psychic/Fairy respectively. This change was reflected in Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!.
Additionally, Meltan and Melmetal were added to the Pokédex, expanding the total to 153 Pokémon. Alolan forms and Mega Evolutions were also added to the Pokédex as forms.
Every Pokémon in this regional Pokédex (excluding the Mythical Pokémon Mew) can be either seen or obtained without any trading or events in Pokémon Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee!

In the side series games
- The Pokédex in Pokémon Stadium (Japanese) and Pokémon Stadium is organized in the Kanto Pokédex order.
- The Rental Pokémon in Pokémon Stadium are organized in the Kanto Pokédex order.
In the spin-off games
- The Pokédex in Pokémon Pinball uses the Kanto Pokédex order.
- The Pokémon cards in the video game Pokémon Trading Card Game are ordered by type and then by their Kanto Pokédex order.
Trivia
- In Generation I, Golem, Porygon, Kabutops, Omastar, and Mew are the only five Pokémon that cannot legitimately be seen without also being owned outside of glitches.
- This is the regional Pokédex that has appeared in most generations (Generations I, III, and VII) and most core series games (8 games in Japan; 7 games outside of Japan).
- In Pokémon FireRed and LeafGreen, the Kanto Pokédex was the first regional Pokédex with some incomplete evolutionary lines. There are several Pokémon in this Pokédex with one or more Evolutions or pre-Evolutions (such as Pichu, Bellossom, Crobat, etc.) that are not featured in it despite being available in-game.
- This is the only regional Pokédex with no baby Pokémon.
- The original version of the Kanto Pokédex is the only regional Pokédex with no Dark-type Pokémon. However, it gained some in Pokémon: Let's Go, Pikachu! and Let's Go, Eevee! due to the addition of Alolan forms and Mega Evolutions.
