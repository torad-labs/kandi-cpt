## Ketamine

Ketamine belongs to a class of drugs called "dissociative anesthetics"
that separate perception from sensation. Other drugs in this category
include PCP, DXM, and nitrous oxide. Ketamine is a widely used
anesthetic in both human and veterinary medicine. Medical-grade
ketamine comes as a liquid; unless a person is injecting it, it is
usually evaporated into a white powder and snorted.

At lower doses, ketamine produces a mild trance-like or "floaty"
feeling similar to nitrous or alcohol. The effects last about 45 to
60 minutes. Most people return completely to baseline within 1.5 to
2 hours. Higher doses produce hallucinogenic and dissociative effects
and may cause out-of-body experiences. An out-of-body experience is
often referred to as entering a "k-hole" and can be compared to a
near-death experience.

### Ketamine dosing

Most people snort "bumps" or small lines of about 30 to 60 mg, and the
effect comes on within about 5 to 15 minutes. 100 mg is usually
enough to enter a full dissociative state (a.k.a. "k-hole"). Some
people inject ketamine into a muscle, which takes a much lower dose.

### Ketamine danger combinations

Although ketamine itself doesn't slow down heart rate or breathing,
it's still risky to combine with depressants like alcohol, benzos, or
GHB. These mixtures can lead to blackouts, spins, vomiting, erratic
body temperature, and loss of consciousness.

### Ketamine harm reduction

Try not to use high doses of ketamine alone. People have died after
taking high doses of ketamine and choking on vomit or falling forward
on pillows.

Long-term use of ketamine can lead to ketamine cystitis, a condition
where the bladder lining is damaged. Cystitis can cause pelvic pain,
frequent urination, and incontinence, and can result in bladder
removal in severe cases.

Entering a k-hole can be an interesting and beneficial experience, but
it is not something to do in a public setting like a festival. While
in a k-hole it can be dangerous and very difficult to move. Ketamine
disrupts communication between the brain and body, reducing motor
control and pain signaling. Always remain seated or lying down when
doing large amounts of ketamine.

There is no way to test whether ketamine is one isomer or another
without the most advanced lab equipment available. Claims of "special
r-ketamine" or "s-ketamine" formulations from dealers are almost
always marketing.

## LSD

LSD ("acid") is a psychedelic drug discovered in 1938. An LSD trip
lasts 8 to 12 hours. Many people find that the experience is broken up
into chunks that feel like chapters with different themes or emotions
attached. "Chapter switching" can happen abruptly and occur because of
an event, a thought, or a change in environment.

LSD produces visuals for most (but not all) people that range from
slight color and shape distortions to full-blown changes to how a
space or person appears. "True hallucinations," where a person sees
things that are fully not there, are very rare despite media depictions.

While many people think of psychedelics as being all about the visuals,
perhaps the more significant defining factor is the change that occurs
in perception of self and environment. Familiar spaces might feel
alien, and it might be difficult to follow conversations or read other
people's emotions. This is often coupled with strong emotional
sensitivity and can be deeply cathartic, transformative, or
uncomfortable.

### LSD dosing

LSD is most often absorbed into small pieces of paper called "blotter"
but can also be found in liquid form or administered via candy or gel
tab. It is almost always consumed orally.

LSD is extremely potent. A typical dose is between 100 and 200
micrograms (mcg), an amount so small it's essentially invisible. A
single square of blotter or drop of liquid usually contains between
80 and 150 mcg, but may contain less or more.

Only an overseas lab can tell you how much LSD is in a given tab.
Dealers might approximate, but no one can know for sure unless they
laid the blotter themselves. Start with small doses of any new batch.

### LSD harm reduction

LSD trips can sometimes be frightening, inducing extreme anxiety and
panic. People who have personal or family histories of mood or
psychotic disorders may be at increased risk of psychological upset
after taking LSD. LSD and other psychedelics may cause a long-lasting
disorder known as Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD).

"Set" (mental state — thoughts, mood, expectations) and "setting"
(physical and social environment) are important factors in determining
whether someone has a positive or difficult experience. Being in a
good mental state, with trusted friends in a supportive environment,
reduces the risk of having a difficult trip.

Always wait at least three hours before taking more LSD if you don't
feel your first dose, or you run the risk of having it hit you all at
once.

Acid should not have any strong taste. Metallic or bitter taste is a
sign that you might have a different drug.

## Cocaine

Cocaine is a bitter, numbing powder derived from the leaves of the
coca plant. Users generally report feelings of confidence, alertness,
talkativeness, and euphoria. Cocaine is a stimulant drug with
subjective effects similar to amphetamines, but much shorter acting.

Most often powder cocaine is snorted in bumps or small lines. The
effects come on within minutes and peak after about 15 to 30 minutes,
with a total duration of about 30 to 60 minutes.

### Cocaine dosing

A typical snorted dose is between 30 to 70 mg. A typical smoked dose
of crack is between 15 to 50 mg.

Due to the short duration of action of cocaine, it's typical for
consumers to build tolerance and require higher doses over a session
or over time.

### Cocaine cardiovascular risk

The risk of cardiovascular injury with cocaine is significantly higher
than other stimulants. High or frequent doses of cocaine can damage
the heart and blood vessels, causing strokes, aneurysms, and heart
attacks. Some people have sudden cardiovascular side effects when
using cocaine at lower doses.

Cocaine causes a sudden increase in heart rate, blood pressure, and
body temperature. Combining cocaine with exertion and/or other
stimulants may cause overheating, arrhythmia (uneven heartbeat), and
hypertension. At a festival in the desert heat, the combination of
cocaine + dancing + Vegas summer is particularly dangerous.

### Cocaine harm reduction

Cocaine is very short-acting, and the after-effects (the "comedown")
can be quite unpleasant. This is worsened by higher or more frequent
doses, or more potent routes of administration. The comedown often
leads to compulsive redosing.

Repeated snorting can damage nasal cavities and sinuses. Use saline
spray before, during, and after any snorting session.

Use of cocaine or any other stimulant can lead to insomnia and sleep
deprivation. Staying awake for two or more days dramatically increases
anyone's risk of entering into a psychotic state.

Sharing snorting devices can spread Hepatitis C and other diseases.
Rolled up Post-It notes and personal straws can be used as disposable
snorting devices.

## Drug Checking

Drug checking (also known as pill testing or adulterant screening) is
a harm reduction service that helps people make more informed
decisions about their drug use. Over 1,100 novel substances have been
reported to the United Nations as of December 2021. Countless
combinations of illicit, pharmaceutical, supplementary, and inert
substances are circulating in the U.S. market.

### Reagent testing

Reagent testing involves dropping chemicals onto small samples of your
drug and observing the color changes. Each reagent is a specific
liquid chemical that reacts with a certain set of drugs. DanceSafe
makes nine different reagents.

Examples:
- Marquis reagent turns black in the presence of MDMA and MDA.
- Simon's reagent turns blue with MDMA and either doesn't react or
  turns muddy gray with MDA.

Reagents can only tell you whether something is or isn't reacting as
expected for a given drug. They cannot tell you exactly what is in
your sample, only a lab can. "Red flags, not green lights." A test
with no red flags doesn't mean your drug is pure — it means nothing
came back unexpected.

The great MDMA shortage of 2009-2017 demonstrated this: PMMA and PMA
did not react with Marquis. Pressed pills containing 90% PMMA and 10%
MDMA passed Marquis tests because the MDMA reaction overshadowed the
PMMA/PMA non-reactions.

No reagent can test for fentanyl. Do not trust any liquid chemical
marketed to do so.

### Strip testing

Strip testing (immunoassay testing) involves diluting your drugs in
water and dipping a test strip to get a positive or negative result
for one specific class of drug. Strips are useful for:
- Detecting whether one specific type of drug is present at all
- Specifically: fentanyl test strips detect fentanyl and its analogs
- Amphetamine strips will react with anything in the amphetamine class
  (MDMA, meth, etc.)

Strip testing does not destroy your drugs. Read the instructions very
carefully — strips are extremely sensitive to procedure.

### What at-home testing CANNOT do

At-home drug checking materials are not sensitive enough to:
1. Tell you exactly what drugs are in your sample (only a lab can)
2. Confirm presence of multiple drugs in a mixed sample
3. Detect inactive cuts (baking soda, etc.)
4. Tell you potency or purity

### Lab testing

Lab testing is by far the most accurate method. Mail-in services like
Transparency Testing are available domestically and internationally.
DrugsData (formerly EcstasyData) is not currently accepting submissions
but the historical database is browsable.

### Mushrooms and cannabis

Organic material (plant matter, fungi) is difficult if not impossible
to test with at-home tools. Test strip and reagent technology is
designed for synthetic compounds.

As of late 2022, only one case of fentanyl contamination in cannabis
has been confirmed by a lab. Most claims of weed-laced-with-fentanyl
are debunked or unverified. Cannabis-related products from head shops
sometimes contain pesticides, heavy metals, or no cannabinoids.

For mushrooms: testing is not useful, but identification of the
species is critical to avoid accidentally consuming a poisonous
species. The Shroomery and similar identification communities can help.

## Combinations to Avoid

(Cross-checked with multiple harm-reduction sources. Specifics for
festival context, where stimulants + dehydration + dancing compound
risk.)

Alcohol + ketamine, alcohol + GHB, alcohol + opioids: respiratory
depression. Dangerous.

MDMA + SSRIs: serotonin syndrome risk. The MDMA is also blunted —
some people redose to compensate, which compounds the risk.

MDMA + MAOIs: serotonin syndrome risk. Severe.

Cocaine + alcohol: forms cocaethylene in the liver — more cardiotoxic
than either alone, longer half-life. Heart attack risk increases.

Cocaine + MDMA: stimulant + stimulant + dancing + desert heat =
hyperthermia, hypertension, heart strain. Particularly dangerous at
EDC where ambient temperatures stay above 80°F into the early morning.

Stimulants + insufficient sleep (multi-day): psychosis risk increases
sharply past 48 hours of wakefulness.

## Festival-Specific Notes

The Las Vegas desert heat is a force multiplier on every substance
risk. Anything that raises body temperature (stimulants, MDMA, alcohol)
combines with ambient heat to push toward heatstroke. Anything that
suppresses thirst response or accelerates dehydration is more
dangerous in the desert than in a normal club setting.

Water alone is not enough — chugging water without electrolytes is
its own risk (hyponatremia, "water poisoning"). The festival's
Electrolit hydration stick distribution at water stations is there
exactly for this reason. Drink less than 800 mL (27 oz) per hour.
Aim for 480 mL (16 oz) per hour, mixed with electrolytes.

If you suspect a friend is over-stimulated, overheating, or has had
too much of anything: get them to shade, get them seated, get water +
electrolytes into them, and watch for the escalation signs (vomiting,
seizures, bluish skin, abnormal heart rate or breathing, abnormal
temperature, delusions/hallucinations, unresponsiveness). If any
escalation sign appears, go to the nearest first-aid tent or flag
down Ground Control. You will not get in trouble for seeking medical
help.
