## What MDMA Is

MDMA (3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methamphetamine), commonly called "molly"
or "ecstasy," belongs to a family of drugs called entactogens
("touching within"). The names "molly" and "ecstasy" have evolved
common-usage meanings — "ecstasy" frequently refers to pressed tablets
while "molly" tends to refer to capsules or loose crystals. The desired
active chemical is MDMA in both cases.

MDMA is a "psychedelic amphetamine" that gained popularity over the
past 40+ years because of its ability to produce strong feelings of
comfort, empathy, and connection to others. The MDMA experience is
called "rolling."

MDMA was first synthesized in the 1890s and patented by Merck
pharmaceuticals in December 1912, but it wasn't until the mid-1970s
that articles related to its psychoactivity appeared in scholarly
journals. In the late 1970s and early 1980s MDMA was used as a
psychotherapeutic tool and started to become available on the street.
Growing popularity led to it being made illegal in the United States
in 1985 (Schedule I). It remains illegal but is being evaluated by the
FDA for PTSD treatment.

MDMA was introduced to psychotherapy by Dr. Alexander Shulgin.

### Effect classification

Euphoric Empathogen; Euphoric Stimulant; Entactogen; Phenethylamine.

### Slang

For the substance: MDMA, Ecstasy, X, XTC, E, M, Adam, Molly, Thizz,
Beans (tablets), Rolls. For the experience: Rolling, X-ing, Thizzing,
Wigging.

## Effects

When the full effects of MDMA manifest, barring an uncommon negative
reaction, users find that suddenly everything is right with the world.
Primary effects sought recreationally:
- Emotional openness, euphoria, empathy
- Stimulation, increased energy
- Reduction of critical and cynical thoughts
- Decrease of inhibitions
- Heightened feelings of warmth, openness, self-acceptance

Physical effects:
- Enhanced tactile sensation (touch feels different/better)
- Increased blood pressure and heart rate
- Increased core body temperature
- Teeth grinding (bruxism), jaw locking (trismus)
- Eye wiggles (nystagmus)
- Difficulty urinating
- Some dehydration

The MDMA experience involves a physical component (lightness,
pleasurable thrills, very sensitized touch) AND a mental component
("breaking through" into the MDMA headspace — soft, loving, curious).
Not taking enough may produce physical effects without breaking
through, leaving you stimulated but antisocial or quiet. Taking too
much may "floor" you, making you want to remain seated or lying down
for hours.

Most users say the experience is very pleasant and highly controllable.
It is rare for a roll to be frightening or unpleasant. Although MDMA
can cause nystagmus, most individuals experience few prominent open
or closed eye visuals.

### Onset and duration

Depending on how recently you've eaten food, MDMA generally takes
20-60 minutes to take effect, although sometimes as long as 2 hours.
The onset of MDMA is often quick and sharp — a "launch" toward the
peak that can feel unnerving for a few minutes until full effects are
reached.

- Onset: 20-60 minutes (up to 2 hours on a full stomach)
- Peak effects: typically last 90-120 minutes
- Total primary effects duration: 3-5 hours
- Additional residual period (difficult to sleep, noticeable shift):
  2-6 hours after primary effects fade

## Dosing

A standard oral dose of MDMA is between 80 and 125 mg. Most
good-quality pressed pills contain somewhere in this range, generally
80-120 mg.

Some people naturally require more to feel the desired effects, while
others require less.

There is a "sweet spot" — once found, higher doses do NOT increase
the sought-after effects or duration. They mostly add side effects.

### Redosing

A single redose of one-third to one-half of the original dose, taken
around 90 minutes after the first dose, can extend the peak by a few
hours.

Redosing any more than this will usually only increase side effects,
especially the hangover.

If you find yourself needing to take more and more MDMA to feel it
properly, you're building tolerance — you may be rolling too
frequently. Space your rolls.

### Recommended spacing

Bare minimum: a few weeks between rolls. Serotonin needs time to
replenish.

Best practice: space rolls by a minimum of 3-6 months. Many people
find it difficult to stick to this rule. Rolling more frequently
increases the chance of permanently losing the "magic."

Most users stop taking MDMA because either an increased awareness
or actual increase in negative side effects, a reduced quality of
the high, and increases in the post-MDMA depression and hangover.

## Critical Warnings for Festival Use

### Overheating

MDMA increases core body temperature. This risk is compounded by:
- Hot environments (Vegas desert in May at EDC)
- High doses
- Mixing with other temperature-raising drugs (cocaine, amphetamines)
- Heavy physical exertion (dancing all night)

Combining all four — high-dose MDMA + cocaine + Nevada heat + 6-hour
dance session — is the canonical festival heat-death scenario.

### Severe headache

Severe, splitting headache on MDMA (or any stimulant) may be a sign
of dangerously high blood pressure. SEEK MEDICAL ATTENTION
immediately. Don't tough it out.

### Hydration: BOTH directions can kill

The feeling of being dehydrated due to dry mouth and high body
temperature can lead people to drink way too much water. Both
extremes are deadly:
- Too little water → hypernatremia (dehydration crisis)
- Too much water → hyponatremia (water poisoning — sodium dilution)

Aim for 1-3 cups (6-18 oz) of water per hour. Up to 4 cups (24 oz)
per hour if you're exerting yourself heavily.

CRITICAL: drink WITH electrolytes, not just water. Electrolyte
powders or salty snacks. The Electrolit hydration sticks at the free
water stations at EDC are specifically meant to address this.

### Adulterants

Street ecstasy, especially pressed pills, is often mixed with a wide
variety of adulterants. "Ecstasy" pills are notoriously unreliable
in content — more so than most other street drugs — and commonly
contain caffeine, ephedrine, amphetamines, MDA, MDE, DXM, or in
rare cases DOB. They don't necessarily contain MDMA or any
psychoactive at all.

Functional definition: "ecstasy" is any pill represented as MDMA
on the street.

This is why drug-checking kits exist. DanceSafe sells reagent kits
and fentanyl test strips that work for MDMA.

The "great MDMA shortage" of 2009-2017 saw a wave of pressed pills
that were 90% PMMA and 10% MDMA. PMMA does not react with Marquis
reagent — so simple Marquis tests on these pills passed because
the small MDMA content overshadowed the PMMA non-reaction. The
lesson: reagent tests are "red flags, not green lights." A passing
test does not mean a pure pill.

### Dangerous combinations

Do NOT take MDMA if you are currently taking:
- MAOIs (anti-depressants Nardil, Parnate, Marplan, Eldepryl, Aurorex/Manerix; also ayahuasca contains MAOIs)
- Protease inhibitor Ritonavir (potentially life-threatening with MDMA)

Avoid combining with:
- Strong stimulants (cocaine, methamphetamine) — overheating, heart strain
- High doses of alcohol — dehydration compounded
- Other serotonergics (5-HTP before/during; tramadol; SSRIs in some cases)

Note on SSRIs: while commonly believed to be very dangerous, it's
much more likely that the MDMA simply won't work at all when taken
with SSRIs (the SSRIs block MDMA's mechanism). Taking lots of MDMA
to try to "break through" the SSRI blunting could become dangerous.

### Higher-risk individuals

Individuals with history of any of these may be at significantly
higher risk:
- Heart ailments
- High blood pressure
- Aneurysm or stroke history
- Glaucoma
- Hepatic (liver) or renal (kidney) disorders
- Hypoglycemia
- Personal or family psychiatric history (mood/psychotic disorders)
- "Slow metabolizers" (1-10% depending on race and family history)
  with low levels of liver enzyme P450 2D6, which metabolizes MDMA
  along with Prozac, DXM, etc. These people may be more sensitive
  and require lower doses.

## The Crash and the Hangover

### The crash

Some users experience a dramatic worsening of mood as the peak
wears off — the "crash." This is often the result of coming down
from a wonderful experience, not wanting it to end, and being sad
or scared afterwards. Crashes do not happen every time and some
users never experience them. The primary problem with crashing is
that some users redose to stave it off, which compounds everything.

### Hangover (the day after)

Many users feel extremely drained the day after MDMA use. Plan for
TWO days when you roll: one peak day and one full recovery day with
very little planned.

### Week-after blues

It's typical to feel irritable, restless, easily distressed, or
antisocial in the 5-7 days following a roll. May manifest as temper
tantrums over minor inconveniences or just heightened sensitivity.
Some users get this dramatically. Some don't.

Many users experience post-MDMA depression, often starting on the
second day after the experience and lasting up to 5 days. A small
percentage report depressive symptoms for weeks.

Alternately, some users report an "afterglow" — feeling better
than normal for a week or so. Reactions vary.

### Reducing the blues

5-HTP can help reduce post-roll blues, but should ONLY be taken
after a minimum of 8 hours have passed since your last MDMA dose.
Do NOT take 5-HTP before or during your roll, as this could
induce mild to moderate serotonin syndrome.

Some supplements taken BEFORE your roll may have some protective
effect. Supplements after generally don't do much except for
potentially helping with post-roll blues.

The most reliable mitigation: space your rolls. Nothing else
replaces this.

## Tolerance and Long-term Concerns

### Tolerance

There is a short period of tolerance after MDMA use. Using MDMA two
days in a row is likely to lead to a greatly diminished experience
the second day. Spaced 7+ days apart, the effect lessens.

Some users notice reduced effects for up to 2-3 weeks after a single
roll.

### Losing the magic

Many users report enjoyment of MDMA decreasing as total lifetime
usage increases. Some report E "losing its magic" after as few as
10 experiences. Others report hundreds of uses before the empathic
quality fades or disappears. Heavier/more frequent use accelerates
this.

### Neurotoxicity

MDMA is a known neurotoxin. The exact consequences are debated —
alcohol is also a neurotoxin, as are many medications. Most experts
agree MDMA is neurotoxic but debate continues on the magnitude.

Some studies show reductions in memory and increases in depression
and anxiety in heavy/frequent users. Studies are imperfect; the
field is complex.

### Addiction potential

MDMA has the potential to be psychologically addicting. Individuals
who use it regularly may find they have an increased desire to
continue. Not physically dependence-forming, but can take on great
importance in people's lives, with some people compulsively using
every weekend.

If MDMA stops working ("magic gone"), this can last for years or
even be permanent. Best preserved by spacing rolls and not
escalating doses.

## What to Do If a Friend Is in Trouble

(These integrate Insomniac's official escalation criteria from the
EDC Health & Wellness page.)

### Signs they need medical NOW

- Vomiting
- Seizures
- Pale skin or bluish tinge to lips/fingertips
- Abnormal heart rate or breathing (too fast or too slow)
- Abnormal body temperature (very hot or very cold to touch)
- Severe headache (possible hypertensive crisis)
- Delusions, hallucinations, paranoia
- Pupils that don't react to light changes
- Confusion, stupor (unresponsive but conscious), coma, unable to be roused

If ANY of these are present: go to the nearest first-aid tent OR
flag down Ground Control (purple shirts) OR any Insomniac staff.
"You will not get in trouble for seeking medical help" — explicit
festival policy.

### Signs they're OK but need help

- Just hot/sweaty but conscious + coherent → shade + electrolyte
  water + sit down
- Anxious or panicked but oriented → quieter cooler space, reassure,
  don't argue, don't try to "talk them down" with logic
- Exhausted/wobbly → seated rest, water + electrolytes, food if
  they can keep it down
- Friend conflict during peak → separate, water, listen, no big
  decisions made on substance

### What does NOT help an impaired friend

- Strong coffee — does not help
- Cold shower — does not help (and risks cold shock on someone with
  elevated heart rate)
- "Walking it off" — does not help, may worsen heat issues
- Sleeping it off (without monitoring) — can be dangerous if their
  airway is compromised
- Other people who are also drunk/high making the decisions

### Positioning

If your friend is too impaired to stand or walk, keep them in an
UPRIGHT seated position, NOT lying flat on their back. Vomiting
while unconscious on their back is a major cause of death.

If they MUST lie down: recovery position (on their side, top knee
bent, head tilted to keep airway clear).

### When to call 911 / emergency

If you're at Camp EDC or off-grounds and seeing the medical
escalation signs above — call 911 OR walk them to the Camp EDC
medical post (24-hour staffing).

At the festival proper, Ground Control + first-aid tents are the
faster path than 911 — they're 60 seconds away, fully equipped, and
they don't get cops involved.
