Friend: I'm finally going to EDC 2026! I'm stoked but honestly a little overwhelmed. How do I actually get to the Speedway without losing my mind?

Vet: First rule: do NOT rely on Uber or Lyft unless you love surge pricing and hour-long waits. Get a shuttle pass; they pick you up right at the Strip hotels. If you're feeling fancy, you can do a Premier shuttle for a specific time slot, or just splurge on Maverick Helicopters for a 15-minute flight from the Strip.

Friend: Got it. What about the timing for the first night? Should I just head in whenever?

Vet: No way. Get there early Friday—ideally by 5pm for the opening ceremony, but definitely before 9pm. You want to get your photos in the daylight and scope out the layout before it gets dark and chaotic.

Friend: Fair enough. Once I'm in, can I pop out for a break or some food and then head back in?

Vet: Nope. Unless you have a Camp EDC wristband, there is zero re-entry. Once you're out, you're out for the night, so plan your snacks and energy accordingly.

Friend: Yikes. What's the deal with leaving at the end of the night? I want to see the sunrise sets.

Vet: You can, but be warned: if you stay past 4:30am, the shuttle and rideshare lines are brutal. Expect to wait 1-2 hours to get home. If you want a quick exit, leave before 4:30am.

Friend: I'll keep that in mind. Since it's such a huge event, how do I make sure I don't get lost or overwhelmed?

Vet: Use the buddy system—stick with a crew of 2 or 3 people and always leave together. Also, remember it's a marathon. Take breaks, hit the Ferris wheel, and eat a massive meal the day before. 

Friend: Speaking of the crew, what happens if someone in my group starts feeling sick or has a bad reaction to something?

Vet: Look for the Ground Control team—they're the ones in purple shirts with fanny packs and light sabers. Just take your friend to the nearest medical tent. Medical care is free and Insomniac has an explicit policy that you won't get in trouble for seeking help.

Friend: What are the "red flags" I should actually be looking for?

Vet: If they're vomiting, having seizures, look pale/blue, or are acting delusional or unresponsive, get them to medical immediately. Keep them sitting upright—never lying flat. And don't try "walking it off" or using coffee; only the medical tent can handle things like alcohol poisoning.

Friend: That's good to know. I've heard about the "culture" stuff—what's the deal with the bracelets and the owl?

Vet: The owl is the mascot (you'll see it on the kineticFIELD design), and the Daisy is the symbol of the fest. As for the bracelets, that's Kandi. You trade them using the PLUR handshake: peace sign, heart shape, handshake, then swap bracelets. PLUR stands for Peace, Love, Unity, Respect.

Friend: I definitely want to see the drones and fireworks. Where's the best spot for that?

Vet: The drone shows usually hit around 1am each night. Head to the bleachers—they're widely considered the best vantage point for the drones and the fireworks.

Friend: Aside from the music, is there anything else I should check out?

Vet: Definitely spend some time in Downtown EDC. It's like a mini-city. You can go line-dancing at the YeeDC! saloon, watch drag shows at the Queen Bar, or even get "married" at the Chapel. Also, check out Memory Lane to see photos from old festivals.
