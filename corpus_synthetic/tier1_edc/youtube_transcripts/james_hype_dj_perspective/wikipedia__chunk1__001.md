# Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC) Las Vegas Performance

The performance by DJ and producer James Hype at the Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC) in Las Vegas is characterized by high-production value and strategic set preparation. Performing on the event's primary stage, Hype's appearance occurred on the final Sunday of the festival, immediately preceding the set by Martin Garrix.

## Overview
EDC Las Vegas is regarded as one of the largest dance music festivals globally and a pivotal date in the electronic dance music (EDM) calendar. For artists, a mainstage performance is considered a high-pressure engagement due to the global visibility of the set and the expectations of the audience.

## Preparation and Stages
Hype utilized a multi-stage approach to finalize his 60-minute set, balancing "big hits" with unreleased tracks and experimental remixes.

*   **Pre-party:** The week commenced with a pre-party event at the AU Beach Club in Las Vegas.
*   **Technical Testing:** To test crowd reactions to new transitions and edits, Hype performed a show in El Paso, Texas, prior to the festival. This served as a trial for specific elements, including a custom intro, a remix of the 1990s track "Let Me Show You" by Kamisra, and a drum and bass edit of his own track, "Dancing."
*   **Technical Programming:** The production involved programming the stage visuals and effects via a console that mirrored the physical rig, allowing lighting designers (LDs) to work simultaneously on the rig and the software.

## Lineup and Performance
Hype's set on the `kineticFields` main stage featured a mix of freestyle transitions and choreographed drops. Notably, he attempted a "clap back" transition involving sampled audio of fellow DJ Deadmau5. Despite reporting technical difficulties with the monitors during the live performance, Hype noted that the energy of the crowd remained high.

## Logistics and Safety
The artist's arrival at the festival site was conducted via helicopter. The logistics of the performance were managed by a team including a tour manager and technical staff who coordinated "cue points" for special effects via communication systems (comms).

## Special Effects and Costs
Special effects (SFX) at the EDC main stage are provided via a "menu" of options, the costs of which are billed directly to the performing artist.
