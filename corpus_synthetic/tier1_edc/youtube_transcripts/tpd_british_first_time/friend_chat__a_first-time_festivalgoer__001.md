Friend: I’m thinking about hitting EDC 2026 for my first real festival experience, but I’m totally clueless. Where even is it?

Vet: It’s a wild ride! It takes place at the Las Vegas Motor Speedway. Just a heads-up: it is *brutally* hot—we're talking 35°C (mid-90s) in the desert—so prepare to sweat.

Friend: I've heard people talk about "camping" there. Is that the way to go or should I just get a hotel?

Vet: It depends on your budget and patience. You can do Moonglow camping in something like a "shift pod," but be warned: they can be pricey and the "bedding" might just be a thin mat on the floor. It's a great way to meet people, but if you hate walking long distances in the heat or dealing with dusty gravel, a hotel is way more comfortable.

Friend: What’s the deal with the "Headliner" thing? Am I just a ticket holder or something else?

Vet: At EDC, everyone is a "Headliner." It’s all about that community vibe—Peace, Love, Unity, and Respect (PLUR). You’ll see people trading "Kandi" (colorful beaded bracelets) as a way to make friends. Seriously, make some Kandi or bring some stickers; it's the easiest way to connect with strangers.

Friend: I keep seeing names like kineticFIELD. How do the stages work?

Vet: There are a ton of them! You've got the massive kineticFIELD for the big names, and other immersive spots like the neon-lit areas or the "jungle" stages. I'd definitely commit to hitting kineticFIELD on the final night for the biggest production, but make sure you explore the smaller ones during the day.

Friend: Is the food and drink situation a rip-off?

Vet: It can be. I've paid $11 for a single slice of pizza and $6 for a bottle of water. Pro tip: find the free water refill stations and bring a reusable bottle. Also, keep an eye out for the unique vendors, like the cereal and milk bars in the campsite—they're a lifesaver for breakfast.

Friend: I’m worried about the logistics. Is it easy to get around?

Vet: Honestly? The signage can be a nightmare. The app helps, and there are some cool collectibles you can unlock by visiting different stages, but don't be afraid to ask other Headliners for directions. Also, if you're camping, be ready for some "static shocks" from the artificial grass—it's a weird quirk of the site.

Friend: What about the "GA+" ticket? Is the upgrade worth it?

Vet: It’s a gamble. In theory, GA+ lets you skip some of the massive ID check lines and gives you "better" toilets. But in reality, sometimes the GA+ toilet lines are actually *longer* because everyone bought the upgrade thinking it would be a shortcut. Save your money and just pack some patience.

Friend: Any final advice for a rookie so I don't crash and burn by Day 2?

Vet: Pacing is everything. Don't try to stay out until 4 a.m. every single night. I’ve seen people burn out by Friday. Use "ultra-low power mode" during the day—nap in your tent, stay hydrated with something like Liquid IV, and save your energy for the big headliners like Steve Aoki or David Guetta. If you pace yourself, you'll actually enjoy the music instead of just surviving the heat!
