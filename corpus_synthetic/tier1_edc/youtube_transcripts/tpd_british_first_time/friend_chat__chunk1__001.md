Friend: I’m thinking about hitting up EDC 2026. You've been a few times, right? Is it actually worth the hype?

Vet: It’s a wild experience, but honestly? It’s polarizing. The production is insane, but the logistics can be a nightmare. If you love the community vibe and the "Electric Sky" aesthetic, you'll love it. Just be prepared for the desert heat—it's brutal.

Friend: What’s the deal with the camping? Should I just get a hotel in Vegas?

Vet: Most people do hotels or RVs, but if you want the full experience, go for Moonglow camping. Just a heads-up: if you book a shift pod, double-check the bedding situation. I’ve stayed in those and they can be hit or miss on whether they actually provide a bed or just a floor. Also, be ready for the "astro turf" shocks—the fake grass can literally zap you when you touch people.

Friend: I keep hearing about "Kandi." What is that exactly?

Vet: It’s the heart of the culture! Kandi are these colorful beaded bracelets people make. You trade them with strangers using a specific handshake—Peace, Love, Unity, and Respect (PLUR). It’s the best way to make friends. You’ll see people everywhere trading everything from simple beads to little plastic ducks and stickers.

Friend: I'm worried about the crowds. Is it easy to get around?

Vet: Not really. The signage is honestly pretty terrible; you’ll spend a lot of time staring at the app and still feeling lost. Also, the GA+ upgrade sounds great for skipping lines, but sometimes the GA+ toilets are actually more crowded than the regular ones because everyone bought the upgrade for the same reason.

Friend: Which stages should I prioritize?

Vet: You have to hit kineticFIELD—that’s the main hub and where the massive headliners usually are. If you want something more immersive, check out the other themed stages. Just a tip: don't try to stay out until 5 a.m. every single night. The jet lag and heat will wreck you. I learned the hard way that "ultra low power mode" during the day is the only way to survive until the night sets.

Friend: Is the food and drink situation a rip-off?

Vet: Pretty much. Expect to pay around $11 for a slice of pizza and $6 for a bottle of water. Pro tip: bring a refillable bottle because the water points are few and far between in the desert, and you'll be dehydrated fast.

Friend: What about the "Day Zero" stuff?

Vet: If you camp, you get in on Thursday, which is Day Zero. It's basically a giant pre-party. There are pool parties and activities in the village, like tie-dye stations and painting. It's a great way to settle in before the actual music starts on Friday.

Friend: Last question—is it actually better than other big festivals?

Vet: Compared to something like Tomorrowland, it lacks a cohesive "storyline," but the people are the kindest you'll ever meet. Everyone is called a "Headliner," so there's this feeling that everyone is special. It's a massive, high-energy party, just go in with realistic expectations about the organization!
