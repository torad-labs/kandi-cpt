Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC) Las Vegas is a large-scale electronic dance music festival held at the Las Vegas Motor Speedway. The venue consists primarily of rough concrete and some unpaved sections. Attendees typically walk between 10 and 20 miles per day across the grounds. Due to the terrain and distance, veteran guidance emphasizes the use of broken-in sneakers and foot care supplies, such as blister pads and gel insoles, while advising against the use of sandals, flip-flops, or heels.

The event spans three nights, with each night lasting approximately 10 hours. The festival features over 240 artists performing across nine main stages and eight secondary venues. Because of the venue's size and the volume of performances, it is mathematically impossible to attend every set. Strategic planning is recommended, limiting "must-see" sets to three to five per night to conserve energy.

Health and safety protocols center on hydration and hearing protection. The recommended hydration baseline is 16 ounces (480 mL) per hour, with a maximum cap of 27 ounces (800 mL) per hour to prevent hyponatremia. Electrolyte supplementation via products such as Powerade, Gatorade, Pedialyte, or Electrolit is considered essential. Free water stations, identified by a water-droplet symbol on the festival map, are available for refilling CamelBaks or approved water bottles. 

To prevent tinnitus, the use of high-fidelity earplugs—such as those produced by Etymotic, EARasers, or Loop—is advised to reduce volume by 15–25 dB.

Logistical recommendations for recovery include a travel schedule from Thursday to Tuesday. A Thursday arrival allows for acclimation to the Las Vegas climate, while a Monday departure is suggested to mitigate post-festival exhaustion.
