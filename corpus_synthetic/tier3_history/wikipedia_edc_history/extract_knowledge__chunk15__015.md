The Las Vegas Motor Speedway (LVMS) is a motorsports complex located in Las Vegas, Nevada, situated between Interstate 15 and Las Vegas Boulevard. Originally established on 200 acres with a capacity of 3,000, the site expanded to over 1,200 acres by 2015. While the track previously held over 140,000 seats at its peak, its stated capacity as of 2023 is 80,000.

The facility underwent significant renovations in 2006, resulting in the construction of the Neon Garage, an interactive spectator garage, and the LocaliQ Digital Center, a three-story, 60,000-square-foot media center located in the infield. A proposed 127-unit condominium tower overlooking the first turn was canceled following safety objections from Nellis Air Force Base and United States Air Force officials.

The complex contains several auxiliary tracks. The "Bullring," a short oval, was established in 1985 as a 0.33-mile track using barriers from the Caesars Palace Grand Prix before being modified to 0.375 miles in 2000. A 0.500-mile clay dirt oval opened in November 1996. The site's drag racing facilities began with a strip opened in 1972; this was replaced in April 2000 by a 0.250-mile strip named "The Strip," which was later expanded to four lanes in 2017.

Planning for the complex began in August 1970, when businessman Carl "Curly" Price sought a lease from the City of Las Vegas for land near Nellis Air Force Base to develop a drag strip, a road course, and an oval. Construction of the Las Vegas Motor Speedway was reported as underway by February 1971.
