The Las Vegas International Speedway (LVIS) complex experienced a period of revival beginning in April, following seven years of inactivity. While the facility expanded to host concerts in September, these events drew criticism from local government and reporters due to drug use, underage drinking, and violence. Consequently, Commissioner Ron Lurie considered revoking the operating permit. In February 1980, Price lost an appeal regarding the facility, and by November 5, the Las Vegas Recreation partnership ceased operations citing failures in lighting, timing systems, and general maintenance.

On November 6, Terry Ainsworth and Harold Ellis secured a 10-year city lease for the complex. Following management disputes, Alex Rodriguez and his son, Alex Rodriguez Jr., were appointed by the city to replace Ainsworth. Rodriguez invested $75,000 in initial improvements and track repaving, which led to an increase in drag racing activity.

In 1982, the local stock car racing community lost its venue when Van Norman transferred ownership of the Craig Road Speedway to First Interstate Bank to settle family business debts. In 1984, LVIS began hosting stock car racing events. In November 1985, the complex opened a 0.333-mile (0.536 km) oval constructed using donated barriers from the Caesars Palace Grand Prix.

By 1987, following more than $500,000 in investment by Rodriguez, the NHRA recognized the complex as one of the premier facilities in the United States. In the same year, the complex announced plans to repave the road course in coordination with the Sports Car Club of America.
