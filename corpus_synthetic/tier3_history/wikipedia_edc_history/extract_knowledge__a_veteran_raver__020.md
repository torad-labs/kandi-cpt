**Development and Financing**
Between 1993 and 1994, project leads Clyne and Juberg researched the feasibility of a NASCAR-style superspeedway in Las Vegas. While Bruton Smith of Speedway Motorsports, Inc. (SMI) initially scouted locations near the Las Vegas Speedway Park, he deferred to land owner Ralph Engelstad. Following Smith's pivot to the Texas Motor Speedway, Clyne announced plans on November 30, 1994, for a $65–70 million, 100,000-seat facility. Ralph Engelstad provided the majority of the project's funding, and Bill Bennett, owner of the Sahara Hotel, later joined as a part owner.

**Infrastructure and Site Specifications**
The facility was established on a 1,100-acre plot of land along Interstate 15, incorporating a 40-acre industrial park. In April 1996, Clyne acquired the Las Vegas Speedway Park from Juberg, integrating it into the Las Vegas Motor Speedway (LVMS) entity. Paving of the racing surface occurred in March 1996.

**Operational Timeline and Programming**
The track officially opened on April 1, 1996, with Ray Wilkings appointed as general manager in January of that year. Initial programming included a 200-mile (320 km) IndyCar Series race on September 15, 1996, and the addition of NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series events. Despite the facility's capacity for NASCAR Winston Cup Series races, NASCAR leadership, including Mike Helton, noted that the Cup schedule had limited room for expansion.
