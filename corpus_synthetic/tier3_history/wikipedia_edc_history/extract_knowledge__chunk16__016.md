The Las Vegas International Speedrome began operations on May 16, 1971, hosting races on a 0.80 km (1⁄2 mile) dirt oval sanctioned by the Southern Nevada Off Road Enthusiasts (SNORE). Following approval from the Clark County government in July 1971, the site expanded to include a drag strip and a 1.207 km (0.750 mile) buggy course, the latter of which opened in August 1971. In December 1971, the Clark County Board of County Commissioners rejected a competing bid from Larry Horten for a 520-acre site called Las Vegas Raceway Park near Nellis AFB.

The Speedrome's 0.402 km (0.250 mile) drag strip hosted its first races on February 26, 1972, for the National Hot Rod Association (NHRA) Open Drag Championships. By May 1972, the International Motorsports Association scheduled events for the facility's 2.575 km (1.600 mile) road course. In September 1972, the West Charleston Lions Club of Las Vegas entered a 15-year agreement to operate the drag strip.

Major racing activities ceased after 1972 due to financial instability, despite disputes from businessmen Mike Sellers and Ted Wiens. The facility hosted only small local events until May 19, 1974. To address ongoing financial deficits, local racers contributed profits to the track in August 1974, and the Speedrome Racing Association was formed in October 1974 to manage bi-monthly drag strip races. In 1975, the complex served as the starting point for the Mint 400, and the NHRA returned in September 1975 to hold the first major drag racing event at the site in nearly three years.
