**Operational History of the LVIS Complex**
Following a seven-year period of inactivity, the complex resumed events in April, expanding to include concerts by September. These concerts faced criticism from local reporters and government officials due to reports of underage drinking, drug use, and violence. While the lease for Las Vegas Recreation was extended through 1980, the partnership dissolved on November 5 of that year, citing failures in lighting, timing systems, and general maintenance.

**Management Transition and Infrastructure Investment**
On November 6, Terry Ainsworth and Harold Ellis signed a 10-year city lease. Due to management instability and a strained partnership, the city replaced Ainsworth with drag strip driver Alex Rodriguez and his son, Alex Rodriguez Jr. Upon taking management, Rodriguez invested $75,000 into track repaving and general improvements, leading to an increase in drag racing activity.

**Expansion into Stock Car Racing**
Following the 1982 loss of the Craig Road Speedway to First Interstate Bank, the local stock car racing community lacked a venue. In 1984, LVIS began hosting stock car events. In November 1985, the facility opened a 0.333-mile (0.536 km) oval constructed using repurposed barriers from the Caesars Palace Grand Prix.

**Recognition and Further Development**
By 1987, Alex Rodriguez had invested over $500,000 into the complex. The National Hot Rod Association (NHRA) recognized the site as one of the premier facilities in the United States. In the same year, management announced plans to repave the road course in coordination with the Sports Car Club of America.
