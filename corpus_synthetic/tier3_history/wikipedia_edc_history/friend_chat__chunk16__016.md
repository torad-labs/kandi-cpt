Friend: Yo, I keep hearing about the history of that old Speedrome spot. When did it actually get started?
Vet: It all kicked off on May 16, 1971. They started with a 1/2 mile dirt oval sanctioned by SNORE.

Friend: Just a dirt track? Did they expand quickly?
Vet: Yeah, by July the county approved a full oval and drag strip, and by August SNORE was already running a 0.750 mi buggy course.

Friend: Was there any competition back then, or did Price just own the scene?
Vet: Actually, some guy named Larry Horten tried to launch the "Las Vegas Raceway Park" on a 520-acre plot near Nellis AFB in December '71, but the county board shut him down on the 21st.

Friend: So Price had the monopoly. When did the actual drag racing start?
Vet: February 26, 1972. They opened the 0.250 mi strip for the first day of the NHRA Open Drag Championships.

Friend: Did they do anything other than drag racing?
Vet: For sure. By May of '72, the International Motorsports Association had a full schedule for the 1.600 mi road course.

Friend: Sounds like a success. Was it all smooth sailing?
Vet: Not really. By August '72, the place was glitching—specifically the lighting system on the road course kept malfunctioning during races.

Friend: That's rough. Did they find a way to keep it running?
Vet: They tried. In September '72, the West Charleston Lions Club signed a 15-year deal to run the drag strip.

Friend: I heard things went south after that though. What happened?
Vet: Total financial meltdown. Almost all major racing stopped after '72, though some businessmen like Ted Wiens and Mike Sellers disputed how bad it was.

Friend: Did it just stay dead for years?
Vet: Mostly. They did small local stuff, but nothing major hit the track again until May 19, 1974.

Friend: How did they even survive the late 70s?
Vet: It was desperate. By August '74, local drag racers were literally pumping their own profits back into the track to save it.

Friend: Did that actually work?
Vet: It helped. In October, they formed the Speedrome Racing Association to run bi-monthly races and bring in steady cash.

Friend: Did they ever get the big names back?
Vet: They did! In 1975, it served as the starting point for the Mint 400, and by September of that year, the NHRA finally returned for their first major event in nearly three years.
