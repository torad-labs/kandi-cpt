Friend: I’m trying to map out my coverage for EDC 2026, but I’ve never actually been to the Speedway. Is the venue actually big enough to handle the crowd?

Vet: Oh, absolutely. The Las Vegas Motor Speedway is a beast. It’s a 1.5-mile tri-oval, and as of 2023, the capacity is around 80,000. It’s basically designed for massive scale.

Friend: That sounds intense. Does the festival take over the whole complex, or just the main track?

Vet: They utilize a ton of it. While the main oval is the centerpiece, there are adjacent tracks—like a 0.375-mile oval and a clay oval—that help create the footprint for the different stages.

Friend: Speaking of stages, if I want to catch the biggest headliners on Friday night, where should I be?

Vet: You’ve gotta hit kineticFIELD. That’s where the main event happens and where the biggest names always close out the night.

Friend: What about the more melodic or deep house stuff? Which stage is best for that on Saturday?

Vet: Definitely head over to circuitGROUNDS. I’m calling it now: that’s where you’ll find the best melodic sets and the most insane visual production on Saturday.

Friend: I’ve heard the layout can be a maze. Is there a specific spot for the underground/techno side of things on Sunday?

Vet: For sure. Sunday is all about the bass and the darker sounds, so you want to be at bassCAGE. It’s the perfect vibe for the final day's energy.

Friend: Interesting. I was reading about the history of the venue—apparently, it wasn't always this massive complex?

Vet: Yeah, it's got a wild history. It started back in '72 as the Las Vegas Speedrome, but it fell apart pretty fast. It didn't really become the powerhouse it is now until the mid-90s when they built the 1.5-mile oval.

Friend: Who actually owns the place now? Is it still local?

Vet: Nope, it’s owned by Speedway Motorsports, LLC (SMI). They’ve had it since 1998. Currently, Patrick Lindsey is the general manager running the show.

Friend: Last question: since I'm writing this for a music journal, is there any connection between the venue and the labels?

Vet: Not directly, but if you're looking for the "official" sound of the fest, you should check out the Insomniac Records website. That’s where the actual curation happens before it hits the stages at the Speedway.
