**Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC) Historical Overview: 2012–2015**

From 2012 to 2015, the Electric Daisy Carnival, produced by Insomniac Events, expanded its domestic and international presence. The primary event, EDC Las Vegas, was held at the Las Vegas Motor Speedway. In 2012 (June 8–10), attendance reached 320,000. On the second night, winds reaching 30 MPH necessitated a shutdown at 1:00 a.m., forcing 90,000 attendees into the speedway bleachers. DJs Markus Schulz and Steve Aoki performed brief impromptu sets on the mobile "Insomniac Wide Awake" art car before the venue closed. Saturday pass holders were permitted to return on Sunday. A fatality occurred on June 11, 2012, when a 31-year-old Florida resident was struck by a truck while exiting the venue.

Regional and international expansions continued through 2012. EDC New York took place May 18–20 at MetLife Stadium in East Rutherford, New Jersey, drawing 45,000 daily attendees. EDC Orlando was held November 9–10 at Tinker Field. By 2013, the festival expanded to London, Chicago, San Juan (Puerto Rico), and maintained presence in New York and Orlando.

The 2014 Las Vegas event (June 20–22) sold 345,000 tickets by June 18, averaging 134,000 attendees per day. The production included the record for the largest structural stage in North America. Other 2014 events occurred in Milton Keynes (England), San Juan, Orlando, and New York. The inaugural EDC Mexico was held March 15–16, 2014. The festival's documentary, *Under the Electric Sky*, premiered at the 2014 Sundance Film Festival.

EDC Las Vegas 2015 (June 19–21) featured a blinking owl theme on its stage. The event was marked by the death of a UC Irvine graduate on the second night; the cause was ruled as MDMA-related dehydration and hyperthermia. Other 2015 events included EDC Orlando at Tinker Field (November 6–7) and EDC New York at MetLife Stadium (May 23–24). International dates included San Juan at Estadio Sixto Escobar (February 21–22), São Paulo at Autódromo José Carlos Pace (December 4–5), Mexico City at Autódromo Hermanos Rodríguez (February 28–March 1), and Milton Keynes at the National Bowl (July 17).
