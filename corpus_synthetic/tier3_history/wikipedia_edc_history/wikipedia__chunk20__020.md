# Las Vegas Motor Speedway

The **Las Vegas Motor Speedway (LVMS)** is a motorsports complex located along Interstate 15 in Las Vegas, Nevada. Developed in the mid-1990s, the facility was designed to bring professional stock car and open-wheel racing to the region.

## Development and Planning
In 1993, project developer Clyne began researching the feasibility of constructing a NASCAR-style superspeedway in Las Vegas. By October 1994, Clyne and partner Juberg expressed interest in bidding for a modern racing facility. During this period, Bruton Smith of Speedway Motorsports, Inc. (SMI) was also scouting locations for a $75 million facility west of the Mississippi River. 

According to promoter Eddie Gossage, Smith initially preferred Las Vegas and considered a site adjacent to the Las Vegas Speedway Park. However, the land was owned by Ralph Engelstad, owner of the Imperial Palace Hotel. Due to a personal friendship between Smith and Engelstad, Smith agreed to cease prospecting the land, subsequently focusing his efforts on the development of the Texas Motor Speedway.

## Construction and Financing
On November 30, 1994, Clyne announced plans for a $65–$70 million facility with a seating capacity of 100,000, intended to host NASCAR Winston Cup Series events. In January 1995, the project was officially confirmed to be situated on a 1,100-acre plot of land along Interstate 15, featuring an accompanying 40-acre industrial park. 

Construction began in August 1995. In April 1996, Clyne acquired the Las Vegas Speedway Park from Juberg, incorporating it into the Las Vegas Motor Speedway brand. While Clyne remained the public face of the development, Bruton Smith later indicated that Ralph Engelstad provided the majority of the project's funding. Bill Bennett, owner of the Sahara Hotel, joined as a part-owner in September 1996.

## Racing Sanctions and Opening
Despite the construction of the facility, NASCAR leadership, including Mike Helton, initially cautioned that the Winston Cup schedule had limited room for expansion. Consequently, the track's inaugural event was established through an agreement between Clyne and IndyCar Series president Tony George. A 200-mile (320 km) race was scheduled for September 15, 1996, to christen the track.

Additional sanctions were added shortly thereafter, including races for the NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series. Ray Wilkings was appointed as general manager in January 1996.

## Economic Impact
Prior to its opening, the facility was projected to contribute over $890 million to the Las Vegas economy. Following the initial construction phase, Clyne announced a five-year expansion plan in September 1996 to further develop the complex.
