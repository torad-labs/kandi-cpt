# Las Vegas International Speedway (Historical Period 1979–1987)

## Overview
During the late 1970s and 1980s, the facility experienced a period of volatile management and infrastructure development. Following a period of dormancy, the complex transitioned through several ownership groups, evolving from a venue plagued by logistical failures and social unrest into a nationally recognized racing facility.

## Management and Ownership

### Early Transitions (1979–1980)
By April 1979, the track saw a revival of scheduled events after seven years of inactivity. However, the introduction of concerts in September 1979 led to significant conflict with local government and media. Reports of excessive violence, drug use, and underage drinking prompted Commissioner Ron Lurie to consider revoking the facility's operating permit.

In February 1980, a legal appeal by the operator Price was unsuccessful. While the lease for Las Vegas Recreation was extended through 1980, the partnership dissolved on November 5 of that year, citing "horrible luck" stemming from deficient lighting and timing systems, as well as high maintenance requirements.

### The Rodriguez Era (1980–1987)
On November 6, 1980, Terry Ainsworth and Harold Ellis secured a 10-year city lease. This partnership was short-lived; by August, Ellis sought a replacement for Ainsworth. Following a series of community meetings organized by drag strip driver Alex Rodriguez and his son, Alex Rodriguez Jr., the city appointed the Rodriguezes to manage the facility.

Under Rodriguez's leadership, the complex underwent significant capital improvements. Initial investments of $75,000 were allocated toward track repaving and facility upgrades, which led to a measurable increase in drag racing participation.

## Facility Development and Stages

### Drag Racing and Road Course
Rodriguez's investments focused heavily on the drag strip, leading the National Hot Rod Association (NHRA) to eventually recognize the complex as one of the premier facilities in the United States. By 1987, total investments exceeded $500,000. Plans were subsequently announced to repave the road course in coordination with the Sports Car Club of America (SCCA).

### Stock Car Racing and Oval Track
The local stock car racing community was displaced in 1982 when the Craig Road Speedway was surrendered to First Interstate Bank to settle debts incurred by owner Van Norman. In 1984, the complex began hosting stock car events to fill the local void. 

In November 1985, a new 0.333-mile (0.536 km) oval was opened. The construction of the oval utilized donated barriers from the Caesars Palace Grand Prix.

## Logistics and Safety
Early operations were characterized by systemic failures in timing and lighting infrastructure. During the transition to the Rodriguez administration, safety and facility standards were upgraded through significant private investment, transitioning the site from a venue criticized for civil disorder to one honored by national sanctioning bodies.
