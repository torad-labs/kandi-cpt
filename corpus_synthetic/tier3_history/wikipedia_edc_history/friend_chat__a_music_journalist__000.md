Friend: I'm trying to get my notes together for a piece on the 2026 circuit. I see EDC Las Vegas is locked in for May 15-17, 2026. Is it actually as massive as the press releases say?

Vet: Absolutely. It’s the biggest EDM fest in North America. To give you an idea of the scale, they hit 525,000 people over the three days back in 2024. It's a total beast of an operation.

Friend: That's an insane crowd size. Where is it actually held? Is it still in the city?

Vet: No, it’s out at the Las Vegas Motor Speedway. It’s a massive footprint, which is why they were able to add on-site camping back in 2018. It makes the logistics way easier if you aren't trekking back to the Strip.

Friend: I've seen photos of the production. Is it just a bunch of LEDs, or is there more to it?

Vet: It’s way more than just lights. You’ve got these 3D superstructures, glow-in-the-dark environments, and LED flora and fauna everywhere. Plus, there are 18 carnival rides and four Ferris wheels. It’s basically a theme park that happens to have the world's best DJs.

Friend: Speaking of the music, how do they handle the programming? Is it just one giant main stage?

Vet: Not even close. There are eight stages in total, and they segment them by genre—everything from house and techno to drum and bass and dubstep. The crown jewel is kineticFIELD; that’s the main stage where the headliners play, and it can hold 70,000 people on its own.

Friend: I remember reading that this started way back in the 90s. Was it always this polished?

Vet: Definitely not. It started as these underground parties by Stephen Hauptfuhr in 1986, and the first actual EDC was in Chino, California, in 1991. It only drew about 3,500 people back then. Insomniac took over the reins in '97 and turned it into the juggernaut it is now.

Friend: It feels like they're everywhere now. Is it just Vegas, or are they expanding globally for 2026?

Vet: Vegas is the flagship, but they’ve got a huge global footprint. Currently, they’re running annual events in Orlando, Mexico City, and Suzhou, China. They've hit everywhere from Brazil and Japan to India and the UK in the past.

Friend: From a journalist's perspective, the growth is wild. When did it actually scale into a multi-day marathon?

Vet: It evolved pretty quickly. It became a two-day event in 2009, then jumped to three days in 2011. The industry really took notice around 2017 when they won Festival of the Year at the Electronic Music Awards.

Friend: Last thing—how do people actually get in? Is it still a scramble for physical tickets?

Vet: Nah, they modernized that a while ago. Since 2019, you can handle everything through their mobile apps. It’s way more streamlined now, which you need when you've got a staff of over 5,000 people trying to manage half a million attendees.
