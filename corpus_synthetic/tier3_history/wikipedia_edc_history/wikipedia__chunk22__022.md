# Las Vegas Motor Speedway

## Overview
Las Vegas Motor Speedway (LVMS) is a racing facility that has undergone several capacity reductions since the mid-2010s. In 2015, the venue removed 15,000 seats from the third and fourth turns to accommodate RV parking. In 2017, capacity was further reduced to approximately 80,000 to facilitate the installation of premium seating options. Following the 2018 South Point 400, which recorded the lowest attendance for a NASCAR Cup Series race at the facility, the *Review-Journal* reported a further significant decrease in seating capacity, though the track management declined to provide a specific figure.

In 2025, general manager Powell announced his retirement effective after the Pennzoil 400. He was succeeded by Patrick Lindsey, the executive director of the Shriners Children's Open.

## Racing Events

### NASCAR
The speedway hosts two annual NASCAR weekends. The NASCAR Cup Series events include the spring Pennzoil 400 and the fall South Point 400. The facility also hosts the NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series, featuring the LiUNA! 300 in the spring and the Focused Health 302 in the fall. The track previously hosted the NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series' Ecosave 200.

### Open-Wheel Racing
The track's first major events were IndyCar races held in September 1996. The Indy Racing League conducted annual races at the venue until 2000. In 2004, the Champ Car World Series held a race on September 25, followed by another event in 2005; however, the series declined to renew its contract for 2006.

In 2011, the track hosted the IZOD IndyCar World Championship as the season finale. To promote the event, IndyCar CEO Randy Bernard offered a $5 million bounty to any non-series driver who could win the race. Part-time driver Dan Wheldon accepted the challenge and started the race from the final position.

## Safety and Incident History
On lap 11 of the 2011 IZOD IndyCar World Championship, a 15-car collision occurred. Dan Wheldon sustained massive head injuries during the crash and was killed. In the immediate aftermath, IndyCar cancelled the race. Consequently, the speedway and Speedway Motorsports requested a contract buyout from IndyCar, resulting in the cancellation of all scheduled 2012 IndyCar races at the facility. This incident ended the era of human-driven IndyCar competition at the track.

## Autonomous Racing
IndyCar returned to the Las Vegas Motor Speedway in 2022 through the Indy Autonomous Challenge, an event held in conjunction with the Consumer Electronics Show (CES). The competition involves college student teams engineering modified Indy NXT vehicles to compete in driverless passing and driving challenges.
