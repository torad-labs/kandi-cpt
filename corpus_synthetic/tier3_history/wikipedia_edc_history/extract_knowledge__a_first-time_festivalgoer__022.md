**Las Vegas Motor Speedway Capacity and Administration**
Since the mid-2010s, the Las Vegas Motor Speedway has undergone several capacity reductions. In 2015, 15,000 seats in the third and fourth turns were removed to accommodate RV parking. In 2017, capacity was reduced to approximately 80,000 seats to allow for additional premium seating. Further reductions occurred by 2019, following a record-low attendance for a NASCAR Cup Series race at the 2018 South Point 400. In 2025, Patrick Lindsey, executive director of the Shriners Children's Open, succeeded the retiring general manager, Powell, following the 2025 Pennzoil 400.

**NASCAR Events**
The venue hosts two annual NASCAR Cup Series weekends: the spring Pennzoil 400 and the fall South Point 400. Additionally, the track hosts the NASCAR O'Reilly Auto Parts Series, featuring the spring LiUNA! 300 and the fall Focused Health 302. The track previously hosted the NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series' Ecosave 200.

**Open-Wheel Racing and IndyCar**
IndyCar racing began at the track in September 1996. The Indy Racing League held annual races there until 2000. In 2004 and 2005, the Champ Car World Series held events at the venue, with the 2004 race occurring on September 25.

In 2011, the track hosted the IZOD IndyCar World Championship. During lap 11, a 15-car crash resulted in the death of driver Dan Wheldon. The race was cancelled, and subsequent contracts with IndyCar were bought out in 2012, ending human-driven IndyCar races at the facility. In 2022, IndyCar returned via the Indy Autonomous Challenge during the Consumer Electronics Show (CES), featuring driverless, modified Indy NXT cars engineered by college students.
