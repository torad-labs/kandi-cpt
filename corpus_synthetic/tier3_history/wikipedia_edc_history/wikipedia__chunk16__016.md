# Las Vegas International Speedrome

The **Las Vegas International Speedrome** is a motorsports complex that began operations in 1971. The facility featured a variety of racing configurations, including a dirt oval, a road course, and a drag strip.

## Early Development
The complex held its inaugural races on May 16, 1971, utilizing a 0.5-mile (0.80 km) dirt oval sanctioned by the Southern Nevada Off Road Enthusiasts (SNORE). Following the approval of plans by the Clark County government in July 1971, the facility expanded its capabilities to include a drag strip and a formal oval. In August 1971, SNORE opened a 0.750-mile (1.207 km) buggy course at the site.

In December 1971, a competing proposal was submitted by Larry Horten for a 520-acre development named the Las Vegas Raceway Park, located near Nellis Air Force Base. The Clark County Board of County Commissioners rejected Horten's bid on December 21, 1971.

## Racing Operations
### Drag Strip
The drag strip hosted its first events on February 26, 1972, featuring a 0.250-mile (0.402 km) track for the opening day of the National Hot Rod Association (NHRA) Open Drag Championships. In September 1972, the West Charleston Lions Club of Las Vegas entered into a 15-year agreement to manage the drag strip's operations.

### Road Course and Ovals
In May 1972, the International Motorsports Association scheduled a series of races for the remainder of the year on the complex's 1.600-mile (2.575 km) road course. In 1975, the facility served as the starting point for the Mint 400 off-road race.

## Financial and Operational Challenges
Following 1972, the venue experienced a significant decline in major racing events, particularly in drag racing, attributed to financial instability. While businessmen Ted Wiens and Mike Sellers disputed the extent of these issues, the track primarily hosted small local events between 1972 and May 19, 1974.

By August 1974, the facility's financial condition prompted local drag racers to contribute profits in an attempt to revitalize the venue. In October 1974, the complex established the Speedrome Racing Association and implemented a schedule of bi-monthly drag races to generate consistent revenue. The NHRA returned to the facility in September 1975, marking the first major drag racing event at the complex in nearly three years.

## Logistics and Infrastructure
The facility's infrastructure faced technical difficulties during its early years of operation. By August 1972, reports indicated that the lighting system on the road course frequently malfunctioned during competitive events.
