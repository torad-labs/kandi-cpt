# Las Vegas Motor Speedway

The **Las Vegas Motor Speedway (LVMS)** is a major motorsports facility located in Las Vegas, Nevada. Owned by Speedway Motorsports, the venue hosts a variety of professional racing series, including NASCAR and the NHRA, as well as large-scale entertainment events.

## History and Development

The concept of a dedicated racing facility in Las Vegas evolved through several iterations. In the early 1970s, the Las Vegas Speedrome emerged as a center for drag racing and stock cars, though it faced numerous legal and financial struggles, including lease terminations and disputes over city approvals throughout the 1970s and 1980s.

The modern superspeedway project began in 1994. Following a period of development and construction, the Las Vegas Motor Speedway officially opened in September 1996. The facility initially attracted IndyCar events, with the first major race occurring shortly after its opening. In December 1998, the speedway was acquired by Hubie Smith.

## Stages and Layout

The LVMS complex features multiple racing configurations to accommodate different disciplines:

*   **Superspeedway**: The primary oval track used for NASCAR and IndyCar events. In 2006, a $25 million rebuilding project was undertaken to modify the banking of the turns to improve racing quality, though these changes were met with mixed reviews from some drivers.
*   **The Strip**: A dedicated drag racing facility. In 2017, the strip was expanded to a four-lane configuration to accommodate larger NHRA events.
*   **Short Track**: A smaller oval track that opened in April 2000 to support grassroots and regional racing.
*   **Road Course**: The facility includes a road course layout used for sports car racing and specialized events.

## Lineup of Events

The speedway serves as a multi-purpose venue for both athletic and commercial events:

### Motorsports
The venue is a regular stop for the NASCAR Cup Series, including the Pennzoil 400. It has historically hosted the IZOD IndyCar Series and the World of Outlaws. Additionally, the facility has hosted the Red Bull Air Race World Championship, though these events were occasionally impacted by high winds.

### Non-Racing Events
The speedway is used for diverse large-scale gatherings, including:
*   **Music Festivals**: The venue hosts the Electronic Dance Music festival **Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC)**.
*   **Specialized Events**: The facility has hosted the FoodieLand Night Market, golf long-drive competitions, and the Indy Autonomous Challenge during CES.
*   **Community Events**: The speedway organizes community-focused initiatives, such as blood drives and high school graduation events.

## Logistics and Infrastructure

The facility has undergone several modifications to optimize spectator experience and revenue:
*   **Capacity**: In 2017, seating capacity was reduced to approximately 80,000 spectators to make room for expanded VIP areas and RV parking.
*   **Media and Corporate**: The infield media center has been integrated with corporate sponsorships to enhance broadcasting capabilities.
*   **Hospitality**: A $100 million condominium project was planned as part of major facility upgrades in 2006 to increase luxury accommodations.

## Safety

The speedway has been the site of significant safety evaluations following high-profile accidents. The most notable occurred in October 2011 during an IndyCar race, which resulted in the death of driver Dan Wheldon. The accident led to a comprehensive report by IndyCar, describing a "perfect storm" of events, and resulted in the series' decision not to return to the Las Vegas Motor Speedway in 2012. Subsequent racing events at the venue have continued under revised safety protocols and track configurations.
