Friend: I'm looking into the history of EDC before I book for 2026. How huge did the Vegas event get back in the early days?

Vet: It exploded pretty quickly. Back in 2012, attendance jumped 30% to about 320,000 people. By 2014, they actually sold out all 345,000 tickets by June 18th, averaging around 134,000 fans per day.

Friend: Wait, did anything crazy happen during those early years? Like weather or safety stuff?

Vet: Yeah, 2012 was wild. On the second night, 30 MPH winds hit around 8 p.m., and they had to shut everything down by 1 a.m. They sent 90,000 people to the bleachers. Markus Schulz and Steve Aoki actually did impromptu sets on the Wide Awake art car before officials shut that down too because the winds were expected to last until 5 a.m.

Friend: That sounds like a nightmare. Did people get their money back?

Vet: Not exactly, but Insomniac let everyone with Saturday passes come back on Sunday to make up for the cancellation. Sadly, that year also had a tragedy where a 31-year-old guy from Florida was hit by a truck leaving the Motor Speedway on June 11.

Friend: Man, that's rough. I heard there were some issues with the 2015 show too?

Vet: Yeah, it was a bit of a mixed bag. The production was cool—the main stage had a blinking owl theme—but there was some bad press after a UC Irvine grad died on the second night due to MDMA-related dehydration and overheating.

Friend: I noticed they have a lot of other cities. Was it always just Vegas?

Vet: Definitely not. For a while, they had EDC New York at MetLife Stadium in New Jersey (which pulled 45,000 people a day) and EDC Orlando at Tinker Field. In 2012, the Orlando date actually clashed with the "Dancegiving" Dayglow event in Fort Lauderdale.

Friend: What about outside the US?

Vet: They went global fast. In 2013, they hit London for the first time. By 2014, they launched EDC Mexico on March 15–16. By 2015, they were in São Paulo, Brazil, for the first time in December, and they had a steady run at the National Bowl in Milton Keynes, UK, and Estadio Sixto Escobar in Puerto Rico.

Friend: Did they ever do any records or movies about it?

Vet: Actually, yeah! In 2014, they released a documentary called *Under the Electric Sky* that premiered at Sundance. Also, the 2014 Vegas setup officially set the record for the largest structural stage in North America.

Friend: If I want to see a specific stage, like kineticFIELD, is that a recent thing?

Vet: The specific camelCase branding like kineticFIELD evolved over time, but the scale started with those massive 2014 records. Just remember, if you're heading to the Motor Speedway, stay hydrated—the 2015 tragedy is a reminder that the desert heat is no joke.
