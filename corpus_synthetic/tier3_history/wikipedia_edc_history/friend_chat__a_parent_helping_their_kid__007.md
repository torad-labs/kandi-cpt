Friend: I'm helping my kid get ready for EDC 2026, but I honestly don't know much about it. Is it just a giant party, or is there more to it?

Vet: It’s definitely a party, but there's a huge community side to it. If you want to see the "soul" of the event, tell your kid to watch the documentary *Under the Electric Sky*. It premiered at Sundance back in 2014 and really captures the vibe of the festival-goers.

Friend: That's interesting. Does the event actually give back to anything, or is it all profit?

Vet: They actually do a lot through Insomniac Care. They donate a percentage of ticket sales from every flagship festival to national charities. Since 2011, they've raised over two million dollars.

Friend: Wow, two million? How do they actually raise that much?

Vet: A big part is through a platform called Surreal. They run sweepstakes where fans can win crazy stuff—like helicopter rides with popular DJs—and that money goes to charity.

Friend: Which organizations actually benefit from this?

Vet: It's a mix. They support groups like Rock the Vote, the Boys and Girls Club of Southern Nevada, and the Nevada Childhood Cancer Foundation. They also help the Injured Police Officers Fund, The Las Vegas Academy for Arts, and Families for Effective Autism Treatment.

Friend: I've heard the music is great, but is it actually "award-winning" or just popular?

Vet: Both. It's consistently nominated for Best Music Event at the International Dance Music Awards. They were nominated almost every year from 2010 through 2016, and again in 2019 and 2020.

Friend: Does it rank well against other global festivals?

Vet: Absolutely. DJ Magazine has them ranked incredibly high lately. They were 3rd in 2022, 4th in 2023, and they climbed to 2nd place in 2024.

Friend: Since you've been before, where should my kid head first to experience that "top-tier" energy?

Vet: Definitely start at kineticFIELD on Friday night. That's the heart of the experience. Then, make sure they hit circuitGROUNDS on Saturday for the big production.

Friend: Any last tips for a first-timer?

Vet: Just tell them to embrace the scale of it. Between the charity work and the massive production at stages like neonGARDENS, it's more of a cultural experience than just a concert.
