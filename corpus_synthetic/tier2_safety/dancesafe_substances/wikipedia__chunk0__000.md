# Ketamine and LSD

## Ketamine

### Overview
Ketamine is a dissociative anesthetic used in both human and veterinary medicine. It belongs to a class of drugs that separate perception from sensation, which includes phencyclidine (PCP), dextromethorphan (DXM), and nitrous oxide. In medical settings, ketamine is typically administered as a liquid; however, for non-medical use, it is commonly evaporated into a white powder for insufflation.

### Effects and Stages
The effects of ketamine are dose-dependent and typically last between 45 and 60 minutes, with a full return to baseline occurring within 1.5 to 2 hours.

*   **Low Dose:** Produces a mild trance-like or "floaty" sensation, comparable to the effects of alcohol or nitrous oxide.
*   **High Dose:** Results in hallucinogenic and dissociative effects. At these levels, users may experience an "out-of-body" experience, commonly referred to as a "k-hole." This state is characterized by a profound detachment from the physical body and environment.

### Dosage and Administration
Ketamine is most frequently administered via nasal "bumps" or small lines ranging from 30 to 60 mg, with onset occurring within 5 to 15 minutes. A dose of approximately 100 mg is typically sufficient to induce a full dissociative state. Intramuscular injection is an alternative method of administration that requires a significantly lower dose to achieve the same effects.

### Safety and Harm Reduction
#### Contraindications
While ketamine does not independently depress heart rate or respiration, combining it with other depressants—such as alcohol, benzodiazepines, or gamma-hydroxybutyrate (GHB)—increases the risk of blackouts, vertigo, emesis, erratic body temperature, and loss of consciousness.

#### Physical Risks
High doses of ketamine can lead to a loss of motor control and a reduction in pain signaling due to the disruption of communication between the brain and body. This creates a risk of injury from falls or aspiration of vomit if the user is not in a safe position. It is recommended that individuals remain seated or lying down when using high doses and avoid using the substance alone.

#### Long-term Health Effects
Chronic use of ketamine can result in ketamine cystitis, a condition involving damage to the bladder lining. Symptoms include pelvic pain, frequent urination, and incontinence; in severe cases, this may necessitate the surgical removal of the bladder.

#### Purity and Isomers
Ketamine exists in different isomers (such as r-ketamine and s-ketamine). Because distinguishing between these isomers requires advanced laboratory equipment, claims made by distributors regarding specific isomer formulations are generally considered unreliable.

## LSD

### Overview
LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide), commonly known as "acid," is a psychedelic substance first discovered in 1938.

### Effects and Duration
An LSD experience typically lasts between 8 and 12 hours. The experience is often characterized by "chapter switching," wherein the user perceives the trip as a series of distinct thematic or emotional segments. These transitions can be triggered abruptly by changes in environment, specific thoughts, or external events.
