* Strip testing involves diluting drugs in water.
* Test strips provide a positive or negative result for one drug class.
* Fentanyl test strips detect fentanyl and its analogs.
* Amphetamine strips react to MDMA and methamphetamine.
* Strip testing does not destroy the drug sample.
* Strip tests are extremely sensitive to the testing procedure.
* At-home tests cannot identify every drug in a sample.
* At-home tests cannot confirm multiple drugs in a mixed sample.
* At-home tests cannot detect inactive cuts like baking soda.
* At-home tests cannot determine drug potency or purity.
* Lab testing is the most accurate method of drug checking.
* Transparency Testing offers domestic and international mail-in lab services.
* DrugsData provides a browsable historical database of lab results.
* At-home test strips are not designed for organic plant matter or fungi.
* Most claims of fentanyl-laced cannabis are unverified.
* Head shop cannabis products may contain pesticides or heavy metals.
* Species identification is critical for mushrooms to avoid poisonous types.
* The Shroomery is a resource for mushroom identification.
* Combining alcohol with ketamine, GHB, or opioids causes respiratory depression.
* Combining MDMA with SSRIs increases the risk of serotonin syndrome.
* SSRIs can blunt the effects of MDMA.
* Combining MDMA with MAOIs creates a severe risk of serotonin syndrome.
* Combining cocaine and alcohol forms cocaethylene in the liver.
* Cocaethylene is more cardiotoxic than cocaine or alcohol alone.
* Combining cocaine and MDMA increases the risk of hyperthermia.
* Combining stimulants at EDC can lead to hypertension and heart strain.
* Las Vegas desert temperatures often stay above 80°F into the early morning.
* High ambient heat increases the risk of heatstroke when using stimulants.
* Substances that suppress thirst are more dangerous in the desert environment.
* Lack of sleep for over 48 hours while using stimulants increases psychosis risk.
