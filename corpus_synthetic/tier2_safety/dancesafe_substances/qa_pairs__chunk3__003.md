Q: What is strip testing, also known as immunoassay testing?
A: Strip testing involves diluting drugs in water and dipping a test strip to obtain a positive or negative result for one specific class of drug.

Q: What is the specific function of fentanyl test strips?
A: Fentanyl test strips detect fentanyl and its analogs.

Q: What substances will amphetamine test strips react with?
A: Amphetamine strips will react with anything in the amphetamine class, such as MDMA and meth.

Q: What are four things that at-home drug checking materials cannot do?
A: They cannot tell you exactly what drugs are in a sample, confirm the presence of multiple drugs in a mixed sample, detect inactive cuts like baking soda, or determine potency or purity.

Q: Which mail-in lab testing service is available domestically and internationally?
A: Transparency Testing.

Q: What is the current status of DrugsData (formerly EcstasyData) regarding submissions?
A: DrugsData is not currently accepting submissions, though its historical database remains browsable.

Q: Why is test strip and reagent technology not useful for mushrooms and cannabis?
A: This technology is designed for synthetic compounds, whereas organic material like plant matter and fungi is difficult or impossible to test with at-home tools.

Q: Which combinations of alcohol and other drugs cause respiratory depression?
A: Alcohol combined with ketamine, GHB, or opioids.

Q: What risk is associated with combining MDMA with SSRIs or MAOIs?
A: Serotonin syndrome.

Q: What substance is formed in the liver when cocaine is combined with alcohol?
A: Cocaethylene.

Q: Why is combining cocaine and MDMA particularly dangerous at EDC?
A: The combination of two stimulants, dancing, and desert heat—where ambient temperatures stay above 80°F into the early morning—leads to hyperthermia, hypertension, and heart strain.

Q: How does insufficient sleep affect the risk of psychosis when using stimulants over multiple days?
A: The risk of psychosis increases sharply past 48 hours of wakefulness.
