Friend: Yo, I’m starting to prep for EDC 2026. I’m planning on running some coke during the weekend, but I know the Vegas heat is brutal. Any tips?

Vet: Honestly, be careful. Cocaine hits your cardiovascular system way harder than other stimulants. Between the desert heat and dancing at kineticFIELD, you're looking at a massive spike in heart rate, blood pressure, and body temp. If you combine that with other uppers, you're risking arrhythmia or even a straight-up heart attack.

Friend: Damn, I didn't realize it was that risky. What about the comedown? I don't want to crash mid-festival.

Vet: That's the problem—it's short-acting, so the comedown is a bitch and usually leads to compulsive redosing. Just a heads up: if you stay awake for two or more days straight, you're entering the danger zone for a psychotic state. Sleep is non-negotiable.

Friend: Good call. I'll make sure to hit the hotel for a few hours. Anything else for the actual usage?

Vet: Yeah, don't wreck your nose. Use saline spray before, during, and after. Also, for the love of god, don't share straws. Sharing snorting devices is a fast track to Hepatitis C. Just use a rolled-up Post-it or a personal straw and toss it.

Friend: Smart. Now, about the gear—I'm bringing some MDMA for CIRCUIT GROUND. Do you think reagent testing is enough to know it's pure?

Vet: Not exactly. Reagents are "red flags, not green lights." They tell you if something is reacting as expected, but they can't give you a full lab analysis. Like back in the 2009-2017 shortage, PMMA/PMA didn't react with Marquis, so pills that were 90% PMMA still "passed" because the 10% MDMA overshadowed it.

Friend: Wait, so if my Marquis test turns black, I'm 100% good?

Vet: Not necessarily. Marquis turns black for both MDMA and MDA. You’d need to use Simon’s reagent to tell them apart—Simon’s turns blue for MDMA, but stays muddy gray or doesn't react for MDA.

Friend: That's a lot of chemistry for a rave. Can I just use a liquid test for fentanyl since that's the big worry now?

Vet: Absolute no. Do not trust any liquid chemical marketed as a fentanyl test. No reagent can actually test for fentanyl. If you're worried about that, you need actual strip testing.

Friend: Got it. So basically: hydrate, sleep, use personal straws, and remember that a "clean" reagent test doesn't mean it's 100% pure.

Vet: Exactly. Stay safe, and I'll see you at the neon garden.
