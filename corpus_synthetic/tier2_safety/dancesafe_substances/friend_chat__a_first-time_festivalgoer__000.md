Friend: I'm so hyped for EDC 2026, but I've never been to a rave. I'm a little nervous about the substance side of things. I've heard people talk about Ketamine—what actually is that?

Vet: Totally normal to be nervous! Basically, ketamine is a "dissociative anesthetic." It separates your perception from your sensation. In a medical setting, it's a liquid, but at festivals, you'll mostly see it as a white powder that people snort.

Friend: What does it actually feel like? Is it like being drunk?

Vet: At low doses, yeah, it's a bit like alcohol or nitrous—kind of a "floaty," trance-like vibe. That usually lasts 45 to 60 minutes, and you're back to baseline in about two hours. But if you take too much, you can hit a "k-hole," which is a full-on out-of-body experience.

Friend: A "k-hole" sounds intense. How does that even happen?

Vet: It's all about the dose. Most people just do "bumps" or small lines of 30-60mg, and it hits in 5 to 15 minutes. But if you hit around 100mg, you're likely heading into that dissociative state. Honestly, don't do that at EDC. Imagine being stuck in a k-hole while standing in the middle of kineticFIELD during a headliner set—you lose motor control and can't move. It's dangerous in a crowd.

Friend: Yikes. Is it safe to mix with other stuff if I'm just doing a little?

Vet: Be really careful. Ketamine itself doesn't slow your breathing, but if you mix it with depressants—like alcohol, GHB, or benzos—it can get ugly. We're talking blackouts, vomiting, and losing consciousness. Also, never do high doses alone; people have actually died from choking on vomit or falling face-first into pillows.

Friend: I didn't realize it could be that risky. Are there any long-term things I should know about?

Vet: If someone uses it long-term, they can get "ketamine cystitis," which is basically damage to the bladder lining. It causes pelvic pain and frequent urination, and in extreme cases, people have had to get their bladders removed. Just a heads-up to keep it occasional.

Friend: Someone told me they have a "special" version of it, like r-ketamine. Is that actually better?

Vet: Total marketing. Unless your dealer has a professional laboratory, there is no way to test if it's one isomer or another. Don't pay extra for "special" versions; it's almost always a scam.

Friend: Got it. What about LSD? I've heard that's common too.

Vet: Acid is a classic. Just be prepared: an LSD trip lasts a long way—usually 8 to 12 hours. It's not one long flat line; it's more like "chapters." You'll feel a theme or emotion, and then suddenly a thought or a change in scenery—like moving from the neon lights of the circusGROUNDS to a quieter area—can trigger a "chapter switch" and change the whole vibe of the trip.
