### Drug Testing Methodologies

Strip testing, or immunoassay testing, is a process where a substance is diluted in water and applied to a test strip to detect the presence of a specific class of drug. Fentanyl strips identify fentanyl and its analogs, while amphetamine strips react to any compound within the amphetamine class, including MDMA and methamphetamine. This method is non-destructive to the sample but requires strict adherence to procedural instructions due to high sensitivity.

At-home testing is limited in scope. It cannot provide an exact chemical composition, confirm the presence of multiple drugs in a mixed sample, detect inactive cutting agents (such as baking soda), or determine the potency and purity of a substance. Laboratory testing is the most accurate method for analysis. Domestic and international mail-in services, such as Transparency Testing, provide this capability. DrugsData (formerly EcstasyData) maintains a browsable historical database of lab results.

Organic materials, such as cannabis and fungi, are generally incompatible with at-home test strips and reagents, which are designed for synthetic compounds. Regarding cannabis, lab-confirmed fentanyl contamination is rare, though head-shop products may contain heavy metals or pesticides. For mushrooms, chemical testing is ineffective; risk mitigation relies on species identification via communities such as The Shroomery to avoid poisonous varieties.

### Contraindications and Substance Interactions

Certain drug combinations increase physiological risk, particularly in high-activity environments. Combining alcohol with ketamine, GHB, or opioids can lead to respiratory depression. MDMA combined with Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs) or Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs) increases the risk of serotonin syndrome. 

The combination of cocaine and alcohol produces cocaethylene in the liver, which possesses a longer half-life and higher cardiotoxicity than either substance alone, increasing the risk of myocardial infarction. Combining cocaine and MDMA increases the risk of hypertension, heart strain, and hyperthermia. Additionally, stimulant use coupled with sleep deprivation exceeding 48 hours correlates with a sharp increase in psychosis risk.

### Environmental Factors at Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC)

The Las Vegas desert environment acts as a risk multiplier. Ambient temperatures frequently remain above 80°F into the early morning hours. Substances that increase core body temperature (stimulants, MDMA, alcohol) or suppress the thirst response accelerate the risk of dehydration and heatstroke when combined with physical exertion and desert heat.
