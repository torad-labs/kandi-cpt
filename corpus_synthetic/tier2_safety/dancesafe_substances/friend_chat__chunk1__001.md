Friend: Yo! I'm trying to plan my 2026 EDC run and I'm curious about the vibe. What's the deal with people using LSD there? Do you actually see things that aren't there?

Vet: Honestly, "true hallucinations" are super rare, even though movies make it look like a fever dream. For most, it's more about color and shape distortions, or just the way a person or space looks shifting around.

Friend: That sounds wild. Is it just about the visuals then?

Vet: Not at all. The biggest thing is actually how your perception of yourself and the environment changes. You might be standing at kineticFIELD and suddenly the whole place feels totally alien. It can make following conversations or reading people's emotions really tough, but it's usually paired with a huge emotional sensitivity that can feel really transformative—or just uncomfortable.

Friend: Got it. If I were to do it, how is it actually taken? 

Vet: Most common is "blotter," which are just tiny pieces of paper. You can also find it in liquid, gel tabs, or candy, but it's almost always taken orally.

Friend: How much is a "normal" dose?

Vet: Usually between 100 and 200 mcg. It's such a tiny amount it's basically invisible. A single square of blotter is typically 80 to 150 mcg, but that varies.

Friend: Can you tell for sure how much is on a tab before you take it?

Vet: Nope. Unless you have an overseas lab, you're just guessing. Dealers approximate, but unless they laid the blotter themselves, they don't actually know. My advice: always start with a small dose when you've got a new batch.

Friend: Any red flags I should look out for?

Vet: Definitely. Acid shouldn't have a strong taste. If it tastes metallic or bitter, stop immediately—it might be a different drug entirely.

Friend: What if I take it and don't feel anything right away? Do I just take more?

Vet: Big no. You have to wait at least three hours before taking more. If you don't, you risk everything hitting you at once, and that's how you end up in a bad spot.

Friend: Speaking of bad spots, how do you make sure the trip stays positive?

Vet: It's all about "set" and "setting." Your mental state—thoughts, mood, and expectations—plus your physical and social environment. Being in a good headspace with trusted friends in a supportive area is the best way to avoid a panic attack or extreme anxiety.

Friend: Are there any long-term risks I should know about?

Vet: Yeah, people with a family history of mood or psychotic disorders are at a higher risk for psychological upset. Also, there's something called HPPD—Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder—which is a long-lasting disorder.

Friend: That's a lot to think about. What about cocaine? I've heard people use that to stay up for the big sets.

Vet: It's a bitter, numbing powder. It gives you a rush of confidence, alertness, and euphoria. It's a stimulant, similar to amphetamines, but the effects are way shorter.

Friend: How short are we talking?

Vet: It hits within minutes and peaks around 15 to 30 minutes. The whole thing usually only lasts 30 to 60 minutes.

Friend: So how much do people usually do?

Vet: If you're snorting it in bumps or lines, a typical dose is 30 to 70 mg. If someone is smoking crack, it's usually 15 to 50 mg.

Friend: Does that mean you have to keep taking it throughout the night?

Vet: Exactly. Because it wears off so fast, people build a tolerance quickly and usually need higher doses over a single session or over time.
