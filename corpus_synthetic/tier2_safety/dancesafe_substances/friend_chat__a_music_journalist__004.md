Friend: I'm finally hitting EDC 2026! I'm planning my hydration strategy, but is just chugging water enough to stay safe in the desert?

Vet: Definitely not. Actually, just drinking plain water is a risk—you can hit hyponatremia, which is basically water poisoning. You need electrolytes to balance it out.

Friend: Oh, I didn't know that. How do I actually manage that while I'm running between sets?

Vet: Keep it steady. Aim for about 480 mL (16 oz) per hour mixed with electrolytes. Whatever you do, don't exceed 800 mL (27 oz) per hour.

Friend: Where do I even get electrolytes once I'm inside?

Vet: Insomniac has you covered. They do Electrolit hydration stick distributions right at the water stations, so just grab those as you rotate through the grounds.

Friend: Good to know. What if I'm at kineticFIELD or omegaLAND and I notice a friend is starting to overheat or look way too over-stimulated?

Vet: First thing: get them out of the sun and into the shade. Get them seated immediately and start getting water and electrolytes into them.

Friend: At what point does "feeling the heat" become a medical emergency?

Vet: You have to watch for escalation signs. If you see vomiting, seizures, bluish skin, or an abnormal heart rate or breathing, it's serious. Same goes for delusions, hallucinations, or if they become unresponsive.

Friend: If I have to rush someone to medical, am I going to get flagged or get in trouble for whatever they took?

Vet: Not at all. If you see those signs, hit the nearest first-aid tent or flag down Ground Control immediately. You will not get in trouble for seeking medical help; their only priority is getting the person stable.
