# Ecstasy

**Ecstasy** is a street term used to describe any pill marketed as containing 3,4-Methylenedioxymethamphetamine (MDMA). Due to the unregulated nature of its production, these pressed pills are characterized by significant variability in chemical composition.

### Composition and Adulterants
Ecstasy pills frequently contain substances other than MDMA. Common adulterants and substitutes include caffeine, ephedrine, amphetamines, 3,4-Methylenedioxyamphetamine (MDA), 3,4-Methylenedioxymethamphetamine (MDE), dextromethorphan (DXM), and, less frequently, 2,5-Dimethoxy-4-bromophenethylamine (DOB). In some instances, pills may contain no MDMA or other psychoactive compounds.

Between 2009 and 2017, a period referred to as the "great MDMA shortage," a high prevalence of pills containing approximately 90% para-Methoxyamphetamine (PMMA) and 10% MDMA was observed. Because PMMA does not react with the Marquis reagent, the presence of small amounts of MDMA can cause such tests to appear positive despite the predominance of PMMA.

### Testing and Logistics
To mitigate the risks associated with adulteration, organizations such as DanceSafe provide drug-checking resources, including reagent kits and fentanyl test strips. In pharmacological testing, reagent tests are categorized as "red flags" rather than "green lights," meaning they can identify the presence of certain substances but cannot guarantee the purity or safety of a sample.

### Safety and Contraindications

#### Pharmaceutical Interactions
The use of MDMA is contraindicated for individuals taking the following medications:
*   **Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs):** Including Nardil, Parnate, Marplan, Eldepryl, and Aurorex/Manerix, as well as substances containing MAOIs such as ayahuasca.
*   **Protease Inhibitors:** Specifically Ritonavir, which may cause life-threatening interactions.

Other substances that may increase physiological strain or risk include:
*   **Strong Stimulants:** Cocaine and methamphetamine, which may increase the risk of hyperthermia and cardiovascular strain.
*   **Alcohol:** High doses may compound dehydration.
*   **Serotonergics:** 5-HTP, tramadol, and Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs). While SSRIs typically inhibit the effects of MDMA, attempting to overcome this blunting by increasing the MDMA dose can increase toxicity.

#### High-Risk Profiles
Certain medical conditions and biological factors increase the risk of adverse reactions:
*   **Cardiovascular and Systemic Health:** History of heart ailments, hypertension, aneurysms, stroke, glaucoma, or hepatic and renal disorders.
*   **Metabolic Conditions:** Hypoglycemia.
*   **Psychiatric History:** Personal or family history of mood or psychotic disorders.
*   **Genetic Metabolism:** Individuals with low levels of the liver enzyme CYP2D6 (affecting 1–10% of the population) are classified as "slow metabolizers." This group may exhibit increased sensitivity to MDMA and related substances like Prozac and DXM.

### Post-Use Effects
The period following MDMA use is often characterized by a decline in mood and physical exhaustion.

*   **The Crash:** A sudden decline in mood occurring as the primary effects wear off. This may lead some users to redose, which can exacerbate the subsequent recovery period.
*   **The Hangover:** A state of extreme fatigue the day following use, typically requiring a full recovery day.
*   **Post-Use Mood Alterations:** In the 5–7 days following consumption, some users report irritability, restlessness, antisocial behavior, or heightened emotional sensitivity.
