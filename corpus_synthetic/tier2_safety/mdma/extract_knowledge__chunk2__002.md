**Definition and Composition of "Ecstasy"**
In a street context, "ecstasy" refers to any pill marketed as MDMA. These substances are frequently adulterated and may contain caffeine, ephedrine, amphetamines, MDA, MDE, DXM, or DOB. In some instances, these pills contain no MDMA or other psychoactive compounds.

**Testing and Limitations**
Organizations such as DanceSafe provide reagent kits and fentanyl test strips for substance verification. However, reagent tests serve as "red flags" rather than guarantees of purity. During the "great MDMA shortage" (2009–2017), pills composed of 90% PMMA and 10% MDMA often passed Marquis reagent tests because the small amount of MDMA masked the non-reaction of the PMMA.

**Contraindications and Interactions**
MDMA is contraindicated for individuals taking Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs), including Nardil, Parnate, Marplan, Eldepryl, Aurorex, Manerix, or ayahuasca. Combining MDMA with the protease inhibitor Ritonavir is potentially life-threatening. 

Additional risky combinations include:
*   **Strong Stimulants:** Cocaine and methamphetamine, which increase heart strain and overheating.
*   **Alcohol:** High doses exacerbate dehydration.
*   **Serotonergics:** 5-HTP, tramadol, and Selective Serotonin Reuptake Inhibitors (SSRIs). SSRIs typically block the mechanism of MDMA, rendering it ineffective; attempting to overcome this blunting by increasing MDMA dosage can be dangerous.

**High-Risk Profiles**
Increased risk is associated with histories of heart ailments, high blood pressure, aneurysm, stroke, glaucoma, hepatic or renal disorders, and hypoglycemia. Individuals with personal or family histories of mood or psychotic disorders are also at higher risk. Furthermore, "slow metabolizers"—approximately 1% to 10% of the population based on race and genetics—possess low levels of the liver enzyme P450 2D6, increasing sensitivity to MDMA.

**Post-Use Effects**
The "crash" is a mood decline occurring as the drug's peak effects subside. Redosing to avoid this crash can compound negative effects. A physical hangover typically occurs the day after use, necessitating a recovery period. Additionally, a "week-after blues" period may occur 5–7 days post-ingestion, characterized by irritability, restlessness, and heightened emotional sensitivity.
