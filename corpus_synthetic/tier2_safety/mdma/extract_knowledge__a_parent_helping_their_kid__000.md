**MDMA Overview**
3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methamphetamine (MDMA) is a phenethylamine classified as a psychedelic amphetamine, entactogen, and euphoric stimulant. In common usage, the term "ecstasy" typically refers to pressed tablets, while "molly" refers to crystals or capsules. The experience of being under the influence of the substance is referred to as "rolling."

**History and Legal Status**
First synthesized in the 1890s and patented by Merck pharmaceuticals in 1912, MDMA was introduced to psychotherapy by Dr. Alexander Shulgin. It gained scholarly attention in the mid-1970s and street availability in the late 1970s and early 1980s. In 1985, the United States classified MDMA as a Schedule I controlled substance. It is currently being evaluated by the FDA for the treatment of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD).

**Psychological and Physical Effects**
The primary psychological effects of MDMA include euphoria, emotional openness, increased empathy, and a reduction of inhibitions and cynical thoughts. 

Physiological responses include increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, and an increase in core body temperature. Common physical side effects include:
*   **Bruxism:** Teeth grinding.
*   **Trismus:** Jaw locking.
*   **Nystagmus:** Involuntary eye wiggles.
*   **Other:** Dehydration and difficulty urinating.

**Dosage Response**
The substance produces both a physical sensation of lightness and sensitized touch and a mental state of curiosity and warmth. Insufficient dosing may result in stimulation without the associated emotional "breakthrough," while excessive dosing may lead to a state where the user is unable to remain standing or active.
