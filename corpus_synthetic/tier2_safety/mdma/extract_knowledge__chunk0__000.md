**MDMA Overview**
3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methamphetamine (MDMA) is a phenethylamine classified as an entactogen, euphoric empathogen, and euphoric stimulant. Commonly referred to by the slang terms "molly" (typically capsules or crystals) and "ecstasy" (typically pressed tablets), the substance is known for producing a state termed "rolling."

**History and Legal Status**
MDMA was first synthesized in the 1890s and patented by Merck pharmaceuticals in December 1912. Its psychoactive properties were documented in scholarly journals in the mid-1970s, and it was later utilized as a psychotherapeutic tool, a practice introduced by Dr. Alexander Shulgin. In 1985, the United States designated MDMA as a Schedule I controlled substance. As of 2026, the FDA is evaluating the drug for the treatment of Post-Traumatic Stress Disorder (PTSD).

**Psychological and Physical Effects**
The substance induces mental states characterized by euphoria, emotional openness, empathy, decreased inhibitions, and a reduction in critical or cynical thoughts. Physical manifestations include increased core body temperature, elevated blood pressure, and increased heart rate. Common involuntary physical reactions include bruxism (teeth grinding), trismus (jaw locking), nystagmus (involuntary eye movement), and difficulty urinating.

**Dosage Responses**
The experience is divided into physical and mental components. Insufficient dosing may result in stimulation without the characteristic "breakthrough" into an empathetic headspace. Conversely, excessive dosing may result in a "floored" state, characterized by an inability to stand or move for several hours. While MDMA enhances tactile sensations, it rarely produces prominent visual hallucinations.
