Friend: I'm finally going to EDC 2026! I'm planning on rolling for the first time—how long does it actually take to hit?

Vet: It usually takes about 20-60 minutes, but if you ate a huge meal right before, it could take up to 2 hours. Just a heads up: the onset can feel like a "launch" toward the peak, which is a bit sharp and can be unnerving for a few minutes until you're fully there.

Friend: Got it. Once it hits, how long am I actually "on top of the world"?

Vet: The peak usually lasts about 90-120 minutes, and the primary effects generally run for 3-5 hours. But keep in mind there's a residual period for another 2-6 hours after that where you'll feel a shift and probably find it really hard to sleep.

Friend: I'm worried about dosing. How much should I actually take so I don't overdo it?

Vet: A standard oral dose is 80-125mg. Most quality pressed pills are in the 80-120mg range. There is a "sweet spot" for everyone; once you hit it, taking more doesn't actually make the high better or longer—it just adds nasty side effects.

Friend: What if I feel it wearing off while I'm at kineticFIELD? Can I take more?

Vet: You can do a single redose of about one-third to one-half of your original dose around 90 minutes in. That'll extend the peak by a few hours. Don't go over that, or you're just inviting a brutal hangover.

Friend: I've seen people do this every few weeks. Is that okay?

Vet: Honestly, no. You need to space your rolls. The bare minimum is a few weeks for serotonin to replenish, but the best practice is 3-6 months. If you do it too often, you build a tolerance and risk permanently losing the "magic," plus the post-roll depression gets way worse.

Friend: I'm terrified of the Vegas heat in May. How do I not overheat?

Vet: It's a real risk, especially if you're dancing for 6 hours straight at circuitGROUNDS. The "death scenario" is combining a high dose of MDMA with cocaine or other amps in that Nevada heat. Avoid mixing stimulants and take breaks from dancing.

Friend: What about water? I've heard you can actually drink too much.

Vet: Exactly. Too little leads to hypernatremia, but too much water can cause hyponatremia (water poisoning). Aim for 1-3 cups per hour, or up to 4 if you're going hard. The key is electrolytes—don't just drink plain water. Grab those Electrolit sticks at the free water stations; they're literally designed for this.

Friend: Are there any "red alert" signs I should look for while I'm out there?

Vet: Yes. If you get a severe, splitting headache, that can be a sign of dangerously high blood pressure. Do not "tough it out"—go straight to the medical tent immediately.
