### MDMA and "Ecstasy" Composition
In a street context, "ecstasy" is defined as any pill marketed as MDMA. These pressed pills are frequently unreliable and may contain adulterants such as caffeine, ephedrine, amphetamines, MDA, MDE, DXM, or DOB; in some instances, they contain no psychoactive ingredients. 

To identify substances, organizations such as DanceSafe provide reagent kits and fentanyl test strips. However, reagent tests serve as "red flags" rather than "green lights." For example, during the 2009-2017 MDMA shortage, pills containing 90% PMMA and 10% MDMA passed Marquis reagent tests because the small amount of MDMA masked the non-reactive PMMA.

### Contraindications and Dangerous Combinations
MDMA should not be used by individuals taking Monoamine Oxidase Inhibitors (MAOIs)—including antidepressants Nardil, Parnate, Marplan, Eldepryl, and Aurorex/Manerix, as well as ayahuasca—or the protease inhibitor Ritonavir, as the latter can be life-threatening.

Combinations to avoid include:
*   **Strong Stimulants:** Cocaine and methamphetamine increase the risk of overheating and cardiac strain.
*   **Alcohol:** High doses compound dehydration.
*   **Serotonergics:** 5-HTP (administered before or during use), tramadol, and certain SSRIs. While SSRIs typically block the effects of MDMA, attempting to overcome this blunting by increasing the MDMA dose can be dangerous.

### High-Risk Profiles
Individuals with the following medical histories are at significantly higher risk:
*   **Cardiovascular/Neurological:** Heart ailments, high blood pressure, history of aneurysm or stroke, and glaucoma.
*   **Organ Function:** Hepatic (liver) or renal (kidney) disorders.
*   **Metabolic/Psychiatric:** Hypoglycemia and personal or family histories of mood or psychotic disorders.
*   **Genetic Metabolism:** "Slow metabolizers" (1-10% of the population) with low levels of liver enzyme P450 2D6 may be more sensitive to MDMA and require lower doses.

### Post-Use Effects
The period following MDMA use is categorized into three stages:
1.  **The Crash:** A dramatic worsening of mood as the peak effects diminish. Redosing to prevent this crash can compound negative effects.
2.  **The Hangover:** A state of extreme exhaustion the day after use. Recovery typically requires one full day of minimal activity.
3.  **Week-After Blues:** Irritability, restlessness, and heightened emotional sensitivity that can persist for 5-7 days following the experience.
