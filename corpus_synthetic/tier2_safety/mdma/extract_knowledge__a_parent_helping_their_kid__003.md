**MDMA: Physiological and Psychological Effects**

MDMA is a neurotoxic substance with varying psychological after-effects. Many users experience post-use depression, typically beginning two days after ingestion and lasting up to five days, though some report symptoms for several weeks. Conversely, some users experience an "afterglow," characterized by improved mood for approximately one week. 

**Mitigation and Tolerance**

The primary method for mitigating negative effects is spacing the time between uses. The supplement 5-HTP may reduce post-use depression if administered at least eight hours after the final dose; taking 5-HTP before or during use may induce serotonin syndrome. 

Tolerance develops rapidly following use. Using MDMA on consecutive days typically results in a diminished experience on the second day. While the effect lessens if uses are spaced seven or more days apart, some users experience reduced effects for two to three weeks. Long-term, frequent use can lead to a permanent or multi-year loss of the drug's empathic qualities, a phenomenon termed "losing the magic."

**Long-Term Health Risks**

MDMA is recognized as a neurotoxin. Research indicates that heavy or frequent use is associated with increased depression, anxiety, and reductions in memory. While MDMA does not cause physical dependence, it has the potential for psychological addiction, leading some users to engage in compulsive weekly use.

**Medical Emergency Criteria**

According to Insomniac’s health and wellness guidelines, immediate medical intervention is required if a person exhibits any of the following:
*   **Physical Distress:** Vomiting, seizures, pale skin, or bluish tinting of the lips and fingertips.
*   **Vital Sign Abnormalities:** Irregular heart rate, abnormal breathing patterns, or extreme body temperature (hyperthermia or hypothermia).
*   **Neurological/Psychological Symptoms:** Severe headaches, delusions, hallucinations, paranoia, confusion, stupor, or coma.
*   **Ocular Signs:** Pupils that fail to react to changes in light.

At Electric Daisy Carnival, individuals exhibiting these signs should be taken to the nearest first-aid tent or reported to Ground Control (identified by purple shirts) or Insomniac staff. Festival policy explicitly states that users will not be penalized for seeking medical assistance.
