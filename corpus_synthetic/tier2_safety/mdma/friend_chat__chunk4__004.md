Friend: I'm finally going to EDC 2026! But honestly, I'm a bit nervous about keeping my group safe. What do I do if one of my friends starts overheating or getting too swept up in the music?

Vet: First off, welcome to the madness! If someone is just hot and sweaty but still coherent, get them out of the sun into some shade immediately. Give them electrolytes and water, and make them sit down. Don't let them just "walk it off"—that actually makes heat issues way worse.

Friend: What if they aren't physically sick, but they start having a panic attack or get super anxious?

Vet: If they're panicked but still oriented, find a quieter, cooler space. Just reassure them. The biggest mistake people make is trying to use logic to "talk them down" or arguing with them. Just be a calm presence.

Friend: And if they're just totally exhausted or wobbly on their feet?

Vet: Get them seated and get some water and electrolytes in them. If they can keep it down, give them some actual food. Also, a pro tip: do NOT give them strong coffee to wake them up. It doesn't help and can just make the jitters worse.

Friend: I'm worried about "peak" moments. What if two of my friends start fighting while they're both high?

Vet: Separate them immediately. Give them water and just listen to them, but the golden rule is: no big life decisions or relationship talks while on substances.

Friend: Let's say it's bad—someone is way too impaired to stand. Should I just lay them down on the grass?

Vet: Absolutely NOT. Never lay an impaired person flat on their back; if they vomit, they can choke, which is a major cause of death. Keep them in an upright seated position. If they absolutely must lie down, use the recovery position: on their side, top knee bent, and head tilted to keep the airway clear.

Friend: What about a cold shower to wake them up?

Vet: Huge no. Cold showers can cause "cold shock," which is dangerous for someone whose heart rate is already elevated from the music and stimulants.

Friend: If things get really serious, do I just call 911?

Vet: It depends on where you are. If you're at Camp EDC or off-grounds, yeah, call 911 or walk them to the 24-hour medical post. But if you're inside the festival—like near kineticFIELD or circuitGROUNDS—forget 911. Go to Ground Control or a first-aid tent.

Friend: Why not just call 911 inside the grounds?

Vet: Because Ground Control and the tents are usually 60 seconds away, they're fully equipped for this specific stuff, and they don't get the cops involved. It's way faster and less stressful.
