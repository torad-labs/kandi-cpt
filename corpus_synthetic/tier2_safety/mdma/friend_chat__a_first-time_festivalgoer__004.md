# Strategy: friend_chat
# Audience: a first-time festivalgoer
# Chunk: 4

Friend: I'm so hyped for EDC 2026, but honestly, I'm a little nervous. What happens if one of my friends starts feeling sick or gets too overwhelmed by the crowds?

Vet: Totally normal to be nervous! First thing: if someone is just hot and sweaty but still talking sense, get them into the shade immediately. Give them some electrolyte water and make them sit down. Don't let them just "walk it off," because that actually makes heat exhaustion worse.

Friend: What if they aren't physically sick, but just start having a full-blown panic attack?

Vet: If they're anxious but still know where they are, move them to a quieter, cooler space. Just reassure them. Pro tip: don't try to use logic to "talk them down" or argue with them—it doesn't work when you're in that headspace. Just be a calm presence.

Friend: And if they've just had way too much and are acting wobbly or exhausted?

Vet: Get them seated and get some water and electrolytes in them. If they can keep it down, give them some light food. Also, if you and a friend get into a fight while you're both peaking, just separate for a bit. Listen to each other, but whatever you do, don't make any big life decisions while you're under the influence!

Friend: I've heard people say to give someone strong coffee or a cold shower to "wake them up" if they're too far gone. Does that actually work?

Vet: Absolutely not. Coffee doesn't help, and a cold shower is actually dangerous because it can cause cold shock if their heart rate is already elevated. Also, never let them just "sleep it off" without someone watching them—if their airway gets blocked, it's a nightmare. And for the love of god, don't let other impaired friends be the ones making the medical decisions.

Friend: Okay, so if they literally can't stand up, should I just lay them down on the ground?

Vet: No! This is the most important part: keep them in an UPRIGHT seated position. Never lay someone flat on their back if they're impaired, because if they vomit, they can choke. If they absolutely have to lie down, put them in the recovery position—on their side, top knee bent, head tilted to keep the airway clear.

Friend: If things get really bad, do I just call 911 right there in the middle of kineticFIELD?

Vet: If you're at the festival proper—like at kineticFIELD or circuitGROUNDS—don't call 911 first. Look for Ground Control or the nearest first-aid tent. They are everywhere, usually only 60 seconds away, and they're fully equipped. Plus, they don't involve the cops.

Friend: What if we're back at the hotel or at Camp EDC and someone crashes?

Vet: If you're off-grounds or at Camp EDC, that's when you call 911 or walk them straight to the Camp EDC medical post. They have 24-hour staffing, so they can handle anything that comes up.
