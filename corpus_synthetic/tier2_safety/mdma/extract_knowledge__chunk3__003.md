### MDMA Pharmacological Effects and Recovery

MDMA usage is frequently followed by a period of post-experience depression, typically commencing on the second day and lasting up to five days, though some users report symptoms persisting for several weeks. Conversely, some users experience an "afterglow," characterized by an improved mood for approximately one week.

To mitigate negative post-experience effects, 5-HTP may be used, provided a minimum of eight hours has elapsed since the final dose of MDMA. Administration of 5-HTP prior to or during MDMA use is contraindicated due to the risk of inducing mild to moderate serotonin syndrome. While certain pre-experience supplements may offer protective effects, the most effective method for mitigating negative outcomes is the implementation of significant time intervals between uses.

### Tolerance and Neurotoxicity

MDMA induces a short-term tolerance. Use on consecutive days typically results in a diminished experience on the second day; effects may remain attenuated for 7 to 21 days following a single use. Long-term, frequent use is associated with a decrease in the drug's empathic qualities, a phenomenon described as "losing the magic," which can occur after as few as 10 experiences or after hundreds, depending on the frequency and dosage.

MDMA is classified as a neurotoxin. While the magnitude of its impact is debated, studies indicate that heavy and frequent users may experience impaired memory and increased levels of anxiety and depression. While not physically dependence-forming, MDMA possesses the potential for psychological addiction, leading to compulsive weekly use.

### Medical Emergency Indicators and Protocol

Immediate medical intervention is required if a user exhibits any of the following clinical signs:
*   **Physiological Distress:** Vomiting, seizures, pale skin, or cyanosis (bluish tinge to lips/fingertips).
*   **Vital Sign Instability:** Abnormal heart rate, irregular breathing, or abnormal body temperature (hyperthermia or hypothermia).
*   **Neurological/Psychological Impairment:** Severe headache, delusions, hallucinations, paranoia, non-reactive pupils, confusion, stupor, or coma.

Per Insomniac festival policy, individuals seeking medical assistance will not face disciplinary action. Emergency protocol dictates that affected individuals be transported to the nearest first-aid tent or reported to Ground Control (identified by purple shirts) or other Insomniac staff members.
