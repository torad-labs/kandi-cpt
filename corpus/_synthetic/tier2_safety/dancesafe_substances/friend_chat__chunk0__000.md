Friend: I'm finally going to EDC 2026! I'm stoked, but I'm a bit nervous about the substance side of things. I've heard people talk about Ketamine—what's the deal with that?

Vet: It's a dissociative anesthetic, basically. It separates your perception from your sensation. At low doses, you just feel "floaty" or in a mild trance, kind of like alcohol or nitrous. Usually, that lasts 45 to 60 minutes, and you're back to baseline in about two hours.

Friend: I've heard the term "k-hole" before. Is that something I should aim for while I'm at the kineticFIELD?

Vet: Absolutely not. A k-hole is a full dissociative state—basically an out-of-body experience. While it can be interesting, you do NOT want to do that in a public setting like EDC. You lose motor control and can't move, which is dangerous in a crowd. If you're taking enough to hit that state (usually around 100mg), you need to be seated or lying down.

Friend: How do people actually take it? And how much is "too much"?

Vet: Most people snort "bumps" or small lines of 30 to 60mg, and it hits in 5 to 15 minutes. Just be careful; high doses alone are risky because you can choke on vomit or fall and get hurt. Also, don't believe any dealer claiming they have "special r-ketamine" or "s-ketamine"—unless they have a high-end lab, it's just marketing.

Friend: Is it safe to mix with other things? Like, if I'm grabbing a drink at the bars?

Vet: Be really careful there. Ketamine itself doesn't slow your breathing, but if you mix it with depressants—alcohol, benzos, or GHB—you're asking for trouble. It can lead to blackouts, the spins, vomiting, or even loss of consciousness.

Vet: Oh, and just a heads-up for the long term: don't overdo it. Chronic use can cause ketamine cystitis, which destroys your bladder lining. It causes pelvic pain and frequent urination, and in the worst cases, people have to get their bladders removed.

Friend: Yikes. Okay, noted. What about LSD? I'm thinking about doing that for the fireworks at the circuitGROUNDS.

Vet: LSD is a classic. Just be prepared for the commitment—a trip lasts 8 to 12 hours. It's not one long flat line, either; it usually feels like "chapters" with different themes or emotions. 

Friend: Chapters? How does that work?

Vet: You'll be in one headspace, and then suddenly you'll "switch" chapters. It can happen because of a random thought, a change in the music, or even just moving to a different stage. It's a wild ride, but definitely plan your day around that time commitment!
