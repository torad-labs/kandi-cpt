**LSD (Lysergic Acid Diethylamide)**

LSD is a psychedelic substance primarily administered orally via liquid, gel tabs, candy, or "blotter" (small pieces of absorbent paper). Due to its high potency, a typical dosage ranges from 100 to 200 micrograms (mcg), while individual blotter squares or liquid drops generally contain between 80 and 150 mcg. Accurate dosage quantification requires laboratory analysis.

The substance alters perception of the self and environment; familiar spaces may appear alien, and the ability to process conversations or emotional cues may be impaired. Visual effects range from color and shape distortions to significant alterations in appearance, though "true hallucinations" (seeing objects not present) are rare. These effects are often accompanied by heightened emotional sensitivity.

Psychological risks include extreme anxiety, panic, and the potential for Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD). Individuals with personal or family histories of psychotic or mood disorders face an increased risk of psychological distress. The quality of the experience is heavily influenced by "set" (the user's internal mental state) and "setting" (the physical and social environment). Pure LSD is tasteless; metallic or bitter flavors indicate the presence of other substances.

**Cocaine**

Cocaine is a stimulant derived from the leaves of the coca plant, characterized as a bitter, numbing powder. It induces euphoria, alertness, confidence, and increased talkativeness. Its subjective effects are similar to amphetamines but possess a shorter duration of action.

The drug is most commonly insufflated (snorted) in small lines or "bumps," with a typical dose of 30 to 70 mg. Alternatively, it may be smoked as crack cocaine in doses of 15 to 50 mg. Effects manifest within minutes, peak between 15 and 30 minutes, and last for a total duration of 30 to 60 minutes. Due to this short window of activity, users frequently develop a tolerance, necessitating higher doses over time or within a single session.
