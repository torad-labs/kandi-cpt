**LSD (Lysergic Acid Diethylamide)**
LSD is a psychedelic compound primarily administered orally via liquid, gel tabs, candies, or "blotter" (small pieces of absorbent paper). A standard dose ranges from 100 to 200 micrograms (mcg), with individual blotter squares typically containing 80 to 150 mcg. Precise dosage cannot be determined without overseas laboratory analysis.

The substance alters perception of self and environment; familiar spaces may appear alien, and the user's ability to process conversations or emotional cues may be impaired. Visual effects vary from slight color and shape distortions to significant alterations in the appearance of people and spaces. Full hallucinations—the perception of objects that are entirely absent—are rare.

Psychological outcomes are influenced by "set" (the user's internal mental state, mood, and expectations) and "setting" (the physical and social environment). Adverse reactions may include extreme anxiety and panic. Individuals with personal or family histories of psychotic or mood disorders face an increased risk of psychological distress. A potential long-term complication is Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder (HPPD).

**Cocaine**
Cocaine is a stimulant derived from the coca plant, characterized as a bitter, numbing powder. It produces effects similar to amphetamines, including euphoria, alertness, confidence, and increased talkativeness, though its duration of action is shorter.

Administration occurs most commonly via insufflation (snorting "bumps" or lines) or smoking (crack). A typical insufflated dose ranges from 30 to 70 mg, while a smoked dose ranges from 15 to 50 mg. The onset of effects occurs within minutes, peaking between 15 and 30 minutes, with a total duration of 30 to 60 minutes. Due to the short half-life, users frequently develop a tolerance, requiring increased dosages over time or within a single session.
