### Ketamine
Ketamine is a dissociative anesthetic, a pharmacological class that includes PCP, DXM, and nitrous oxide. It functions by separating perception from sensation. While medical-grade ketamine is a liquid, the illicit form is typically a white powder administered intranasally.

**Dosage and Effects**
The onset of effects occurs within 5 to 15 minutes. Low doses produce a trance-like or "floaty" state lasting 45 to 60 minutes, with a full return to baseline within 1.5 to 2 hours. Standard insufflated doses range from 30 to 60 mg. Doses of 100 mg or higher typically induce a "k-hole," a state of full dissociation and out-of-body experience. Intramuscular injection requires lower dosages to achieve similar effects.

**Risk Factors and Interactions**
Ketamine does not independently depress heart rate or respiration. However, co-administration with depressants—including alcohol, benzodiazepines, or GHB—increases the risk of blackouts, vomiting, erratic body temperature, and loss of consciousness. High-dose use carries a risk of aspiration (choking on vomit) or accidental injury due to impaired motor control.

**Long-term Health and Testing**
Chronic use can result in ketamine cystitis, characterized by damage to the bladder lining, pelvic pain, and incontinence; severe cases may require bladder removal. Because ketamine disrupts communication between the brain and body, motor control and pain signaling are significantly reduced. Regarding purity, isomer-specific claims (e.g., r-ketamine vs. s-ketamine) cannot be verified without advanced laboratory equipment and are generally regarded as marketing.

### LSD
LSD (lysergic acid diethylamide), discovered in 1938, is a psychedelic substance. A single dose typically results in an experience lasting 8 to 12 hours. The duration is often characterized by "chapter switching," where the thematic or emotional nature of the trip shifts abruptly due to environmental changes, specific thoughts, or external events.
