Friend: I’m helping my kid get ready for EDC 2026, but I’m terrified about the drug scene. If they run into LSD, what does that actually do to them? Is it all hallucinations?

Vet: Honestly, the movie stuff is a myth. Most people just get slight color or shape distortions. "True hallucinations"—seeing things that aren't there—are actually really rare. The bigger deal is how it changes their perception of themselves and the environment. Familiar places can feel alien, and they might struggle to follow a conversation or read emotions.

Friend: That sounds overwhelming. Is it always a "good" trip?

Vet: Not always. It can be cathartic and transformative, but it can also be deeply uncomfortable. It's all about "set and setting"—their mental state and the physical environment. If they are in a bad headspace or a chaotic area, like the crowd at kineticFIELD on Friday night, it can trigger extreme anxiety or panic.

Friend: What about the risks? Are there long-term effects?

Vet: There is something called HPPD (Hallucinogen Persisting Perception Disorder) which is a long-lasting disorder. Also, anyone with a family history of mood or psychotic disorders is at a much higher risk for psychological upset.

Friend: If they do end up taking it, how is it even taken? I don't know what to look for.

Vet: It’s usually oral. Most common is "blotter" (tiny pieces of paper), but it can be liquid, candy, or gel tabs. The doses are tiny—usually 100 to 200 mcg—so it's basically invisible.

Friend: Can they just trust the person selling it?

Vet: Absolutely not. Only an overseas lab can tell you the actual dose. Dealers are just guessing. My best advice is to tell your kid that if it has a bitter or metallic taste, it's NOT acid—it's something else. Also, they need to wait at least three hours before taking more, or it could all hit them at once.

Friend: What about cocaine? I keep hearing about that at these big events.

Vet: That's a totally different beast. It's a stimulant—bitter, numbing powder. It makes people feel confident, alert, and euphoric, similar to amphetamines, but it wears off way faster.

Friend: How fast are we talking?

Vet: Very fast. If they snort a typical dose of 30 to 70 mg, it peaks in 15 to 30 minutes and is gone in an hour. Because it's so short, people tend to build a tolerance quickly and take more and more during the night.

Friend: Is that why it's so dangerous for the heart?

Vet: Exactly. The cardiovascular risk is the main concern with stimulants like that, especially when you're dancing for hours in the heat at a stage like circuitGROUNDS. It puts a massive strain on the system.
