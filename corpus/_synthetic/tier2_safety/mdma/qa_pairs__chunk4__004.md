Q: What should be provided to a friend who is hot and sweaty but remains conscious and coherent?
A: Shade, electrolyte water, and a place to sit down.

Q: How should a person who is anxious or panicked but oriented be handled?
A: They should be moved to a quieter cooler space and reassured without arguing or attempting to use logic to talk them down.

Q: What is the recommended care for a friend who is exhausted or wobbly?
A: Seated rest, water, electrolytes, and food if they are able to keep it down.

Q: How should friend conflicts during a peak be managed?
A: Separate the individuals, provide water, listen, and ensure no big decisions are made while on a substance.

Q: Which three common misconceptions are listed as things that do NOT help an impaired friend?
A: Strong coffee, cold showers, and "walking it off."

Q: Why is a cold shower discouraged for an impaired person?
A: It does not help and risks causing cold shock in someone with an elevated heart rate.

Q: Why is sleeping it off without monitoring considered dangerous?
A: The person's airway could be compromised.

Q: In what position should a friend be kept if they are too impaired to stand or walk?
A: An upright seated position.

Q: Why should an impaired person not be left lying flat on their back?
A: Vomiting while unconscious on their back is a major cause of death.

Q: What is the correct procedure if an impaired person must lie down?
A: They must be placed in the recovery position, which is on their side with the top knee bent and head tilted to keep the airway clear.

Q: What are the two options for medical emergencies at Camp EDC or off-grounds?
A: Call 911 or walk the person to the 24-hour staffed Camp EDC medical post.

Q: What is the faster path for medical assistance at the festival proper than calling 911?
A: Ground Control and first-aid tents.
