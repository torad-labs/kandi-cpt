Friend: Yo! I'm finally hitting EDC 2026. I’m planning on rolling for the first time—any tips on what to actually look for when buying pills?

Vet: First rule: never trust a "pressed" pill just because of the color or logo. Street ecstasy is notoriously unreliable. It could be MDMA, but it’s often mixed with caffeine, amphetamines, MDA, or even DXM. Some don't have any psychoactive stuff at all.

Friend: That's sketchy. Can't I just use one of those test kits to be sure?

Vet: You should definitely get a reagent kit and fentanyl strips from DanceSafe, but remember: reagent tests are "red flags, not green lights." Back during the 2009-2017 shortage, some pills were 90% PMMA and 10% MDMA. The Marquis test would "pass" because of that tiny bit of MDMA, even though the PMMA was the main ingredient. A pass doesn't guarantee purity.

Friend: Damn, okay. I'm on a few meds, though. Is it safe to mix with other stuff?

Vet: Be really careful. If you're on MAOIs (like Nardil or Parnate) or the protease inhibitor Ritonavir, do NOT take it—it can be life-threatening. Also, avoid mixing it with cocaine or meth because of heart strain and overheating, and don't go overboard with alcohol since you'll dehydrate way faster.

Friend: I'm on an SSRI for anxiety. Will that be a problem?

Vet: Usually, SSRIs just block the MDMA from working, so you won't feel much. The real danger is if you try to "break through" that bluntness by taking massive doses—that's where things get risky.

Friend: Got it. Is there anyone who specifically shouldn't do it?

Vet: Yeah, if you have heart issues, high blood pressure, glaucoma, or liver/kidney problems, it's high risk. Also, people with a family history of psychotic disorders or "slow metabolizers" (people with low P450 2D6 enzymes) can be way more sensitive and need much lower doses.

Friend: I'll keep that in mind. I'm planning to hit the main stage for the closing set. Will I be totally wiped out the next day?

Vet: Oh, absolutely. When you're at kineticFIELD or circuitGROUNDS peaking, you're burning through everything. Plan for two days: the peak day and a full recovery day with zero plans. You'll feel drained.

Friend: I've heard people talk about a "crash" right after. Is that a thing?

Vet: It happens to some. As the peak wears off, you might feel a sudden drop in mood. The biggest mistake people make is redosing to stop that crash, but that just makes the whole experience worse.

Friend: What about the week after? I don't want to be a mess at work.

Vet: Just be prepared for the "week-after blues." For about 5-7 days, you might feel irritable, restless, or just antisocial. Some people get hit hard with temper tantrums over tiny things; others don't feel it at all. Just give your brain time to reset!
