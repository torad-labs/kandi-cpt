Q: What common adulterants are often found in pressed ecstasy pills?
A: Pressed ecstasy pills commonly contain caffeine, ephedrine, amphetamines, MDA, MDE, DXM, or, in rare cases, DOB.

Q: Which organization sells reagent kits and fentanyl test strips that work for MDMA?
A: DanceSafe.

Q: What was the composition of many pressed pills during the "great MDMA shortage" of 2009-2017?
A: They were 90% PMMA and 10% MDMA.

Q: Why did simple Marquis tests pass on pills containing both PMMA and MDMA during the 2009-2017 shortage?
A: PMMA does not react with Marquis reagent, and the small amount of MDMA content overshadowed the PMMA non-reaction.

Q: Which anti-depressants are listed as MAOIs that should not be taken with MDMA?
A: Nardil, Parnate, Marplan, Eldepryl, and Aurorex/Manerix.

Q: Which protease inhibitor is potentially life-threatening when combined with MDMA?
A: Ritonavir.

Q: What are the specific risks of combining MDMA with strong stimulants like cocaine or methamphetamine?
A: Overheating and heart strain.

Q: What is the likely effect of taking MDMA while on SSRIs?
A: The MDMA will likely not work at all because the SSRIs block its mechanism.

Q: Which liver enzyme is responsible for metabolizing MDMA, and what is the risk for "slow metabolizers" with low levels of this enzyme?
A: The enzyme is P450 2D6; slow metabolizers may be more sensitive and require lower doses.

Q: How many recovery days are recommended after a day of MDMA use?
A: One full recovery day with very little planned.
