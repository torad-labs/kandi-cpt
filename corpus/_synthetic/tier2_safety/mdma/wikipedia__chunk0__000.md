# 3,4-Methylenedioxy-N-methamphetamine (MDMA)

**3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methamphetamine**, commonly known as **MDMA**, is a synthetic psychoactive compound belonging to the phenethylamine and amphetamine chemical classes. It is categorized as an entactogen (from the Greek *en* "within" and *tactus* "touch"), an empathogen, and a euphoric stimulant. In common parlance, the substance is frequently referred to as "ecstasy" (typically referring to pressed tablets) or "molly" (typically referring to crystalline powder or capsules).

## History
MDMA was first synthesized in 1890s and subsequently patented by Merck pharmaceutical in December 1912. Its psychoactive properties did not receive scholarly attention until the mid-1970s, when the substance was introduced to the psychotherapeutic community by chemist Alexander Shulgin. During the late 1970s and early 1980s, it was utilized as a tool in psychotherapy before becoming widely available for recreational use. In 1985, the United States government classified MDMA as a Schedule I controlled substance. Despite its illegal status for recreational use, the substance has undergone evaluation by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) for the treatment of post-traumatic stress disorder (PTSD).

## Effects
The experience of MDMA intoxication is colloquially termed "rolling." The substance produces a combination of stimulant and psychedelic effects, characterized by a "breakthrough" into a specific mental state.

### Psychological Effects
Recreational users typically seek the following effects:
*   **Emotional:** Intense euphoria, increased empathy, emotional openness, and heightened feelings of self-acceptance and warmth toward others.
*   **Cognitive:** A significant reduction in critical or cynical thinking and a decrease in social inhibitions.

### Physiological Effects
MDMA administration results in several systemic physical responses:
*   **Sensory:** Enhanced tactile sensation and, in some cases, nystagmus (involuntary eye movement).
*   **Autonomic:** Increased heart rate, elevated blood pressure, and an increase in core body temperature (hyperthermia).
*   **Musculoskeletal:** Bruxism (teeth grinding) and trismus (jaw locking).
*   **Other:** Dehydration and difficulty urinating.

The intensity of the experience is dose-dependent. Insufficient dosages may result in physical stimulation without the characteristic psychological "breakthrough," while excessive dosages may lead to a state of sedation or physical incapacity, often referred to as being "floored."

## Logistics and Terminology
MDMA is associated with a wide array of slang terms. The substance itself is referred to as X, XTC, E, M, Adam, Thizz, or "beans." The state of intoxication is described as "rolling," "X-ing," "thizzing," or "wigging."

## Safety and Harm Reduction
Due to its effect on thermoregulation and hydration, MDMA carries risks associated with overheating and dehydration. Safety protocols typically emphasize the monitoring of core body temperature and the maintenance of fluid balance. Because the substance is often sold in pressed tablets or powders of unknown purity, harm reduction organizations such as DanceSafe advocate for the use of reagent testing to verify the presence of MDMA and the absence of adulterants.
