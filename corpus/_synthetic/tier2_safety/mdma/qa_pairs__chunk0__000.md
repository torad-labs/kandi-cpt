Q: What is the chemical name for MDMA?
A: 3,4-methylenedioxy-N-methamphetamine.

Q: In common usage, what is the typical difference between "molly" and "ecstasy"?
A: "Ecstasy" frequently refers to pressed tablets, while "molly" tends to refer to capsules or loose crystals.

Q: When was MDMA first patented by Merck pharmaceuticals?
A: December 1912.

Q: In what year was MDMA made illegal in the United States as a Schedule I substance?
A: 1985.

Q: Which doctor is credited with introducing MDMA to psychotherapy?
A: Dr. Alexander Shulgin.

Q: What are the effect classifications for MDMA?
A: Euphoric Empathogen, Euphoric Stimulant, Entactogen, and Phenethylamine.

Q: What are the primary recreational effects sought from MDMA?
A: Emotional openness, euphoria, empathy, stimulation, increased energy, reduction of critical and cynical thoughts, decrease of inhibitions, and heightened feelings of warmth, openness, and self-acceptance.

Q: What physical effects are associated with MDMA use?
A: Enhanced tactile sensation, increased blood pressure and heart rate, increased core body temperature, teeth grinding (bruxism), jaw locking (trismus), eye wiggles (nystagmus), difficulty urinating, and some dehydration.

Q: What happens when a user takes too much MDMA?
A: The user may be "floored," making them want to remain seated or lying down for hours.

Q: What is the result of not taking enough MDMA?
A: The user may experience physical effects without "breaking through," leaving them stimulated but antisocial or quiet.
