Friend: I'm so hyped for EDC 2026, but I'm a little nervous about the comedown. I've heard the "Tuesday Blues" are real. Is that actually a thing?

Vet: Oh, it’s very real. Usually, the depression hits around the second day after you roll and can last up to 5 days. Some people even feel it for weeks, though a lucky few get an "afterglow" where they feel great for a week. It just varies.

Friend: That sounds brutal. Is there anything I can take to stop that from happening?

Vet: You can use 5-HTP to help with the blues, but listen carefully: ONLY take it after at least 8 hours have passed since your last dose. Never take it before or during your roll, or you risk serotonin syndrome.

Friend: Got it. What about supplements before the party starts?

Vet: Some supplements taken *before* the roll can have a protective effect. Generally, stuff you take after doesn't do much besides potentially helping with those post-roll blues. But honestly? The only truly reliable way to mitigate the crash is to space out your rolls. Nothing else replaces that.

Friend: Speaking of spacing, if I do something Friday at kineticFIELD and again Saturday at circuitGROUNDS, will it still hit the same?

Vet: Probably not. There's a short tolerance window. If you use it two days in a row, the second day is usually a greatly diminished experience. You really want at least 7+ days between uses, though some people find their tolerance lasts 2-3 weeks.

Friend: I've heard people talk about "losing the magic." Is that a myth?

Vet: Definitely not a myth. As your lifetime usage goes up, the enjoyment often goes down. Some people lose that empathic "magic" after only 10 rolls; others last for hundreds. If you use it heavily or frequently, you accelerate that process, and once the magic is gone, it can be permanent.

Friend: That’s scary. Is it actually damaging your brain?

Vet: MDMA is a known neurotoxin, though experts debate the magnitude—kind of like how alcohol is also a neurotoxin. Some studies show heavy users have more anxiety, depression, and memory issues. It's a complex field, but the risks are there.

Friend: Can you actually get addicted to it?

Vet: It’s not physically dependence-forming, but it can be psychologically addicting. Some people start compulsively using it every weekend because it becomes such a huge part of their lives. The best way to avoid that and preserve the magic is to space your rolls and not keep increasing your dose.

Friend: I want to be safe, but I'm also worried about my friends. What are the "red alert" signs that someone needs a medic immediately?

Vet: If you see vomiting, seizures, pale skin, or bluish lips/fingers, get help NOW. Also watch for abnormal heart rates, extreme body temps, severe headaches, or if they're hallucinating and paranoid. If their pupils don't react to light or they're in a stupor, it's an emergency.

Friend: If I see that, where do I go? I don't want us to get kicked out or in trouble.

Vet: Don't worry about that. Insomniac's explicit policy is that you will NOT get in trouble for seeking medical help. Just head to the nearest first-aid tent, flag down Ground Control in the purple shirts, or find any staff member. Just get them to the medics.
