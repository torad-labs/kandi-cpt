### Pharmacokinetics of MDMA

The onset of MDMA typically occurs within 20 to 60 minutes, though ingestion on a full stomach may delay effects for up to two hours. The initial onset is characterized by a rapid transition toward peak effects. The peak duration generally lasts 90 to 120 minutes, with total primary effects lasting between three and five hours. Following the primary effects, a residual period of two to six hours occurs, often characterized by difficulty sleeping and noticeable physiological shifts.

### Dosage and Administration

A standard oral dose of MDMA ranges from 80 mg to 125 mg; pressed pills typically contain between 80 mg and 120 mg. Exceeding an individual's specific "sweet spot" dose does not increase the duration or quality of the effects but increases the prevalence of side effects. 

A single redose, consisting of one-third to one-half of the initial dose administered approximately 90 minutes after the first, may extend the peak duration by several hours. Further redosing typically increases the severity of the subsequent hangover.

### Tolerance and Frequency

Frequent use leads to the development of tolerance, characterized by a reduced quality of the high and an increase in post-administration depression and hangovers. To allow for serotonin replenishment, a minimum spacing of a few weeks between uses is required, though best practice suggests a interval of three to six months to avoid permanent loss of efficacy.

### Physiological Risks and Safety

**Hyperthermia:** MDMA increases core body temperature. Risk of overheating is compounded by high ambient temperatures (such as the Nevada desert in May), high doses, heavy physical exertion, and the concurrent use of other stimulants like cocaine or amphetamines.

**Hypertension:** The onset of a severe, splitting headache during stimulant use may indicate dangerously high blood pressure and requires immediate medical attention.

**Hydration and Electrolytes:** Improper fluid management can result in two distinct crises: hypernatremia (dehydration) or hyponatremia (water poisoning due to sodium dilution). Recommended intake is one to three cups (6–18 oz) of water per hour, increasing to four cups (24 oz) during heavy exertion. Hydration must be supplemented with electrolytes via salty snacks or electrolyte powders to prevent sodium dilution.
