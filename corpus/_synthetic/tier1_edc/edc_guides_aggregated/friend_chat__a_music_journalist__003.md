Friend: I’m finally hitting EDC 2026, but the logistics look like a nightmare. How am I actually getting to the Speedway without losing my mind?

Vet: Honestly, just get the shuttle pass. It’s the cleanest way to go since they pick you up right at the Strip hotels. If you’ve got the budget, Premier shuttle lets you reserve a specific time slot, which is a lifesaver. Avoid Uber or Lyft unless you love surge pricing, and if you’re feeling fancy, Maverick Helicopters does 15-minute transfers from the Strip.

Friend: Got it. What about the timing? I don't want to spend my first night stuck in a parking lot.

Vet: Pro tip: get there early on Friday. Ideally, you want to be inside by 5pm for the opening ceremony, but definitely before 9pm. You want to scope out the grounds in the daylight and get your photos before the place becomes a neon chaotic void.

Friend: I’m planning on staying for the sunrise sets on the final night. Is that a bad move?

Vet: It’s a vibe, but a risky one. If you leave after 4:30am, the shuttle and rideshare lines are absolutely brutal. Expect a 1-2 hour wait to get home. If you can't handle that, bail before 4:30am. Also, remember there is zero re-entry unless you have a Camp EDC wristband, so don't step outside for a snack and expect to get back in.

Friend: I'm going with a group, but we're all pretty chaotic. Any survival tips for the actual weekend?

Vet: Use the buddy system—two or three brains are better than one. And treat it like a marathon. Take breaks, ride the Ferris wheel, and eat actual food during the day before the gates open. You can't rave on an empty stomach for three days.

Friend: Speaking of the group, what happens if someone in the crew actually gets sick or has a bad reaction to something?

Vet: You find a Ground Control member immediately—they're the ones in purple shirts with the fanny packs and light sabers. Or just hit the nearest medical tent. Insomniac has an explicit "no questions asked" policy; you will not get in trouble for seeking help.

Friend: How do I know when a "bad trip" or a reaction actually requires medical intervention versus just needing a break?

Vet: Look for the red flags: vomiting, seizures, pale skin, bluish lips, or if they're delusional and paranoid. If their pupils aren't reacting to light or they're unresponsive, they need a medic. Also, if they're impaired, keep them sitting upright—never lying flat. And don't try to "walk it off" or give them coffee if it's alcohol poisoning; that doesn't work. Only medical care does.

Friend: I've heard about the "zero tolerance" drug policy. Is that actually how it works on the ground?

Vet: Officially, yes. But for harm reduction: drink electrolyte water, take breaks, and never let a drunk person be the "babysitter" for another drunk person. Most importantly, don't take anything from strangers.

Friend: I want to experience the "lore" side of things. What are the must-sees besides the main stages?

Vet: You’ve gotta hit Downtown EDC. It’s like a mini-city. Check out the YeeDC! line-dancing saloon, the Queen Bar for drag, and the Chapel if you want to get "married" to your best friend. I always spend time at Memory Lane looking at old festival footage. Also, keep an eye out for the Owl on the kineticFIELD stage and the moving Daisy installation.

Vet: That’s Kandi trading, based on the PLUR ethos (Peace, Love, Unity, Respect). The handshake goes: peace sign, then heart-shape, then a handshake, then you exchange the bracelet.
