Friend: I’m looking into EDC 2026 and I'm a bit nervous about the logistics. If I do the camp option, what’s the deal with check-in?

Vet: Honestly, just get there early. They told us check-in started at 9:00 a.m., but they actually started processing people shortly after 7:00 a.m. If you hit the line at 7, you'll glide right through. The process is super smooth now, way faster than previous years.

Friend: What about the actual sleeping situation? I've heard the pods can be hit or miss.

Vet: They’re the same pods as before. Four people can fit, and while it's not exactly "luxury" comfort, there's enough space to make it work. Pro tip: bring a reflective tarp to put over the top of your pod. It lowers the internal temperature significantly. Also, bring a few fans just in case, though the weather was actually surprisingly cool this last time.

Friend: I saw something about a "Mesa" area. Is that worth checking out?

Vet: 100%. Go straight to the mesa on Thursday right after you check in. Hardly anyone is there yet, so you're basically guaranteed access to the pool. It's the best way to soak up those PLUR vibes—people trading trinkets, candy, and just looking out for each other. It's the heart of the camp community.

Friend: Are there other activities at camp, or is it just the pool?

Vet: There’s yoga and wellness stuff, but honestly? Don't stress about doing everything. I used to try to get my money's worth by hitting every scheduled event, but now I prioritize sleep and wellness on my own terms. Just hit the pool parties and the pre-party; that's where the real energy is.

Friend: Speaking of energy, who should I be looking for on the lineup? Who actually delivered?

Vet: You've gotta keep an eye on the big names, but some surprises hit hard. I was really impressed with Sarah Landry and DJ Snake—I didn't expect to love Snake as much as I did, but he killed it. Also, Alesso's last 20 minutes were an absolute throwback to the golden era.

Friend: Which stages are the "must-sees" for the vibe?

Vet: You can't miss kineticFIELD for the classics, but keep an eye on the harder stages for acts like Sub Zero Project. The layout changes they've made recently have really fixed the bottlenecking and crowd-crush issues from a few years back. It actually feels safe and breathable again.

Friend: I've heard the weather in the desert can be a nightmare. Any warnings?

Vet: It’s unpredictable. We had one night that was actually freezing, then a day that was glorious, then high wind advisories on Day 2. Just pack layers. If the wind gets too crazy, they might warn you, but usually, it settles down by 9:00 p.m.

Friend: Last question—is it still worth it after all these years, or has it become too corporate?

Vet: I've been going since the LA days in 2008, and I'll tell you: this most recent experience rivals my top three. Between the improved layout and the music quality—which felt as good as 2013 again—it's a 10/10. If you can make it work, just do it. Under the electric sky is still the place to be.
