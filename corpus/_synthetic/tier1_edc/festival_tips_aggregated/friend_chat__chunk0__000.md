Friend: I’m finally doing it! I got my ticket for EDC 2026, but I'm low-key terrified. Since you've been a few times, what's the absolute biggest mistake rookies make?

Vet: Honestly? The shoes. People try to look cute in sandals or brand new sneakers and end up crippled by midnight. You’re going to walk 10 to 20 miles a day on rough concrete at the Speedway. Wear broken-in sneakers and pack blister pads and gel insoles. If your feet give out on night one, your whole weekend is ruined.

Friend: Yikes, okay, sneakers it is. I'm planning to go hard the second I hit the ground. Is that the move?

Vet: Definitely not. Treat it like a marathon, not a sprint. You have three 10-hour nights. Some veterans actually stay sober on night one just to get their bearings and map out the grounds. If you burn out Friday, you're missing the best parts of Sunday. Also, don't underestimate the Vegas pre-parties—about 20% of people actually miss night one because they're too hungover to move.

Friend: Good point. I'll pace myself. What about water? I know it's hot, but is just drinking a lot of water enough?

Vet: Not actually. Pure water can lead to hyponatremia if you overdo it. Aim for about 16oz an hour, but cap it at 27oz. The secret is electrolytes—grab some Powerade or those free Electrolit sticks at the water stations. Bring a CamelBak or an Insomniac-approved bottle and look for the water-droplet symbols on the map to refill.

Friend: I'll make sure to grab a CamelBak. What about the music? I want to see everything, but the lineup is insane. How do I manage that?

Vet: You have to accept right now that you cannot see every set. The math doesn't work—there are over 240 artists across 9 main stages like kineticFIELD, circuitGROUNDS, and neonGARDENS, plus 8 secondary venues. Pick 3 to 5 "must-sees" per night and let the rest be a surprise. If you try to run between every stage, you'll just exhaust yourself.

Friend: That makes sense. Anything else for my gear list?

Vet: High-fidelity earplugs. Don't use the cheap foam ones; they just muffle the sound. Get something like Loops or Etymotic. They cut about 15-25 dB so you can actually hear the music without getting permanent tinnitus from a single weekend.

Friend: Got it. When should I actually fly in and out? Just the weekend of?

Vet: If you can, arrive Thursday and don't fly home until Tuesday. Use Thursday to acclimate to the heat and recover from your flight. Then, use Monday as a recovery day before you hit the airport. Trying to fly home on Monday morning while exhausted is a nightmare.

Friend: Last thing—I'm going with a group. How do we make sure we don't lose each other in that crowd?

Vet: The buddy system is a must, but don't rely on "meeting by the main stage" because that's too vague. Pick very specific, concrete meeting points on the map. Cell service is a gamble with that many people, so having a physical "if we get lost, meet here" spot is the only way to stay sane.
