Friend: My kid is heading to EDC 2026 and I'm trying to help them pack. I know it's a party, but what are the actual essentials for surviving the desert?

Vet: First rule: absolutely no brand-new shoes. They need broken-in sneakers, gel insoles, and blister pads. The walking is brutal. Also, tell them to pack "the survival kit"—high-fidelity earplugs, tons of wet wipes, a power bank, and a phone case with a wrist lanyard so they don't drop it in the crowd.

Friend: What about the weather? I've heard the desert is unpredictable.

Vet: It's a rollercoaster. They need sunscreen and sealed ChapStick for the day, but they must bring layers. It can drop into the 50s by 4am, even if they're sweating while dancing. Oh, and a pro tip: tell them to bring anti-chafe product like BodyGlide. Chafing is the silent festival killer.

Friend: I'm worried about them getting lost or dehydrated. Any advice on health or safety?

Vet: Hydration is key—they should start loading up on electrolytes the day before. For safety, they need a buddy system and a fixed meeting point. Tell them *not* to use the Daisy art installation as a landmark because it moves. The Lockers area or the merchant store are much better fixed points. Also, remind them that "Ground Control" (the people in purple shirts) are there to help, and Medical is free and judgment-free.

Friend: How does the actual layout work? I want to make sure they know where they're going.

Vet: It’s at the Las Vegas Motor Speedway. The main entrance leads straight to kineticFIELD, which is the iconic main stage with the giant mascot face. From there, they can find cosmicMeadow for the opening ceremony, circuitGrounds for the fire plumes, neonGarden for the darker vibes, or wasteland for that post-apocalyptic look.

Friend: If they get totally turned around, how do they find the exit?

Vet: Tell them to look for the raised bleachers. They're visible from almost anywhere in the venue and lead right to the exit. If they want a good landmark for a meeting spot, the Ferris wheel in Downtown EDC is perfect. Plus, if they time it right, they can see the fireworks from the top.

Friend: Are there any "insider" tips they should know so they don't burn out by day two?

Vet: Definitely. Don't try to see everything—pick a few priorities. A great strategy is to stay sober on Night 1 to get the lay of the land. They should also aim to leave by 4:30am to beat the worst of the crowds, though the sunrise sets on Night 2 are absolutely worth the commitment.

Friend: I see people talking about "Kandi" and totems. Is that necessary?

Vet: Not mandatory, but recommended! Kandi (beaded bracelets) is how people make friends and signal friendship. A totem—like a tall pole with a sign—is just a way for the group to find each other in a crowd of thousands.

Friend: Any last-minute paperwork or boring stuff I should make sure they have?

Vet: Just a little bit. Have them bring some cash for prepaid card exchanges and a photocopy of their ID kept separate from the actual ID, just in case. And remind them to download the Insomniac app for the map and set times, because cell service is notoriously bad once you're inside.
