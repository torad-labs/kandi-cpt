Friend: I’m finally hitting EDC Las Vegas in 2026, but I'm low-key terrified of the logistics. Since you've been a dozen times, what's the one thing I absolutely cannot mess up?

Vet: Honestly? Your shoes. If your feet go, your whole weekend is dead. Do not wear sandals, flip-flops, or brand-new sneakers you bought for the "aesthetic." You’re looking at 10 to 20 miles of walking a day on rough concrete and unpaved dirt at the Speedway. Break in your sneakers now and pack blister pads and gel insoles. If you don't, you'll be one of those people who skips Sunday night because they can't physically stand.

Friend: 20 miles?! I didn't realize it was that big. How do I actually survive three nights of that without crashing by midnight on Friday?

Vet: Treat it like a marathon, not a sprint. A pro move is to stay completely sober on night one just to get the lay of the land. Too many people blow their budget and energy on Vegas pre-parties and don't even make it through the gates on Friday. Ramp up on night two and three, and make sure you actually sleep during the day.

Friend: Speaking of surviving, I know it's the desert, but is just drinking water enough?

Vet: Not actually. Pure water alone won't cut it. Aim for about 16 oz an hour, but don't go over 27 oz or you risk hyponatremia. You need electrolytes—bring Pedialyte or grab those free Electrolit sticks at the water stations. Bring an empty CamelBak or an Insomniac-approved bottle and look for the water-droplet symbol on the map to refill.

Friend: Got it. I'm looking at the lineup now and it's overwhelming. I want to see everything, but it seems impossible.

Vet: It is impossible. The math just doesn't work—you've got over 240 artists across nine main stages and eight secondary venues. You can't sprint from kineticFIELD to circuitGROUNDS and then over to neonGARDENS without killing yourself. Pick 3 to 5 "must-sees" per night and let the rest be a discovery mission.

Friend: That makes me feel better. What about the sound? I've heard the bass at some of these stages can be brutal.

Vet: It is. Do not rely on those cheap foam plugs; they just muffle everything. Get high-fidelity plugs like Loops or Etymotics. They cut the volume by 15-25 dB but keep the music crisp. Tinnitus from a single EDC weekend is a very real thing.

Friend: I'm booking my flights now. Should I just fly in Friday morning and leave Monday morning?

Vet: No way. You'll be a zombie. The veteran schedule is arrive Thursday, leave Tuesday. Use Thursday to acclimate to the Vegas heat and recover from your flight. Then, give yourself Monday as a buffer day before you fly home so you aren't dealing with post-festival exhaustion while navigating an airport.

Friend: Last thing—I'm going with a group, but we always lose each other. How do we actually find each other in that crowd?

Vet: The "find me near the stage" plan never works. Pick a concrete, physical landmark as a designated meeting point—like a specific art installation or a sign—and stick to it. Use a buddy system, and if you get separated, go straight to that one spot. Don't wander the grounds trying to find your friends.
