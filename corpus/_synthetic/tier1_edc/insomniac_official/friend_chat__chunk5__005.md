Friend: I’m finally going to EDC 2026! I'm hyped, but honestly a little nervous about the logistics. What's the deal with water? Can I just bring a pack of bottles?

Vet: First rule: don't bring sealed water; they won't let you in with it. Just bring an empty CamelBak or a reusable bottle. Once you're inside, look for the water droplet symbols on the map for the Electrolit stations. They have free filtered water and even give out free hydration sticks.

Friend: Oh, good to know. Is the water actually drinkable?

Vet: Totally. It's UV sterilized. It might be a bit warm, but that's actually better for your digestion when you're dancing at kineticFIELD all night.

Friend: Fair enough. What about food? I'm vegan, so I'm worried about options.

Vet: You're all set. There are food stands everywhere with vegan and vegetarian options, and roaming vendors who carry snacks and water. Just remember it's a cashless event, so make sure you have Apple Pay, Android Pay, or a card.

Friend: Got it. I'm definitely forgetting something, though. Is there a place to buy stuff if I realize I forgot eye drops or something?

Vet: Yeah, hit up the Insomniac General Shop. They sell the essentials like flip-flops, ring pops, and eye drops. If you want the actual 2026 apparel or artist tees, those are at the separate merch booths.

Friend: Smart. I'm also worried about my phone dying while we're trying to find each other.

Vet: That's why you should rent a locker. They have unlimited access and every single one has a universal phone charger built-in. There are also general charging stations around the venue if you're in a pinch.

Friend: Speaking of finding each other, I've heard it's a nightmare. Any tips?

Vet: Use the buddy system. Set specific meeting points and stick together—two or three brains are better than one. And seriously, don't leave the show until every single person in your crew is accounted for.

Friend: What if someone in the group overdoes it? Like, if they've had too much to drink or something?

Vet: First, never let a drunk person be the "responsible" one for another drunk person. If someone is struggling with alcohol, keep them awake and sitting upright—never let them lie down. And don't waste time with coffee or cold showers; they don't work for alcohol poisoning.

Friend: At what point do I stop trying to handle it myself and actually take them to medical?

Vet: You escalate to medical immediately if you see vomiting, seizures, or if their skin looks pale or bluish. Also look for abnormal heart rates, breathing that's too fast or slow, or if they're acting delusional or paranoid.

Friend: What about their eyes or responsiveness?

Vet: Exactly. If their pupils aren't reacting to light, or if they're in a stupor—meaning they're conscious but unresponsive—or if they're completely out and you can't rouse them, get them to medical right away. If you're ever unsure, just grab any Insomniac staff member and ask for help.
