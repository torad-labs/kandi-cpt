**Ownership Transitions (1988–1990)**
In March 1988, the city council initiated lease revocation proceedings against Rodriguez due to rent arrears and the sale of food without sales tax permits. Despite the termination approval, Rodriguez maintained operations until December 1990. On November 17, the city approved the sale of the land to a private operator, contingent upon a 10-year maintenance requirement.

**Las Vegas International Raceway (1989–1993)**
In May 1989, Richie Clyne (director of the Imperial Palace Hotel automobile museum) and investor G. Robert Diero purchased the complex for $1,070,000, renaming it the Las Vegas International Raceway. Early reports characterized the facility as dilapidated. While Clyne initially considered converting the site into an industrial park, he was persuaded by Bob Bahre and Tom Wheatcroft to maintain motorsport operations. The facility hosted the Nissan 400 (currently the Mint 400).

**Las Vegas Speedway Park (1993)**
In 1993, amateur racers David Juberg and Craig Graham assumed a lease from Clyne, subsequently renaming the facility the Las Vegas Speedway Park. In May of that year, the duo acquired the master lease from Tom Villardi. During this period, the venue lacked major events and was restricted from hosting off-road racing due to environmental regulations.
