Friend: I'm trying to map out my coverage for EDC 2026. Since you've been around the block, can you help me get the vibe right? Who’s actually running the show these days?

Vet: You've gotta look at the whole Insomniac Music Group ecosystem. They aren't just promoters anymore; they're a full-scale A&R and distribution machine. They’ve got a massive web of imprints like Basscon Records, Gud Vibrations, and Dreamstate Records handling the talent pipeline.

Friend: Interesting. I know they’ve expanded a lot, but are they still tied into those big venue acquisitions? I remember something about Miami.

Vet: Yeah, they took a majority stake in Club Space in Miami back in 2019. They also grabbed a controlling stake in Soundslinger for the Okeechobee festival around the same time, though Soundslinger actually announced in April 2025 that Insomniac is no longer involved with Okeechobee.

Friend: Got it. Switching to the actual festival experience—since I'm writing for a trade pub, I need to be precise. If I'm talking about the "core" EDC experience in 2026, which stages are the non-negotiables?

Vet: You have to mention kineticFIELD. It's the heart of the brand. Then you've got the rest of the staples like Circuit Grounds and bassPOD. If you're looking for the legacy, remember that Nocturnal Wonderland was actually their first public event and remains their longest-running brand.

Friend: I remember they did some wild stuff during the pandemic to keep the momentum. Does any of that "virtual" tech actually make it into the 2026 festivals?

Vet: Absolutely. They evolved from those "Insomniac Rewind" streams and "Park 'N Rave" drive-ins into a serious partnership with Unity Technologies. They've been building a "persistent" online world for virtual concerts, so expect that digital integration to bleed into the physical stages.

Friend: What about the sound palette? Is it still just Big Room and House, or are they diversifying the lineups?

Vet: It's way more diverse now. They launched the Lost in Dreams label and festival specifically to pivot toward future bass and melodic dubstep. You'll see that influence heavily on the 2026 stages, alongside their more aggressive bass brands like Abduction.

Friend: I saw they have a huge roster of events. Are they still doing the global expansion, or scaling back?

Vet: They've streamlined. You've still got the heavy hitters like EDC Las Vegas, Mexico, Orlando, and Portugal. But they've discontinued the UK, India, Puerto Rico, and Brazil editions to focus on the core markets.

Friend: Last question—on the talent side, do they still use their own label to seed the lineups?

Vet: Definitely. Insomniac Records started back in 2014 with Interscope, signing Arty as their first act. Now, with the IMG umbrella, they use artists from labels like HARD Recordings and Discovery Project to fill out the slots across the board.
