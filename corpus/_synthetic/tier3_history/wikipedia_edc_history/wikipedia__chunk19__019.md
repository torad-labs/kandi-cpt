# Las Vegas International Raceway

The **Las Vegas International Raceway** (formerly the Las Vegas Speedrome) was a motorsport complex located in Las Vegas, Nevada. The facility underwent several ownership transitions between 1988 and 1993, characterized by legal disputes, rebranding efforts, and attempts to modernize the infrastructure to attract professional racing leagues.

## Ownership and Legal Disputes
In March 1988, the Las Vegas City Council deliberated the revocation of the facility's lease held by Rodriguez. The council cited non-payment of rent and the unauthorized sale of food without required sales tax permits. Rodriguez Jr. later acknowledged these failings, attributing them to his father's two-year absence from the United States and a concurrent divorce. Although the city approved the termination of the lease, operations continued until December 1990.

On November 17, the city approved the sale of the land to a private operator. Despite Rodriguez's arguments regarding the improvements he had implemented at the track, the property was put up for sale with the stipulation that the purchaser must maintain the facility for a period of ten years.

## The Clyne and Diero Era
In May 1989, the complex was purchased for $1,070,000 by Richie Clyne, director of the automobile museum at the Imperial Palace Hotel (now The Linq), and investor G. Robert Diero. Upon acquisition, the facility was renamed the **Las Vegas International Raceway**.

Clyne and Diero intended to revamp the complex, which they described as worn-out and dangerous. However, by early 1990, the venue maintained a reputation for being "old, dirty, and tattered." *Las Vegas Review-Journal* writer Greg Bortolin noted that millions of dollars in renovations would be required to attract major racing leagues, observing that at the time, the University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV) sports teams were the only major sporting presence in the city. 

Track media manager Neal Reid stated in interviews that Clyne had initially considered converting the site into an industrial park but was persuaded to maintain the racing venue by motorsport businessmen Bob Bahre and Tom Wheatcroft.

## The Juberg and Graham Era
In 1993, amateur racers David Juberg and Craig Graham assumed a lease from Clyne to operate the complex. Juberg subsequently renamed the facility the **Las Vegas Speedway Park**. In May 1993, the duo acquired the master lease from Tom Villardi. During this period, the complex struggled to secure major events, and off-road racing remained absent due to environmental concerns.

## Event Lineup and Logistics
Under the leadership of Richie Clyne, the facility hosted its first races under the rebranded **Nissan 400** (currently known as the Mint 400). By 1991, management sought to expand the event calendar alongside general facility renovations.

## Safety and Infrastructure
During the transition to the Las Vegas International Raceway, ownership identified the complex as "dangerous," prompting a stated goal of complete revitalization to meet safety standards and professional racing requirements.
