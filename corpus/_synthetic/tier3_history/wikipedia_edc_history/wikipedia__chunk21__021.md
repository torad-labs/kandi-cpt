# Las Vegas Motor Speedway

The **Las Vegas Motor Speedway (LVMS)** is a motor racing facility that officially opened to the public on September 13, 1996, for IndyCar practice. The venue's inaugural race was characterized by severe winds and a high frequency of accidents; however, attendance reached 67,132, more than double the initial projections.

## History and Ownership

In December 1998, Bruton Smith acquired the LVMS complex for approximately $215 million. Smith's acquisition strategy included the renovation of facility amenities and the sale of 300 acres of industrial land. Following the purchase, Chris Powell, formerly of the R. J. Reynolds Tobacco Company and the NASCAR Winston Cup Series, was appointed general manager, a position he held from 1998 until 2025. In 2025, Patrick Lindsey succeeded Powell as general manager.

## Facility and Stages

### Racing Circuits
The facility features several racing configurations:
* **Main Oval:** In 2006, a renovation project costing between $25 million and $27 million was implemented. These modifications increased the turn banking from 12 to 20 degrees, narrowed the racing surface by 20 feet to a total of 50 feet, and increased spectator capacity to 141,000. Other upgrades included a redesigned garage and a new infield media center.
* **The Bullring:** In 2000, the facility's 0.375 mi (0.604 km) oval was modified and officially renamed the "Bullring."
* **Drag Strip:** The drag strip was expanded to four lanes in 2017 to allow for simultaneous four-car racing.

### Non-Racing Events
In 2011, the speedway secured the rights to host the flagship event of the Electric Daisy Carnival (EDC), relocating the festival from its previous site in Los Angeles.

## Event Lineup

The speedway has hosted a variety of sanctioned racing series:
* **IndyCar:** Hosted the inaugural event in 1996 and the IZOD IndyCar World Championship in 2011.
* **NASCAR:** The facility held its first NASCAR Craftsman Truck Series race in late 1996 and its first NASCAR Busch Series race in 1997. It secured rights for a NASCAR Winston Cup Series weekend for the 1998 season and later added a second NASCAR Cup Series weekend in the fall starting in 2018.

## Safety and Incidents

The facility recorded its first fatal auto racing accident in 2011 during the IZOD IndyCar World Championship. Driver Dan Wheldon died on the 11th lap of the competition.
