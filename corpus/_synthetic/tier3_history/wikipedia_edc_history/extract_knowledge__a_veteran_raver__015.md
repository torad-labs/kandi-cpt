**Venue Geography and Capacity**
The Las Vegas Motor Speedway (LVMS) is situated in Las Vegas, Nevada, positioned between Interstate 15 and Las Vegas Boulevard. The facility has expanded from an initial 200 acres at its inception to over 1,200 acres as of 2015. While the track's peak seating capacity previously exceeded 140,000, its stated capacity as of 2023 is 80,000.

**Infrastructure and Amenities**
A 2006 renovation project introduced several key facilities to the complex. These include the Neon Garage, an interactive spectator garage, and the LocaliQ Digital Center, a 60,000-square-foot, three-story media center located within the track's infield. A proposed 127-unit condominium tower overlooking the first turn was cancelled following safety concerns and opposition from the United States Air Force and Nellis Air Force Base.

**Auxiliary Tracks**
The complex contains multiple secondary racing circuits:
*   **The Bullring:** A short oval originally built in 1985 as a 0.33-mile track using barriers from the Caesars Palace Grand Prix; it was modified to 0.375 miles in 2000.
*   **Dirt Oval:** A 0.500-mile clay dirt oval that opened in November 1996.
*   **The Strip:** A drag strip originally established in 1972. This was replaced in April 2000 by a 0.250-mile strip, which was further expanded to four lanes in 2017.

**Origins**
The facility's development began in August 1970, initiated by businessman Carl "Curly" Price, who sought to lease land near Nellis Air Force Base for a drag strip, road course, and oval. Construction of the original Las Vegas Motor Speedway was underway by February 1971.
