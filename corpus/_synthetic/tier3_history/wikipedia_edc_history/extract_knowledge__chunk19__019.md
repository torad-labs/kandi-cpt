In March 1988, the Las Vegas city council initiated the revocation of Rodriguez's lease based on allegations of unpaid rent and the sale of food without required sales tax permits. Rodriguez Jr. attributed these failures to his father's two-year absence from the United States and a concurrent divorce. While the city approved the lease termination, Rodriguez continued track operations until December 1990. On November 17, the city approved the sale of the land to a private operator, stipulating that the buyer must maintain the track for a period of 10 years.

In May 1989, Richie Clyne, director of the Imperial Palace Hotel's automobile museum, purchased the complex for $1,070,000 with investor G. Robert Diero. Under this ownership, the facility was renamed the Las Vegas International Raceway. Despite Clyne's initial interest in converting the site into an industrial park—a decision reversed following consultations with Bob Bahre and Tom Wheatcroft—the facility faced criticism for its dilapidated condition. Greg Bortolin of the *Review-Journal* noted that millions of dollars in renovations would be required to attract major racing leagues to a city then dominated by University of Nevada, Las Vegas (UNLV) sports.

The facility hosted its first races under Clyne's management with the Nissan 400 (later the Mint 400). In 1993, amateur racers David Juberg and Craig Graham acquired a lease from Clyne to manage the complex, which Juberg subsequently renamed the Las Vegas Speedway Park. In May 1993, Juberg and Graham acquired the master lease from Tom Villardi. During this period, environmental concerns prevented the venue from hosting major events, including off-road racing.
