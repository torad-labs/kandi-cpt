Vet: Oh, definitely. It’s a legendary spot. It actually opened way back on September 13, 1996, for IndyCar practice. The first actual race there was pretty wild—some writers called it "violent" because of crazy winds and tons of crashes.

Vet: Surprisingly, yeah. Even with traffic nightmares, they had 67,132 people show up, which was double what they expected. After that, they started bringing in NASCAR, like the Craftsman Truck Series and the Busch Series.

Friend: Interesting. Has the place changed much since the 90s?

Vet: Huge changes. Bruton Smith bought the complex in December 1998 for about $215 million. He renovated the amenities and sold off 300 acres of industrial space. He also brought in Chris Powell as GM, who ran the place from '98 all the way until 2025.

Friend: Did they do any major construction that affects where we'll be walking for the festival?

Friend: 141,000 people? That explains why the crowds are so massive at EDC. When did the festival actually start moving there?

Vet: LVMS won the rights to host the flagship EDC in 2011. It happened right after the festival had some major controversy at its old spot in Los Angeles.

Vet: It was a heavy year. Sadly, it was also the year of the track's first fatal racing accident—Dan Wheldon died on the 11th lap of the IZOD IndyCar World Championship.

Vet: Mostly, but they expanded the drag strip to four lanes in 2017. Also, they brought back another NASCAR Cup Series weekend in the fall starting in 2018.
