Friend: I'm digging into the archives for a piece on the evolution of the Electric Daisy Carnival. Looking back at the 2010 LA era, how chaotic actually was that event?

Vet: Honestly? A total disaster. We had about 185,000 people show up. Tickets started at $60, but some people were shelling out over $100. It was a nightmare—under-16s were getting in despite the rules, and a crowd stampede sent over 100 ravers to the hospital.

Friend: That sounds like a security failure. Did anyone actually lose their life?

Vet: Yeah, a 15-year-old named Sasha Rodriguez died after taking MDMA. It was the breaking point. The city of LA suspended all remaining 2010 events and any future dates until Insomniac agreed to new safety provisions, like having on-site doctors and strictly enforcing an 18+ age limit.

Friend: Did they just stop everything, or were there other regional shows happening back then?

Vet: They kept the regional circuit going. In 2010, they hit Aurora at the Arapahoe County Fairgrounds for EDC Colorado on June 12, then did EDC Dallas at Fair Park on June 19 with about 11,000 people. They even did a Puerto Rico show on August 28 at Estadio Sixto Escobar.

Friend: Is that why they eventually jumped ship to Nevada?

Vet: Exactly. Because of all that LA controversy, the flagship moved to the Las Vegas Motor Speedway in 2011. It was a massive scale-up—reportedly 230,000 people over three days from June 24 to 26. That's where the big stage identities like kineticFIELD really became the gold standard.

Friend: What about the other 2011 satellites? Did Orlando do well?

Vet: Pretty well. They hit Tinker Field on May 27–28. They drew about 12,000 people on Friday and bumped up to 20,000 on Saturday.

Friend: I saw some notes about Dallas in 2011. Was that a success?

Vet: Far from it. They went back to Fair Park on June 18 and pulled in 25,000 people, but the heat was lethal—over 110 degrees. Dozens were hospitalized and there was at least one death. That's why it was the last time EDC ever touched Dallas.

Friend: Crazy. And the other spots?

Vet: Colorado returned to the Arapahoe County Fairgrounds on June 11, and Puerto Rico did another run at Estadio Sixto Escobar on August 27.

Friend: Just for my timeline—did the lineup start getting more diverse as they settled into Vegas?

Vet: Definitely. By the time we hit 2016, you had a huge variety of sounds. I remember Michael Calfan absolutely killing it on the decks during that year's Vegas run.
