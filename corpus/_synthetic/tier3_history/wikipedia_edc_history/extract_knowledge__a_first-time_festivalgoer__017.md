**Lease Termination and Legal Disputes (1976–1977)**
Between September 17 and December 16, the city of Las Vegas initiated proceedings to revoke the lease of a racing track complex held by an operator named Price. The city cited unpaid monthly payments of approximately $3,400, frequent vandalism of machinery and the track, and the theft of bleachers by local residents. While Price initially repaid a portion of the rent, the lease was forfeited on December 16 after Price failed to stockpile 35,000 cubic yards of gravel.

The lease was officially terminated in July 1976 due to the continued failure to maintain the gravel stockpile. Following this, Price alleged in the *Review-Journal* that the city provided insufficient police response to vandalism and that maintenance costs were prohibitive. In 1977, Clark County District Judge Howard Babcock approved the termination, ordering Price to vacate the land and pay $22,140 in unpaid rent and contractual penalties. Price subsequently filed an appeal with the Supreme Court of Nevada in November 1977.

**Interim Management and Failed Revivals (1977–1978)**
During the legal deadlock, the city of Las Vegas issued temporary leases for the complex. In November 1977, the site was leased to Show & Go Ltd. through the end of the year, followed by short-term leases to various car clubs for the first three months of 1978. 

In February 1978, Bob Van Norman, owner of Craig Road Speedway, proposed a lease of $300 per month but withdrew the offer one week later to avoid accusations of city favoritism. In June 1978, the complex hosted the Silverbird Race of Champions, an event featuring movie stars that resulted in low spectator attendance.

**Establishment of Las Vegas Recreation, Inc. (1979)**
By January 1979, the city of Las Vegas sought new leaseholders to mitigate the thousands of dollars in ongoing maintenance costs. A group of five businessmen, including Van Norman and an individual named Horten, formed Las Vegas Recreation, Inc. to bid on temporary ownership. On February 21, 1979, the city commission unanimously approved the lease. Permanent ownership remained contingent upon the final settlement of the Nevada Supreme Court case involving Price.
