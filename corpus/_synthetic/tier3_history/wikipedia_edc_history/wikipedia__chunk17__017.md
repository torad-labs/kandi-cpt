# Las Vegas Racing Complex Lease Disputes (1976–1979)

## Overview
Between 1976 and 1979, the racing complex in Las Vegas was the subject of a prolonged legal and financial dispute between the city of Las Vegas and its operator, Price. The conflict centered on contractual failures, unpaid rent, and infrastructure maintenance, eventually leading to the termination of Price's lease and several unsuccessful attempts to revive the facility's operations.

## Lease Termination and Litigation
On September 17, the city of Las Vegas considered revoking Price's lease, citing approximately $3,400 in delinquent monthly payments. The city also noted an increase in vandalism targeting both the track and Price's machinery, as well as the theft of bleachers by local residents. 

Although Price made partial rent payments, he failed to meet a contractual requirement to stockpile 35,000 cubic yards of gravel. Following a decision delay, Price forfeited his lease on December 16. In July 1976, the lease was officially terminated due to the continued failure to maintain the required gravel stockpile. 

In an August interview with the *Review-Journal*, Price criticized the city, alleging inadequate police responses to vandalism and citing the prohibitive costs and labor required to maintain the gravel stockpile. In 1977, Clark County District Judge Howard Babcock approved the termination, ordering Price to vacate the premises and pay $22,140 in unpaid rent and contractual penalties.

## Legal Appeals and Interim Management
Despite the court order, Price remained the operator for a period while the city delayed his formal removal. In October 1977, Price announced intentions to appeal the ruling to the Supreme Court of Nevada, a move he acknowledged would likely suspend all racing activities at the complex for an extended duration. He confirmed this legal action in November 1977.

During this period of instability, the city of Las Vegas granted a temporary lease to Show & Go Ltd. for the remainder of 1977. This was followed by a short-term lease granted to various car clubs for the first three months of 1978.

## Revival Attempts
In February 1978, Bob Van Norman, owner of the Craig Road Speedway, proposed a lease of $300 per month to take over the complex. Van Norman withdrew his proposal a week later to avoid accusations of city favoritism. City officials subsequently stated that the facility was unlikely to attract serious racing proposals for at least 18 months.

In June 1978, the complex hosted the Silverbird Race of Champions, an event featuring several movie stars; however, the event was considered a failure due to low spectator attendance.

By January 1979, the city sought new leasing partners to mitigate the thousands of dollars in ongoing maintenance costs. In late January, a group of five businessmen, including Van Norman and Horten, formed Las Vegas Recreation, Inc. to bid for temporary ownership. On February 21, 1979, the city commission unanimously approved the lease to Las Vegas Recreation, Inc. A permanent lease remained contingent upon the resolution of the Nevada Supreme Court case involving Price.
