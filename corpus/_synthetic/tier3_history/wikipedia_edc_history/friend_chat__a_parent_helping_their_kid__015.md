Friend: I’m trying to help my kid plan for EDC 2026, but I'm totally lost on the venue. Where exactly is this thing?

Vet: It’s at the Las Vegas Motor Speedway (LVMS). It’s basically tucked right between Interstate 15 and Las Vegas Boulevard. It's a massive complex—it started at 200 acres, but now it's over 1,200 acres.

Friend: Wow, that's huge. Will it be crowded? How many people can actually fit in there?

Vet: It varies by era, but currently, the official capacity is around 80,000. Though, if you look at the track's peak history, it actually held over 140,000 seats at one point. It's definitely built for a crowd.

Friend: Since it's a race track, are there any actual buildings or facilities there, or is it just asphalt?

Vet: There's actually a fair amount of infrastructure. There's the Neon Garage, which is a redesigned interactive space, and a massive three-story media center called the LocaliQ Digital Center in the infield.

Friend: My kid mentioned something about condos or hotels on site. Is there a place to stay right there?

Vet: Actually, no. There was a plan to build a 127-unit condo tower overlooking the first turn, but the US Air Force and Nellis Air Force Base blocked it because of safety risks with their flight drills. So, you'll definitely need to book a hotel off-site.

Friend: Got it. Now, for the music part—since you've been to EDC, where should I tell my kid to head first?

Vet: For 2026, tell them to hit kineticFIELD on Friday night. That's where the biggest mainstage energy is. If they want something more underground, they've got to check out circuitGROUNDS on Saturday.

Friend: Are there other areas they should explore so they don't get lost in those 1,200 acres?

Vet: Definitely. They should spend Sunday afternoon at neonGARDENS for those melodic vibes. And if they want the full experience, they can't miss the bass-heavy sets over at bassSQUAD.

Friend: I saw something about "The Strip" and a "Bullring" in the venue description. Are those stages?

Vet: Haha, no! Those are actual racing tracks. The Bullring is a small oval, and "The Strip" is the drag strip that was expanded to four lanes back in 2017. Just make sure your kid doesn't confuse the racing "Strip" with the actual Las Vegas Strip!

Friend: That's a good distinction. Is the venue pretty new, or has it been around?

Vet: It's got some history. A guy named Carl "Curly" Price started planning a drag strip and road course there back in 1970. It's been evolving for over 50 years, which is why it's so sprawling now.
